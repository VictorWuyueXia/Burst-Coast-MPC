# Wake Sleep MPC Formulation Plan

## 0. Final convention

Use the following convention throughout the inverted pendulum benchmark:

$$
\theta = 0
\quad\text{upright target},
$$

$$
\theta = \pi \;\text{or}\; -\pi
\quad\text{bottom direction}.
$$

The raw state is

$$
x =
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta\\
\omega
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where $\theta$ is the pendulum angle measured from the upright direction, and $\omega=\dot{\theta}$ is angular velocity.

The target state is

$$
x^\star =
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

This convention is the most suitable for this project because the simulator already treats $\theta=0$ as upright. The cost, goal set, phase proxy, and later Lyapunov function should use the same target convention as the simulator.

The potential energy reference is set at the bottom:

$$
V(\pi)=0.
$$

This avoids negative potential energy while keeping the upright target at a positive energy level.

## 1. Continuous dynamics

$$
\dot{\theta}=\omega,
$$

$$
I\dot{\omega}=mgl\sin\theta-b\omega+u.
$$

Here:

$I$ is the moment of inertia about the pivot.

$m$ is the pendulum mass.

$g$ is gravitational acceleration.

$l$ is the distance from the pivot to the center of mass.

$b$ is the viscous damping coefficient.

$u$ is the scalar torque input.

This sign convention is correct for $\theta=0$ upright. Near the upright equilibrium,

$$
\sin\theta\approx \theta,
$$

so the passive linearized equation is

$$
I\ddot{\theta}\approx mgl\theta,
$$

which is unstable, as expected for an inverted pendulum.

## 2. Discrete prediction model

Use the continuous dynamics only as the physical model. The MPC prediction should use a discrete dynamics map

$$
x_{k+1}=f_d(x_k,u_k;\Delta t),
$$

where $\Delta t$ is the fixed sampling time.

For the first benchmark, $f_d$ should be the fourth order Runge Kutta discretization of the continuous dynamics.

The discrete prediction model is preferred for this stage because the controller, split ratio, cost summation, and execution all operate on sampled time steps. It also makes the burst and coast split exact in step counts.

The MPC should not be formulated as a continuous time collocation problem in the first version. Continuous transcription is more general, but it introduces more variables, equality constraints, scaling issues, and solver tuning. For the current two state benchmark, discrete single shooting is the cleaner formulation.

## 3. Input constraint and underpowered condition

The input constraint is

$$
\mathcal{U}=\{u: |u|\leq u_{\max}\},
$$

where $u_{\max}>0$ is the torque limit.

The underpowered condition is

$$
u_{\max}<mgl.
$$

This condition prevents the actuator from simply dominating gravity everywhere. The controller must exploit energy pumping, phase timing, and saturation.

## 4. Mechanical energy

Use kinetic energy

$$
K(x)=\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2.
$$

Use potential energy

$$
V(x)=mgl(1+\cos\theta).
$$

This gives

$$
V(0)=2mgl,
$$

and

$$
V(\pi)=0.
$$

Thus, the bottom has zero potential energy, and the upright target has maximum potential energy.

The total mechanical energy is

$$
E(x)=K(x)+V(x),
$$

or explicitly,

$$
E(x)=
\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2
+
mgl(1+\cos\theta).
$$

The target upright energy is

$$
E^\star=2mgl.
$$

The energy scale is

$$
E_s=E^\star=2mgl.
$$

Use the normalized energy error

$$
e_E(x)=\frac{E(x)-E^\star}{E_s}.
$$

At the upright target,

$$
\theta=0,\quad \omega=0,
$$

so

$$
E(x)=E^\star,
\qquad
e_E(x)=0.
$$

At bottom rest,

$$
\theta=\pi,\quad \omega=0,
$$

so

$$
E(x)=0,
\qquad
e_E(x)=-1.
$$

The energy error may be negative. That is acceptable. It means the system has less total mechanical energy than the upright target. The potential energy itself is not negative.

## 5. Local upright error

Define the wrapped upright angle error as

$$
e_\theta(x)=
\operatorname{atan2}(\sin\theta,\cos\theta).
$$

This maps the angle error to $(-\pi,\pi]$ and gives

$$
e_\theta(0)=0.
$$

Define the natural velocity scale

$$
\omega_s=\sqrt{\frac{mgl}{I}}.
$$

Define the local upright error vector

$$
e_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
e_\theta(x)\\
\omega/\omega_s
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

This term is for local stabilization around the upright target. It should not dominate the early swing up phase unless the system is already near the useful energy shell.

## 6. Phase proxy

Do not use true action angle coordinates for the first benchmark. Use a smooth phase proxy computed from the raw state.

Define

$$
a(\theta)=\cos\frac{\theta}{2},
$$

$$
b_\phi(\omega)=\frac{\omega}{\omega_s}.
$$

The subscript in $b_\phi$ avoids confusion with the viscous damping coefficient $b$.

Define

$$
r_\phi(x)=
\sqrt{
a(\theta)^2
+
b_\phi(\omega)^2
+
\epsilon_\phi^2
},
$$

where $\epsilon_\phi>0$ is a small regularization constant.

Define

$$
c_\phi(x)=\frac{a(\theta)}{r_\phi(x)},
$$

$$
s_\phi(x)=\frac{b_\phi(\omega)}{r_\phi(x)}.
$$

At upright rest,

$$
\theta=0,\quad \omega=0,
$$

so

$$
c_\phi(x)\approx 1,
\qquad
s_\phi(x)\approx 0.
$$

Define the phase proxy error

$$
e_\phi(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
c_\phi(x)-1\\
s_\phi(x)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The phase proxy is a cost feature, not a dynamics state. The dynamics should still be propagated in $(\theta,\omega)$.

## 7. Energy gate

Phase and local stabilization should matter most when the system has approximately the right energy. Define the energy gate

$$
w_E(x)=
\exp\left(
-\frac{e_E(x)^2}{\sigma_E^2}
\right),
$$

where $\sigma_E>0$ controls the width of the useful energy neighborhood.

When $|e_E(x)|$ is large, $w_E(x)$ is small. The cost then mainly encourages energy correction.

When $|e_E(x)|$ is small, $w_E(x)$ is close to one. The phase and local upright errors then become active.

This implements the intended logic:

$$
\text{first obtain the right energy, then care about phase and stabilization}.
$$

## 8. Energy phase value candidate

Use the following smooth state value candidate:

$$
V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x)=
q_E e_E(x)^2
+
q_\phi w_E(x)e_\phi(x)^TQ_\phi e_\phi(x)
+
q_{\mathrm{loc}}w_E(x)e_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)^TQ_{\mathrm{loc}}e_{\mathrm{loc}}(x).
$$

Here:

$q_E > 0$ is the energy error weight.

$q_\phi \geq 0$ is the phase error weight.

$q_{\mathrm{loc}} \geq 0$ is the local stabilization weight.

$Q_\phi \succ 0$ is the positive definite phase error weight matrix.

$Q_{\mathrm{loc}} \succ 0$ is the positive definite local error weight matrix.

This value candidate is zero at the upright target:

$$
V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x^\star)=0.
$$

It is not guaranteed to be globally positive definite in the strict mathematical sense because the energy feature alone can be zero at multiple states on the upright energy shell. The phase and local terms reduce that ambiguity. This is acceptable for the first MPC cost. For a later CLF or CLBF proof, the candidate should be analyzed over the relevant domain and may need local strengthening.

## 9. Stage costs

Use the burst stage cost

$$
\ell_b(x,u,u_{\mathrm{prev}})=
V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x)
+
\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}
\left(
1-\left(\frac{u}{u_{\max}}\right)^2
\right)^2
+
\rho_{\Delta u}
\left(
\frac{u-u_{\mathrm{prev}}}{u_{\max}}
\right)^2.
$$

Here:

$$
\rho_{\mathrm{sat}}\geq 0
$$

weights the preference for saturated burst actuation.

$$
\rho_{\Delta u}\geq 0
$$

weights input smoothness.

$$
u_{\mathrm{prev}}
$$

is the previous applied input.

The saturation attraction term is smooth. It is suitable for SQP style solvers. Do not start with a hard lower input magnitude constraint such as

$$
|u|\geq \eta_u u_{\max},
$$

because that creates a nonconvex feasible set with disconnected branches.

For the current stage, coast input is zero:

$$
u_h=0.
$$

Therefore use the coast stage cost

$$
\ell_c(x)=V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x).
$$

Use terminal cost

$$
\ell_f(x_N)=q_fV_{\mathrm{ep}}(x_N),
$$

where $q_f>0$ is the terminal cost weight.

## 10. Prediction horizon

Use one fixed total prediction horizon.

The passive small angle time scale is

$$
\omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{mgl}{I}}.
$$

Since $\theta=0$ is upright, this quantity is the unstable growth rate near upright and also the small oscillation frequency around the bottom direction. The half period horizon should be interpreted as one half of the small oscillation period about the bottom equilibrium.

Define

$$
T_0=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}.
$$

Use

$$
T_N=\frac{1}{2}T_0.
$$

Then set

$$
N=\operatorname{round}\left(\frac{T_N}{\Delta t}\right).
$$

Here:

$T_0$ is the passive small angle period about the bottom direction.

$T_N$ is the total prediction time.

$N$ is the total number of discrete prediction steps.

The horizon should remain fixed in the first version. Do not optimize $N$ yet.

## 11. Split ratio formulation

Use a finite burst to whole horizon ratio set

$$
\Lambda=\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}.
$$

For each candidate $\lambda\in\Lambda$, define

$$
N_b(\lambda)=
\max\left(
1,
\operatorname{round}(\lambda N)
\right),
$$

$$
N_c(\lambda)=N-N_b(\lambda).
$$

Here:

$N_b$ is the burst horizon.  
$N_c$ is the coast horizon.  
$N$ is the full prediction horizon.  
$\lambda$ is the burst to whole horizon ratio.

The ratio $\lambda$ should not be a continuous decision variable inside SQP. It should be enumerated outside the solver. Each candidate split produces a smooth fixed dimension nonlinear program.

## 12. MPC problem for one split candidate

At decision epoch $k$, let the measured state be $x_{t_k}$. For a fixed $\lambda$, solve

$$
J^\star(x_{t_k},\lambda)=
\min_{v_0,\ldots,v_{N_b-1}}
\left[
\sum_{i=0}^{N_b-1}
\ell_b(x_i,v_i,v_{i-1})
+
\sum_{j=0}^{N_c-1}
\ell_c(x_{N_b+j})
+
\ell_f(x_N)
\right].
$$

The initial condition is

$$
x_0=x_{t_k}.
$$

The burst dynamics are

$$
x_{i+1}=f_d(x_i,v_i;\Delta t),
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,N_b-1.
$$

The coast dynamics are

$$
x_{N_b+j+1}=
f_d(x_{N_b+j},0;\Delta t),
\qquad
j=0,\ldots,N_c-1.
$$

The burst input constraints are

$$
|v_i|\leq u_{\max},
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,N_b-1.
$$

State constraints may be included as

$$
x_i\in\mathcal{X},
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,N.
$$

For the first version, only use state constraints that are necessary for numerical sanity or safety. Do not overload the first benchmark with complicated path constraints.

The selected split is

$$
\lambda^\star_k=
\arg\min_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
J^\star(x_{t_k},\lambda).
$$

After selecting $\lambda_k^\star$, apply the corresponding optimized burst sequence:

$$
u_{t_k+i}=v_i^\star,
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,N_b^\star-1.
$$

Then apply zero coast:

$$
u_{t_k+i}=0,
\qquad
i=N_b^\star,\ldots,N-1.
$$

Then run the next MPC solve at the next planned decision epoch.

## 13. Solver suitability

This formulation is suitable for off-the-shelf nonlinear solvers, including SQP style solvers, because:

1. The dynamics are smooth.
2. The cost terms are smooth.
3. The input bound is simple.
4. The split ratio is enumerated outside the nonlinear program.
5. Each candidate split has fixed dimension.
6. The coast input is fixed at zero in the current stage.
7. Single shooting keeps the number of decision variables small.

Use single shooting first. The decision variables are only

$$
V_k=
\begin{bmatrix}
v_0\\
v_1\\
\vdots\\
v_{N_b-1}
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The predicted states are generated by forward simulation. This is adequate for the two state pendulum benchmark.

## 14. Current stage exclusions

The following elements are intentionally excluded from the current formulation:

1. Learned Q critic.
2. Learned terminal value.
3. Hold last coast input.
4. Early wake trigger.
5. Model mismatch.
6. Process noise.
7. Piece wise linear prediction.
8. CLBF constraints.
9. Continuous time collocation.
10. Spacecraft benchmark.

These are future additions. The current formulation should first establish whether the energy phase cost and split ratio MPC can generate deterministic burst coast swing up behavior.

## 15. Later CLF and CLBF compatibility

The value candidate

$$
V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x)
$$

is designed so it can later become part of a control Lyapunov or control Lyapunov barrier formulation.

A future discrete time CLF condition can be written as

$$
V_{\mathrm{ep}}(f_d(x,u))-V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x)
\leq
-\alpha_V V_{\mathrm{ep}}(x)+s_V,
$$

where:

$\alpha_V \in (0,1)$ is the required decrease rate.

$s_V \geq 0$ is a relaxation slack.

A future discrete time barrier condition can be written as

$$
h(f_d(x,u)) \geq (1-\alpha_h)h(x) - s_h,
$$

where $h(x)\geq 0$ defines the safe set.

$\alpha_h \in (0,1)$ is the barrier rate.

$s_h \geq 0$ is a relaxation slack.

Do not add these constraints in the first implementation. The current requirement is only to keep the cost and feature design compatible with this later extension.

Use:

$$
\theta=0
\quad\text{upright},

$$
V(\theta)=mgl(1+\cos\theta),
$$

$$
E(x)=\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2+mgl(1+\cos\theta),
$$

$$
E^\star=2mgl,
$$

$$
e_E(x)=\frac{E(x)-E^\star}{E^\star}.
$$

Use dynamics:

$$
\dot{\theta}=\omega,
$$

$$
I\dot{\omega}=mgl\sin\theta-b\omega+u.
$$

Use discrete prediction:

$$
x_{k+1}=f_d(x_k,u_k;\Delta t).
$$

Use fixed horizon:

$$
T_N=\frac{1}{2}\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{mgl/I}}.
$$

Use split set:

$$
\Lambda=\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}.
$$

$$
u_h=0.
$$

J^\star(x_{t_k},\lambda)=
\min_{v_0,\ldots,v_{N_b-1}}

This is the recommended formulation for the first deterministic benchmark.
