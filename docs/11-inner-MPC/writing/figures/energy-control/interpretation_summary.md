# Figure 3: energy control and input sensitivity

Read the figure from top to bottom. The teacher requests a total-energy plane, but only part of that plane is mechanically compatible. The constant-torque endpoint curve then shows a narrower, duration-dependent construction, including a work-matching point. The lower panels explain why physical state matters: torque sensitivities change during natural motion, and error-weighted preferences can pass between agreement and conflict.

The relative swing energy is the pendulum's hinge kinetic energy plus potential, rather than the full pendulum-body energy used in the top geometry. Gray quadrants indicate a shared torque sign for the input-dependent parts of the two energy-error derivatives. Drift remains in the complete relative-energy rate; neither the shaded energy subset nor these quadrants are a reachability, coast-duration, constraint, or stability certificate. The traces are newly computed explanatory model diagnostics, not controller-performance records.
