# Furuta Pendulum: Phase Triggered Energy Control with Predictive Constraint Screening

**Implementation handoff | Proposed controller improvement | 2026-11-06**

## 1. Purpose and scope

Develop a **simple, deterministic, dense feedback controller** for swing up of the Furuta pendulum, using phase scheduled torque pulses, proportional mechanical energy correction, and predictive screening of rotary arm angle constraints. This is an **experimental controller alternative and baseline**, not a modification to the existing MPC or reinforcement learning architecture. Preserve existing implementations and add the proposed controller as an independently selectable mode.

The objective is to evaluate whether a physically informed phase schedule with a small finite set of control decisions can achieve swing up with fewer powered intervals or switch ons than an unconstrained dense feedback controller, without unacceptable violations of the rotary arm angle limits. Do **not** claim global swing up, recursive feasibility, or guaranteed safety from this heuristic without additional proof.

The principal method is:

1. Identify the current nominal conservative orbit and its phase, if valid.
2. Consider energy correction torque pulses centered at phase `π/2` and `3π/2`, symmetric in phase and bounded by maximum pulse duration and torque.
3. Set the requested energy increment to a fraction of the remaining total mechanical energy error.
4. Before applying a pulse, compare predicted passive and powered motion over half and one nominal orbit period, enforcing the angle limit **at all points along the predicted trajectories**.
5. Skip an unsafe energy pulse if passive motion remains safe. If passive motion is at risk, enter a braking or recovery mode immediately, subject to actual braking feasibility.
6. Transition to an existing local upright stabilizer within a verified capture region.

This project is **not** intended as a study of any particular researcher's Koopman method. A neural latent model or full offline orbital atlas is *not required* for the first controller implementation.

## 3. Furuta mechanical model and orbital variables

The implemented model has kinetic and potential energies

\[
\begin{aligned}
K &= \tfrac12 A(\alpha)\omega^2+c(\alpha)\omega\nu+\tfrac12 J_p\nu^2,\\
V &= G(1-\cos\alpha),\\
A(\alpha)&=J_b+J_p\sin^2\alpha,\\
c(\alpha)&=J_c\cos\alpha,\\
G&=m_p g l_p.
\end{aligned}
\]

`J_b`, `J_p`, `J_c`, and `G` are model constants from the existing dynamics. In particular, `J_b` includes the arm inertia plus the pendulum mass carried at the arm radius; `J_c` is the arm and pendulum inertial coupling coefficient. Keep exact formulas consistent with the implementation.

The mechanical Hamiltonian / total mechanical energy and generalized rotary arm momentum are

\[
\boxed{H=K+V},\qquad
\boxed{p=A(\alpha)\omega+c(\alpha)\nu},\quad p=p_\theta.
\]

With viscous damping coefficients `b_a` and `b_p` and commanded arm torque `u`, the exact power and momentum balances for this model are

\[
\boxed{\dot H=u\omega-b_a\omega^2-b_p\nu^2},\qquad
\boxed{\dot p=u-b_a\omega}.
\]

The desired upright rest equilibrium has

\[
H^\star=2G,\qquad p^\star=0.
\]

**Caution:** `H=2G, p=0` alone does not identify upright rest. Energy may reside in other degrees of freedom, and the energy objective must not replace physical state based upright capture checks.

### 3.1 Conservative Routh reduction

For `u=0`, `b_a=b_p=0`, both `H` and `p` are conserved. Eliminate arm velocity:

\[
\omega=\frac{p-c(\alpha)\nu}{A(\alpha)}.
\]

Define effective inertia and potential:

\[
D(\alpha)=J_p-\frac{c(\alpha)^2}{A(\alpha)},\qquad
V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha;p)=G(1-\cos\alpha)+\frac{p^2}{2A(\alpha)}.
\]

The reduced energy identity is

\[
\boxed{H=\tfrac12D(\alpha)\nu^2+V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha;p)},\qquad
\nu=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2[H-V_{\mathrm{eff}}(\alpha;p)]}{D(\alpha)}}.
\]

At a regular libration, turning points `α_-`, `α_+` satisfy `H=V_eff`, and the ideal orbit period is

\[
\boxed{T(H,p,\mathcal B)=2\int_{\alpha_-}^{\alpha_+}\sqrt{\frac{D(a)}{2[H-V_{\mathrm{eff}}(a;p)]}}\,da}.
\]

`\mathcal B` identifies the connected orbital branch. For rotations, integrate the time increment over a full `2π` angular revolution instead. This phase and period construction is not globally valid at equilibria, separatrices, or where an orbital family is not identifiable. In particular, the nominal period may diverge near a separatrix.

For a regular libration define

\[
\tau(\alpha;H,p)=\int_{\alpha_-}^{\alpha}\sqrt{\frac{D(a)}{2[H-V_{\mathrm{eff}}(a;p)]}}\,da,
\]

and choose phase

\[
\phi=\begin{cases}
2\pi\tau/T, & \nu>0,\\
2\pi-2\pi\tau/T, & \nu<0.
\end{cases}
\]

with continuous conventions at turning points, modulo `2π`. For the ideal conservative orbit:

\[
\boxed{\dot\phi=\Omega(H,p,\mathcal B),\qquad\Omega=2\pi/T}.
\]

This is the **nominal orbital phase**, constructed by applying established Routh reduction and action angle ideas to the Furuta model. It is **not** an exact phase law under arbitrary motor torque and damping. Keep this distinction visible in code comments and documentation.

For initial implementation, evaluate `H`, `p`, period and phase using either numerical phase quadrature / lookup on a limited tested domain, or use the already implemented geometric phase as an explicitly named provisional trigger with an interface allowing replacement by true orbital phase. **Do not silently label a geometric `atan2` proxy as orbital phase.**

## 4. Control modes and event timing

Three commanded modes:

| Mode | Motor torque | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `COAST` | `0` | Default when no useful safe intervention is scheduled |
| `ENERGY_PULSE` | bounded constant or piecewise constant `u_E` | Correct a fraction of mechanical energy error near a permitted phase center |
| `BRAKE` | bounded feedback braking torque `u_B` | Protect arm angle safety when passive future motion approaches limits |

Optional fourth mode `UPRIGHT_CAPTURE`: delegate to the existing local upright stabilizer only within its tested capture region.

### 4.1 Phase windows

Initial fixed nominal pulse centers:

\[
\boxed{\phi_1=\pi/2,\qquad\phi_2=3\pi/2}.
\]

Each pulse must lie symmetrically around its selected phase center `φ_i`, within a configured half width `Δφ_max`, subject to a maximum powered duration `τ_max`. Define the *desired* interval as `φ_i ± Δφ`, using `Δφ ≤ Δφ_max`. Predict actual phase crossing times under the pulse if the orbital coordinate model is valid; otherwise schedule the symmetric window on a nominal coast trajectory and stop at bounded time or safety intervention. Exact temporal symmetry is not expected because torque changes the phase rate.

**Do not presume these centers maximize energy injection.** Actual instantaneous energy input is `u ω`. The phase of maximal `|ω|` depends on `(H,p,branch)` and may differ from `π/2` and `3π/2`. Use fixed centers for the first controlled experiment, then compare against orbit dependent maxima of `|ω(H,p,φ)|`.

The controller should update densely at every control step, but **start an energy pulse only when a new eligible phase window is encountered**. Maintain a mode latch / event identifier to prevent repeated pulse restarts or double counting switch ons inside the same window. Safety and pulse termination are checked continuously at each control step.

## 5. Proposed pulse energy goal and bounded torque

At a phase opportunity, set a *signed* desired mechanical energy change

\[
\boxed{\Delta H_{\rm goal}=k_E(H^\star-H)},\qquad 0<k_E<1.
\]

Clamp the requested energy change to any configured per pulse energy bound `|ΔH_goal| ≤ ΔH_max` if used. The gain is strictly below unity to prevent a nominal single step from demanding complete energy error correction. The actual increment may differ due to torque dynamics and dissipation.

For a candidate pulse duration `τ_a ≤ τ_max`, the exact energy increment is

\[
\Delta H(u,\tau_a)=\int_{t_0}^{t_0+\tau_a}[u\omega(t)-b_a\omega(t)^2-b_p\nu(t)^2]dt.
\]

For a **first torque estimate only**, neglect damping and use the unactuated predicted arm displacement over the candidate pulse interval:

\[
\Delta\theta_{\rm coast}=\int_{t_0}^{t_0+\tau_a}\omega_{\rm coast}(t)dt,\qquad
u_E^{\rm init}=\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{\Delta H_{\rm goal}}{\Delta\theta_{\rm coast}},-u_{\max},u_{\max}\right).
\]

The variable in this formula is motor **torque** `u_E_init` (the symbol `ν` is reserved for pendulum angular velocity). If `|Δθ_coast|` is smaller than a configured threshold, the estimate is ill conditioned; reject or use a deliberately limited bounded candidate instead. In all cases evaluate actual candidate energy and safety through the complete nonlinear dynamics.

A minimal first implementation can use fixed `τ_a=min(τ_nom, τ_max, time_available_in_window)`, estimate bounded constant torque, and optionally use 1 to 3 short bounded scalar corrections to better match `ΔH_goal`. Avoid a large trajectory optimization. If a requested pulse cannot deliver the target work without exceeding constraints, choose the closest **safe** feasible pulse or skip.

**Avoid symbol confusion:** `u` is torque; `ν` is pendulum angular velocity.

### 5.1 Momentum regulation and pulse score

A total energy target is insufficient for upright capture. Penalize the terminal generalized arm momentum as well:

\[
J_{\rm pulse}=w_H(H_{\rm end}-H^\star)^2+w_p(p_{\rm end}-p^\star)^2,
\]

with positive weights `w_H`, `w_p`. Optionally include arm position and velocity terms, powered duration, and a per switch on cost. Do not assume every energy correction pulse reduces momentum. The secondary momentum objective provides a mechanism for selecting smaller pulses or eventually braking the arm.

For the first heuristic, use a discrete candidate set `{COAST, ENERGY_PULSE, BRAKE}` with safety priority. Do not solve for an arbitrary control trajectory. Positive value from a pulse should mean improvement in the selected terminal objective relative to safe coasting, net of actuation cost.

## 6. Full trajectory predictive constraint screening

### 6.1 Constrained quantity and horizons

Default hard constraint for the study:

\[
\boxed{|\theta(t)|\le\theta_{\max}}.
\]

Use the current configured arm limit and a positive optional margin `δ_θ`, so screening may enforce `|θ| ≤ θ_max - δ_θ`.

Obtain nominal `T_0=T(H,p,branch)` from a validated regular orbit and define

\[
T_{1/2}=0.5T_0,\qquad T_1=T_0.
\]

These are **forecast horizon lengths in seconds**, not guarantees that the actuated or damped trajectory repeats after those durations. Clamp to configured minimum and maximum prediction horizons as needed; do not use near infinite separatrix periods. If phase / period invalid, use a conservative fixed horizon from the baseline physical controller or delegate to a safe fallback.

For candidate mode `m` and horizon `T_h`, integrate the **full nonlinear physical state** and compute the maximum constraint excursion over the whole path:

\[
M_m(T_h)=\max_{0\le s\le T_h}|\theta_m(t+s)|.
\]

Do **not** test only `θ(t+T_half)` or `θ(t+T_one)`; transient overshoot can occur between endpoints. Sample fast enough for the dynamics, use substep sampling or continuous crossing checks, and add robust safety margins for numerical / model errors.

### 6.2 Three forecasts

- `P0`: no input for the prediction horizon.
- `PE`: the scheduled energy pulse for `τ_a`, followed by no input.
- `PB`: braking torque using the braking feedback law (with continuous feedback updates during rollout), followed by a defined safe policy; test its actual feasibility.

Prediction of `PE` or `PB` must start from the current physical state and include the actual torque, damping, mass coupling, and constraint. **Do not** use constant `(H,p)` under actuation or damping.

### 6.3 Decision priority

At each dense update:

1. **Immediate protection:** If `P0` has an unsafe predicted excursion within `T_half` (or another more immediate stopping condition is triggered), evaluate braking now; select a safe feasible brake if available. Do not wait for the next `π/2` or `3π/2` window.
2. At an eligible phase window, compare `P0` and `PE` over the full `T_one` path:
   - both safe: apply a pulse only if its predicted objective is sufficiently better than coasting;
   - passive safe, pulse unsafe: skip the pulse and coast;
   - passive unsafe, pulse safe: the pulse could be necessary for safety, but treat this as a safety decision, not automatically as energy correction; compare against braking;
   - both unsafe: evaluate braking / recovery immediately and flag infeasibility if no admissible safe action is found.
3. Outside a phase window, coast if predicted safe; otherwise brake / recover.
4. During an ongoing pulse, **recompute and interrupt immediately** if the safety screen detects new risk. Do not require finishing a symmetric pulse at the expense of a constraint.
5. Enter `UPRIGHT_CAPTURE` only when the measured physical state satisfies verified local capture conditions.

The suggestion that a skipped pulse will be followed by a braking opportunity is a hypothesis, not a safety guarantee. The controller must actively schedule braking when necessary.

### 6.4 Recursive safety qualification

Finite horizon trajectory screening is a heuristic, not a formal invariant set proof. Safe prediction over one period does not imply recursive feasibility, and a braking command may be insufficient near the boundary. Always log **forecast infeasibility** and **actual violations** separately. A later stage could add a viability / braking margin constraint or high order control barrier function, but a proof is not required for this first implementation.

## 7. Braking law

Write the arm acceleration exactly from the full dynamics as

\[
\ddot\theta=a_\theta(x)+b_\theta(x)u,\qquad
b_\theta(x)=\frac{J_p}{A(\alpha)J_p-c(\alpha)^2}>0.
\]

Choose a desired braking acceleration `θ̈_des = -k_b ω`, with gain `k_b > 0`, and compute

\[
\boxed{
u_{B}^{\rm command}=\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{-k_b\omega-a_\theta(x)}{b_\theta(x)},-u_{\max},u_{\max}\right)}.
\]

The command `u_B` is **torque**, not pendulum angular velocity. This is a feedback linearization inspired arm velocity damper, not an exact stopping guarantee. At zero arm velocity, pendulum coupling can induce later arm acceleration. Roll out braking over the relevant horizon and reject unvalidated assumptions about available deceleration.

When `PB` is itself predicted unsafe, do not silently pass the candidate; report infeasibility / initiate an explicitly documented contingency. For simulation, count constraint violations and test the full region of initial conditions.

## 8. Phase validity and capture switching

The Routh orbital phase is valid only for regular periodic reduced conservative orbits. It becomes singular or unreliable near equilibrium and separatrix regions; torque and damping change the orbit parameters during motion. Required behavior:

- Return `(phase, period, orbit_branch, valid)` from the phase provider.
- Support a validated orbital lookup provider and a separately named geometric proxy provider for tests.
- If the orbital phase is invalid, do not schedule nominal phase window pulses based on fabricated phase; use a clearly specified fallback controller or full state short horizon decision mode.
- **From exact downward rest**, an energy only pulse law based on nonzero arm velocity may not initiate motion. Add an explicit, bounded symmetry breaking excitation / startup mode if simulation demonstrates this failure.
- Near upright, enter a local stabilizer only when `wrap(α-π)`, pendulum velocity `ν`, arm angle `θ`, and arm velocity `ω` all satisfy configured capture thresholds and the existing stabilizer's domain has been validated. Use hysteresis to avoid chattering.
- Never infer capture from `H≈2G` alone.

If safety control overrides phase pulses frequently, characterize whether the nominal schedule is compatible with the arm constraint rather than hiding the overrides.

## 9. Proposed minimal control loop

```python
# Pseudocode; adapt to existing simulator/control interfaces.
def control_step(x, phase_provider, state_machine, cfg):
    H = total_mechanical_energy(x)
    p = generalized_arm_momentum(x)
    phase, period, branch, valid = phase_provider(x)

    if in_verified_capture_region(x, cfg):
        return local_upright_controller(x), "UPRIGHT_CAPTURE"

    if not valid:
        return safe_fallback_full_state_controller(x), "PHASE_INVALID_FALLBACK"

    T_half = bounded_horizon(0.5 * period, cfg)
    T_one = bounded_horizon(period, cfg)
    coast_half = rollout_full_ode(x, mode="COAST", horizon=T_half)

    if not path_within_arm_limit(coast_half, cfg):
        brake = rollout_full_ode(x, mode="BRAKE", horizon=T_half)
        if path_within_arm_limit(brake, cfg):
            return braking_torque(x, cfg), "BRAKE"
        return infeasibility_contingency(x, cfg), "NO_SAFE_BRAKE"

    if state_machine.pulse_active:
        return continue_or_interrupt_pulse_with_safety_recheck(x, cfg)

    if not newly_entered_eligible_phase_window(phase, branch, state_machine, cfg):
        return 0.0, "COAST"

    pulse = build_energy_pulse_candidate(x, H, p, phase, period, cfg)
    coast_one = rollout_full_ode(x, mode="COAST", horizon=T_one)
    pulse_one = rollout_full_ode(x, mode="ENERGY_PULSE", candidate=pulse, horizon=T_one)

    if path_within_arm_limit(pulse_one, cfg):
        if pulse_improves_objective(pulse_one, coast_one, cfg):
            state_machine.start_pulse(pulse)
            return pulse.current_torque, "ENERGY_PULSE"

    if path_within_arm_limit(coast_one, cfg):
        return 0.0, "COAST"

    brake = rollout_full_ode(x, mode="BRAKE", horizon=T_one)
    if path_within_arm_limit(brake, cfg):
        return braking_torque(x, cfg), "BRAKE"
    return infeasibility_contingency(x, cfg), "NO_SAFE_ACTION"
```

The pseudocode shows intended priorities, not an exact API contract. Dense safety checks must continue even outside phase opportunities. Use an appropriate safety evaluation horizon when `T_half` is too short to establish stopping feasibility.

## 10. Development sequence

### Stage A: Conservative phase and sensitivity diagnostics

1. Validate `H(x)` and `p(x)` against existing dynamics; verify conservation in undamped, unforced simulations.
2. Implement regular orbit identification, `T(H,p,branch)` and `φ(x)` on a limited tested domain, initially with numerical quadrature or interpolation. Do not begin with a large atlas if a smaller domain suffices.
3. For several orbits, plot `ω(H,p,φ)` and compare `π/2`, `3π/2` with true phase dependent peaks of `|ω|`.
4. Compare phase predicted motion to the full ODE; explicitly mark invalid orbit domains and singularities.

### Stage B: Dense heuristic controller, full ODE predictions

1. Implement mode machine, pulse event latch, proportional energy goal, pulse torque estimation, constant torque pulse duration constraint, and horizon forecasts.
2. Implement the arm constraint screening over the *entire* forecast.
3. Implement braking, infeasibility signaling, startup excitation if needed, and local upright capture.
4. Use full ODE rollouts for all candidate forecasts. **Do not** use ideal constant `H,p` propagation for actuated candidates.

### Stage C: Evaluate and refine

1. Compare fixed `π/2,3π/2` windows against identified orbit dependent `|ω|` peaks.
2. Compare fixed pulse durations against a minimal discrete duration set, without general MPC.
3. Tune `k_E`, momentum weight, phase half width, braking gain, safety margin, and capture thresholds using training scenarios; report results on separate held out scenarios.
4. Only then test replacing repeated passive full ODE rollouts with orbital atlas prediction to measure speedup and fidelity loss.

## 11. Experiment design and required metrics

Use the same physical parameters, integration tolerances, and initial conditions as existing control experiments. Include exact downward rest, perturbed downward, nonzero initial pendulum velocity, nonzero arm velocity, and near upright cases, within admissible initial states.

Compare at minimum:

- Existing energy controller or current MPC baseline (unchanged).
- New phase triggered controller without predictive screening, for ablation.
- New controller with full predictive safety screening and braking.
- Optional dynamically chosen phase centers versus fixed nominal centers.

Record:

- Upright capture rate and capture time, with complete physical state capture criteria.
- Final upright errors, and post capture holding performance.
- Peak `|θ|`, constraint violation count, and maximum violation magnitude.
- Powered duration, number of **actuation switch ons**, total absolute torque impulse `∫|u|dt`, and work `∫uωdt`.
- Number of scheduled, executed, skipped, interrupted pulses and braking events.
- Prediction feasibility failures, actual prediction errors, and invalid phase occurrences.
- Average and worst case controller compute time per dense step.
- Predicted versus actual `ΔH` for each pulse, and momentum after each intervention.

Do not confuse the count of nonzero torque *samples* with the number of *actuation switch ons*. Count a switch on only when transitioning from coast to powered actuation, according to an explicit zero torque / hysteresis threshold.
