# 5.7.2 New Formulation Decisions

## Part I — Human-readable decisions

## 1. Recommended formulation

Use a **variable-amplitude, single-enabled-prefix burst-coast controller**:

1. The learned scheduler selects only the replanning horizon $H$.
2. The inner controller selects the burst cutoff $B$ and a feasible continuous torque sequence.
3. Each event contains one enabled prefix followed by one nonempty exact-zero coast suffix.
4. Torque magnitude and torque-change penalties are positive but weak; they prefer minimum sufficient effort, not saturation.
5. Parallel fixed-$(B,H)$ nonlinear MPC is the deterministic reference. MPPI, CEM, and continuous-duration optimization remain acceleration or research extensions.
6. No artificial activation budget is added. Global quantities enter the state only when they affect future dynamics, feasibility, termination, or cost.

Certifiable and explainable neural networks are explicitly outside the present scope. This method is also not exact bang-off-bang control because the enabled torque is continuous rather than restricted to $\pm u_{\max}$.

## 2. Current-code boundary

The active implementation is narrower than the proposal:

- `src/inverted_pendulum/mpc/solver.py` solves continuous burst torques for one fixed burst/coast split and imposes exact zero torque during coast.
- `src/inverted_pendulum/mpc/controller.py` evaluates configured split ratios serially at one fixed total horizon in MPC-only mode.
- `src/inverted_pendulum/RL/policy.py` scores a joint $(B,H)$ grid in one neural batch, selects one pair, and solves only that selected MPC candidate.
- No inverted-pendulum MPPI controller currently exists in the repository.
- The current MPC has normalized energy error and a small torque-change term active, but no positive torque-magnitude term or terminal value.

Accordingly, parallel horizon MPC, $H$-only learning, MPPI, and continuous-duration optimization below are proposed directions, not claims about current runtime behavior.

## 3. Event state and execution contract

Index replanning events by $j=0,1,\ldots$. One event is fully executed before the next replan, except when terminal success or the fixed episode boundary stops it early.

With a guaranteed nonempty coast suffix, use the event state

$$
s_j=(x_j,r_j,g_j).
$$

Here $s_j$ is the Markov state at event $j$; $x_j=(\theta_j,\omega_j)$ is the measured pendulum angle in radians and angular velocity in radians per second; $r_j=N_{\max}-t_j$ is the computed number of physics steps remaining before the configured episode limit $N_{\max}$; $t_j$ is the executed-step index; and $g_j$ is the measured consecutive-goal hold count. These quantities are observed or computed, not tuned.

The previous torque is unnecessary at event boundaries only if every event finishes its zero-input coast. If early replanning can end an event with nonzero torque, add the previous applied torque $u_j^-$ because the torque-change cost depends on it.

## 4. Governing constraints

For a candidate horizon $H$, solve

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{i+1} &= F_{\Delta t}(x_i,u_i), && i=0,\ldots,H-1,\\
z_i &\in\{0,1\}, && i=0,\ldots,H-1,\\
-u_{\max}z_i &\le u_i\le u_{\max}z_i, && i=0,\ldots,H-1,\\
z_{i+1} &\le z_i, && i=0,\ldots,H-2,\\
z_0 &=1,\qquad z_{H-1}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $i$ indexes physics steps inside the event; $H\in\mathbb N$ is the number of planned steps until the next replan; $x_0=x_j$; $F_{\Delta t}$ is the known nonlinear one-step RK4 dynamics over the fixed sampling period $\Delta t$ seconds; $u_i$ is torque in newton-metres; $u_{\max}>0$ is the physical torque limit from plant configuration; and $z_i$ is a binary enable variable. When $z_i=0$, the bounds force $u_i=0$ exactly. Monotonicity permits one prefix of ones followed by zeros. The endpoint constraints require both a nonempty enabled prefix and a nonempty coast, so $H\ge2$.

The implicit burst length is

$$
B=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}z_i,
\qquad 1\le B\le H-1.
$$

Here $B$ is computed from the optimized or enumerated mask, not learned. Its physical duration is $B\Delta t$, and coast duration is $(H-B)\Delta t$. Because $z$ is monotone, the sum identifies one cutoff exactly. However, $z_i=1$ means torque is permitted, not necessarily nonzero; this formulation counts enabled episodes rather than nonzero-torque samples.

## 5. Objective and torque preference

Without a mask-dependent cost, $B$ is not identifiable. If $B_1<B_2$ and $\mathcal U_B$ is the feasible torque set for cutoff $B$, then

$$
\mathcal U_{B_1}\subseteq\mathcal U_{B_2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
J_0^*(B_2)\le J_0^*(B_1).
$$

Here $J_0^*(B)$ is the minimum objective without an enable-duration term. A longer prefix can reproduce every shorter solution by assigning zero to its additional allowed torques, so the optimizer weakly prefers the largest $B$ unless the objective explicitly values switch-off time.

Use the dimensionless event cost

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_j={}&c_{\mathrm{event}}+w_t d_j\Delta t
+\sum_{i=0}^{d_j-1}\ell_x(x_i)
+w_z\sum_{i=0}^{d_j-1}z_i\\
&+\varepsilon_u\sum_{i=0}^{d_j-1}
\left(\frac{u_i}{u_{\max}}\right)^2
+\varepsilon_{\Delta u}\sum_{i=0}^{d_j-1}
\left(\frac{u_i-u_{i-1}}{2u_{\max}}\right)^2
+\Phi(x_{d_j}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $c_j$ is computed after deterministic rollout; $c_{\mathrm{event}}>0$ prices one replan-plus-enable event; $d_j\le H$ is the realized number of executed steps; $w_t\ge0$ prices physical time; $\ell_x(x_i)\ge0$ is the task-progress cost computed from state; $w_z>0$ prices enabled duration and makes $B$ identifiable; $\varepsilon_u>0$ is a weak normalized torque-magnitude weight; $\varepsilon_{\Delta u}>0$ is a weak normalized torque-change weight; and $\Phi(x_{d_j})\ge0$ is the terminal task cost. Division by $u_{\max}$ makes torque magnitude order one at saturation, while division by $2u_{\max}$ makes the largest possible signed torque jump order one.

All weights are fixed experimental priorities. Normalize the terms first, then use a small logarithmic sweep. A valid choice preserves task completion, produces a repeatable cutoff, and uses torque terms only to break near-ties.

### Decision: prefer minimum, not maximum, torque

Set $\varepsilon_u>0$ and weakly prefer the smallest feasible magnitude. Do not reward maximum torque through a negative quadratic or saturation-seeking term: it encourages unnecessary saturation, increases nonconvexity and model sensitivity, and contradicts variable-amplitude control. If saturation is actually required, the task-progress and time costs will drive $u_i$ to the physical bounds naturally.

Keep $\varepsilon_{\Delta u}>0$ small. It provides a shallow smooth gradient and suppresses meaningless sign chatter without preventing decisive pulses.

## 6. Learn $H$ only versus learn $(B,H)$

Define the optimal horizon-conditioned value

$$
Q_H^*(s,H)=
\min_{z,\mathbf u\;\mathrm{feasible}}
\left[c(s,H,z,\mathbf u)+(1-\delta)V^*(s^+)\right],
\qquad
V^*(s)=\min_{H\in\mathcal H(s)}Q_H^*(s,H).
$$

Here $Q_H^*(s,H)$ is the minimum remaining cost after committing to horizon $H$ from event state $s$; $\mathbf u=(u_0,\ldots,u_{H-1})$ is the torque sequence; $z$ is the enable mask; $c$ is immediate event cost; $s^+$ is the successor event state; $\delta$ is one at terminal transitions and zero otherwise; $V^*$ is optimal future cost; and $\mathcal H(s)$ is the finite admissible horizon set.

A learned critic $\widehat Q_\phi(s,H)$ with trained parameters $\phi$ selects

$$
\widehat H(s)=\arg\min_{H\in\mathcal H(s)}\widehat Q_\phi(s,H).
$$

Here $\widehat H(s)$ is the chosen horizon. This is a finite-grid critic policy, not actor-critic. If exact horizon search is already fast enough, use the exact table or direct search and omit the neural model.

| Criterion | Learn $H$; solve $B,u$ inside | Learn $(B,H)$; solve $u$ inside |
|---|---|---|
| Learned action space | Smaller | Larger Cartesian grid |
| Data efficiency | More coverage per horizon | Samples spread across timing pairs |
| Torque–cutoff consistency | $B$ is chosen with its torque trajectory | Critic chooses $B$ before the new torque solve |
| Online low-level cost | Still searches $B$ | One fixed-$B$ solve after selection |
| Long-term coupling | Correct only if each $H$ target minimizes $B,u$ with downstream value | Represented directly by $Q(s,B,H)$ |
| Ranking difficulty | Lower | Higher for near-tied pairs |
| Main failure | A myopic or inaccurate inner $B$ solve corrupts $Q_H$ | Critic ranking can select a poor timing pair |

**Decision:** learn $H$ only in the primary formulation. $H$ is the global feedback-scheduling decision; $B$ is a within-event switching decision that should remain coupled to feasible torque. This is better only when every $H$ label genuinely searches or minimizes over $B$ and $\mathbf u$. Joint $(B,H)$ learning remains the required ablation and may win if inner cutoff search is the measured online bottleneck.

### 6.1 Action value or state value with a stopping rule?

There are three distinct value objects:

| Learned object | What it answers | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| $Q(s,B,H)$ | “What is the remaining cost if this exact timing pair is chosen?” | Directly ranks the existing joint grid | Learns a larger action space and chooses $B$ before the new torque solve |
| $Q_H(s,H)$ | “What is the remaining cost for this $H$ after the inner controller chooses $B,u$?” | Directly ranks horizons while preserving the selected decomposition | Every label must contain a valid inner minimization over $B,u$ |
| $V(s)$ | “What is the minimum remaining cost before choosing the next timing action?” | Compact terminal cost for MPC and reusable across action sets | Does not by itself say which horizon to choose or when to stop |

The two recommended value functions are related by

$$
V^*(s)=\min_{H\in\mathcal H(s)}Q_H^*(s,H).
$$

Here $V^*(s)$ is the optimal remaining cost before selecting a horizon, and $Q_H^*(s,H)$ is the cost after committing to $H$ and optimally choosing the inner cutoff and torque. Therefore, $V$ can be derived from the solved horizon-conditioned values; it need not replace them.

Do not stop a predicted rollout by applying an absolute heuristic threshold to $V(s_i)$. A low value can mean that the state is promising, but it does not compare the price of replanning now with the benefit of continuing the current plan. A defensible stopping rule is an optimal-stopping comparison:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{stop}}(s_i) &= c_{\mathrm{event}}+V(s_i),\\
C_{\mathrm{continue}}(s_i) &=
\min_{u_i,z_i\;\mathrm{feasible}}
\left[\ell_i(s_i,u_i,z_i)+W(s_{i+1})\right],\\
W(s_i) &= \min\left\{C_{\mathrm{stop}}(s_i),C_{\mathrm{continue}}(s_i)\right\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $s_i$ is a predicted state at possible stopping step $i$; $C_{\mathrm{stop}}$ is the cost of ending the current event and paying for a new plan; $C_{\mathrm{continue}}$ is the best cost of executing at least one more step; $\ell_i$ is that step's cost; and $W$ is the optimal stopping value. Stop only when $C_{\mathrm{stop}}\le C_{\mathrm{continue}}$. This comparison is itself action-conditioned and reintroduces a stop-versus-continue $Q$ decision; it is not a rule obtainable from $V$ alone.

**Decision:** learn $Q_H(s,H)$ as the primary scheduler, derive $V(s)=\min_H Q_H(s,H)$ for use as MPC terminal cost, and retain $Q(s,B,H)$ as the joint-grid ablation. Do not use a heuristic $V$ threshold to terminate rollout. If adaptive stopping is later desired, implement the formal stop-versus-continue Bellman comparison or optimize horizon explicitly by grid search or free-duration NLP.

## 7. One prefix versus several on/off segments

Multiple activation segments would remove monotonicity and introduce activation indicators such as

$$
y_i\ge z_i-z_{i-1},
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{on}}=\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}y_i.
$$

Here $y_i\in\{0,1\}$ indicates an off-to-on transition, $z_{-1}$ is the enable mode before the event, and $K_{\mathrm{on}}$ counts within-plan activation episodes. Additional upper bounds would make $y_i$ exact.

| Criterion | One enabled prefix | Several activation segments |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed-$H$ patterns | $H-1$ cutoffs | Up to exponentially many masks |
| Feedback | Before every new pulse | Several pulses execute open loop |
| Counts | Activation count equals replan count | Pulses can be packed into one replan |
| Model-error sensitivity | Lower | Higher for later open-loop pulses |
| Flexibility | Lower within one event, but repeated events create repeated pulses | Higher within one solve |
| Research identity | Burst-coast scheduling | General switched/sparse-control MPC |

**Decision:** use exactly one enabled prefix followed by coast in each event. Multiple pulses remain possible across events, with feedback before each pulse. Add multi-segment plans only if experiments show that single-prefix feedback cannot complete the task or that planning computation makes several open-loop pulses demonstrably preferable; then activation and replan counts must be reported separately.

## 8. Horizon-solution methods

The binary formulation specifies the feasible structure; it does not require a mixed-integer solver. For fixed $H$, the monotone mask has only $H-1$ admissible nonempty burst/coast patterns, so cutoff enumeration is its exact specialized combinatorial solver.

| Method | Pros | Cons | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parallel fixed-$(B,H)$ nonlinear MPC | Deterministic; explicit constraints; exact coast; reuses CasADi/RK4 | Many local NLPs; local optima; solver overhead | **Primary reference** |
| $H$ critic plus inner $B$ search | Small learned action and fast horizon ranking | Needs Bellman-consistent labels and an inner cutoff solve | **Primary learned architecture** |
| Fixed-$H$ MPPI with sampled $B,u$ | Parallel nonlinear rollouts; derivative-free; multimodal exploration | Stochastic; constraint penalties/rejection; sample tuning; not implemented yet | **Acceleration experiment** |
| Joint discrete CEM over $(B,H)$ | Categorical timing fits elite updates naturally | Approximate; possible early distribution collapse | Cleaner than calling discrete horizon perturbation canonical MPPI |
| Continuous two-phase duration NLP | No binary variables; compact smooth formulation | Time-scaling conditioning, local minima, rounding | **Future research direction** |
| Nonlinear monotone-binary MINLP | Direct unified encoding | One binary per physics step and difficult branch-and-bound | Reference or coarse MIQP benchmark only |

### 8.1 Deterministic reference

Use

$$
(B^*,H^*,\mathbf u^*)=
\arg\min_{H\in\mathcal H}
\min_{B\in\{1,\ldots,H-1\}}
\min_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal U(B,H)}J(s,B,H,\mathbf u).
$$

Here $\mathcal U(B,H)$ is the continuous feasible torque set for one fixed cutoff and $J$ is the common deterministic Bellman-consistent objective. Each fixed-$(B,H)$ continuous problem is independent and can be prepared and solved in parallel. This is equivalent to prefix-mask search without a nonlinear mixed-integer solve.

### 8.2 MPPI and CEM

MPPI is an importance-sampling-based MPC method designed for parallel nonlinear rollout optimization ([Williams, Aldrich, and Theodorou](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01149); [Williams et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02342)). For a small set of horizon anchors $H_a$, sample cutoff $B_{a,k}$ and torque perturbation by

$$
u_{a,k,i}=\mathbf 1_{\{i<B_{a,k}\}}
\operatorname{clip}
\left(\bar u_{a,i}+\epsilon_{a,k,i},-u_{\max},u_{\max}\right).
$$

Here $a$ indexes horizons, $k$ indexes rollouts, $i$ indexes steps, $\bar u_{a,i}$ is nominal torque, $\epsilon_{a,k,i}$ is sampled noise, $\operatorname{clip}$ enforces bounds, and the indicator enforces exact coast. Score every sample with the same objective and terminal value, then roll out updated nominals without noise before comparing horizons. If both $B$ and $H$ are categorical random variables, call the timing search CEM or elite shooting; reserve MPPI for continuous torque updates conditional on timing anchors.

### 8.3 Continuous free-duration alternative

Optimize continuous burst and coast durations $T_b,T_c>0$ using normalized phase $\tau\in[0,1]$:

$$
\frac{dx_b}{d\tau}=T_b f(x_b,u_b),
\qquad
\frac{dx_c}{d\tau}=T_c f(x_c,0),
\qquad T=T_b+T_c.
$$

Here $x_b,x_c$ are phase states, $u_b$ is burst control, $f$ is continuous dynamics, and $T$ is time until replan. Time scaling changes physical duration while retaining a fixed node count. Free-final-time model-based optimization is established in optimal-control literature ([Sun, Theodorou, and Tsiotras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01186)). Map the result to the simulator by

$$
B=\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{T_b}{\Delta t}\right),
\qquad
H=\operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{T_b+T_c}{\Delta t}\right),
$$

where $\operatorname{round}$ returns the nearest executable step count. The rounded plan must be re-simulated and re-scored. This is the most mathematically elegant “optimize the horizon inside the NLP” direction, but it should follow the discrete reference because conditioning and rounding introduce new approximations.

### Solver decision

1. Build parallel fixed-$(B,H)$ MPC first.
2. Add $\widehat Q_\phi(s,H)$ only if exact horizon search is a measured bottleneck.
3. Evaluate fixed-$H$ MPPI for torque/cutoff sampling or CEM for categorical timing.
4. Study continuous two-phase duration as the principal future formulation extension.

### 8.4 Should MPPI replace a project component?

Standard MPPI is a sampling optimizer, not the hard-constraint authority in this project. Constraint-aware MPPI variants add safety filters or barrier machinery precisely because vanilla MPPI commonly represents state constraints through costs or sample rejection ([Gandhi, Almubarak, and Theodorou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03441)). The present project should keep constrained MPC as the final executable-plan owner.

| Proposed MPPI role | Benefit | Main problem | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replace MPC solves during RL-grid label generation, then distill | Batched rollouts may generate many approximate labels quickly | Learns the cost and successors of the MPPI proposal controller; infeasible or differently optimized edges do not equal deployed-MPC edges | **Do not use for authoritative labels** |
| Replace the learned horizon grid with online soft/perturbed-$H$ MPPI | Adapts online without a trained horizon critic and explores unseen horizons | $H$ is discrete, rollout lengths differ, comparisons are stochastic, and final constraint-compliant MPC solves are still required | **Do not use as the primary scheduler** |
| Use MPPI without replacing any owner | Supplies torque/cutoff proposals, warm starts, hard examples, and top-$k$ candidates; MPC preserves feasibility and final scoring | Adds a second optimizer and is useful only if it reduces measured total cost or latency | **Recommended MPPI role** |

If MPPI replaces MPC while producing training transitions, the learned quantity is approximately

$$
Q_{\mathrm{MPPI}}(s,B,H),
$$

where $Q_{\mathrm{MPPI}}$ is the remaining cost under MPPI-generated low-level trajectories. The deployed controller instead needs $Q_{\mathrm{MPC}}(s,B,H)$ or $Q_{H,\mathrm{MPC}}(s,H)$, defined by constrained MPC-generated transitions. These values coincide only if MPPI and MPC produce the same feasible successor and immediate cost, which should not be assumed.

Use the proposal-to-label pipeline

$$
(B,\mathbf u)_{\mathrm{MPPI}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\text{constrained MPC feasibility and refinement}
\;\longrightarrow\;
(c_{\mathrm{MPC}},s^+_{\mathrm{MPC}},\delta)
\;\longrightarrow\;
y_{\mathrm{MPC}}.
$$

Here $(B,\mathbf u)_{\mathrm{MPPI}}$ is an MPPI-proposed cutoff and torque sequence; $c_{\mathrm{MPC}}$ is the deterministically rescored immediate event cost; $s^+_{\mathrm{MPC}}$ is the successor obtained from the accepted MPC plan; $\delta$ is the terminal flag; and $y_{\mathrm{MPC}}$ is the authoritative Bellman training label. MPPI may pretrain a proposal network or shortlist candidates, but final critic fitting must use MPC-accepted transitions or clearly be labeled as proposal pretraining followed by MPC fine-tuning.

For soft-horizon exploration, sample a small distribution over discrete $H$ anchors, retain the best few, solve each with constrained MPC, and train from those accepted results. Because this is categorical horizon search, call it CEM or stochastic horizon screening rather than soft-horizon MPPI. It is an optional data-collection accelerator, not the deployed scheduling rule.

**Final MPPI decision:** do not replace MPC, the Bellman label owner, or the $Q_H$ scheduler with MPPI. Use MPPI only as an offline/online auxiliary proposal mechanism, and retain it only if controlled experiments show lower end-to-end label-generation cost or planning latency at unchanged MPC feasibility and closed-loop performance.

## 9. Global planning quantities

Use one rule for MPC and MPPI:

> Include a quantity in the event state only if it changes future dynamics, feasible actions, termination, or future cost.

| Quantity | Include now? | Reason |
|---|---:|---|
| Physical state $x_j$ | Yes | Determines dynamics and task progress |
| Previous torque $u_j^-$ | Conditional | Needed for $\Delta u$ unless completed coast guarantees zero |
| Remaining episode steps $r_j$ | Yes in the finite task | Deadline failure depends on it |
| Goal-hold count $g_j$ | Yes | Terminal success depends on it |
| Activations or replans already spent | No | Sunk additive cost without a hard cap |
| Activation budget-to-go $q_j$ | No | No physical or mission budget is declared |
| Elapsed time | Usually no | Use remaining time only when a deadline matters |
| Fuel, battery, temperature, recharge | Not yet | Include only after modeled in dynamics or constraints |

The total event count is already accumulated by

$$
K_{\mathrm{event}}=\sum_j1.
$$

Here $K_{\mathrm{event}}$ is the total number of replanning/enabling events. Past events are already paid for and cannot affect the optimal continuation without a hard budget.

If a real maximum $K_{\max}$ is later imposed, add

$$
q_{j+1}=q_j-1,
\qquad q_0=K_{\max},
\qquad q_j\ge0.
$$

Here $q_j$ is remaining activations before event $j$, and $K_{\max}$ must come from a physical, mission, or experimental requirement. It then belongs in the state because it changes future feasibility. Do not introduce it only to make the formulation appear global.

## 10. Defensible claim and research path

The primary claim is:

> The method separates feedback scheduling from feasible continuous control: an exact or learned event-level value chooses when to replan, while a structured inner optimizer chooses the earliest useful switch from bounded variable-amplitude control to exact coast.

This leaves clear extensions: joint $(B,H)$ learning as an ablation, MPPI/CEM acceleration, continuous timing, multi-segment activation only when empirically needed, and resource-to-go state only for a real constrained resource.

## 11. Validation milestones

### Milestone A — deterministic reference

Validate parallel fixed-$(B,H)$ solves, bounded torque, $z_0=1$, $z_{H-1}=0$, exact-zero coast, complete event execution, and deterministic same-state selection.

Success flag: all constraints hold and repeated same-state solves select the same deterministically scored candidate.

### Milestone B — identifiable cutoff and weak regularization

Sweep normalized $w_z$, $\varepsilon_u$, and $\varepsilon_{\Delta u}$ over a small logarithmic range. Compare completion, task cost, $B$, torque energy, torque variation, and convergence.

Success flag: task completion is preserved, $B$ is repeatable, and unnecessary saturation and chatter disappear.

### Milestone C — $H$-only scheduler

Create same-state labels by minimizing every $H$ over retained $B$ values and feasible torque solves. Compare $H$-only and joint $(B,H)$ critics with exhaustive enumeration using top-1 agreement, top-$k$ recall, regret, feasibility, total cost, event count, completion time, and planning latency.

Also derive $V(s)=\min_H Q_H(s,H)$ and test it as terminal cost inside the same constrained MPC. If adaptive stopping is tested, compare formal stop-versus-continue costs; an absolute $V$ threshold is not a valid baseline for optimal stopping.

Success flag: the $H$-only scheduler preserves exhaustive-search closed-loop performance within the declared tolerance while reducing online selection cost.

### Milestone D — optional sampling acceleration

Validate batched RK4 parity, increasing sample budgets, multiple random seeds, deterministic rescoring, and exact refinement of top candidates.

Report MPPI proposal top-$k$ recall, MPC acceptance rate, pre/post-refinement cost gap, accepted-label throughput, and the closed-loop effect of proposal pretraining followed by MPC-label fine-tuning.

Success flag: MPPI/CEM meets the same feasibility and task metrics with lower measured online latency, not merely a lower noisy sample score.

These milestones support a show-of-concept claim, not global continuous-state optimality, formal safety, or industry-ready realtime performance.
