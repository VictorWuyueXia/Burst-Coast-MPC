# CR3BP Problems for Learned-Horizon Burst-Coast MPC

The best first CR3BP benchmark is Earth-Moon planar $L_1$ Lyapunov-orbit
stationkeeping. It is simple enough to implement cleanly, but unlike the
pendulums it has genuine long passive arcs, phase-dependent instability, sparse
correction opportunities, and a meaningful trade-off between waiting and
replanning.

The recommended progression is:

| Priority | Problem | Difficulty | Main scientific signal |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Planar Lyapunov-orbit stationkeeping | Low | Learned state/phase-dependent replanning |
| 2 | Stable-manifold injection and capture | Medium | Long ballistic coast with short correction bursts |
| 3 | Far rendezvous to a hold point | Medium-high | Learned scheduling around a moving target and safety constraints |

The current [project formulation](801-presentation.md#2-general-formulation-and-fast-grid-computation)
transfers directly: RL selects $(B_k,H_k)$, the inner MPC/MPPI selects thrust,
and the complete burst plus exact zero-thrust coast is executed. The current
CR3BP package is only a scaffold, so none of the pendulum-specific energy,
phase, critic, or normalization should be reused numerically.

## Common CR3BP Formulation

Begin with the nondimensional planar Earth-Moon CR3BP:

$$
x=[x,y,\dot x,\dot y]^\top,\qquad
\dot x=f_{\mathrm{CR3BP}}(x)+
\begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\u_x\\u_y\end{bmatrix},
\qquad \|u\|_2\le a_{\max}.
$$

The standard rotating-frame equations and Jacobi constant are documented in
NASA's [Astrodynamics Convention and Modeling Reference](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220014814/downloads/NASA%20TP%2020220014814%20final.pdf).

At replanning epoch $k$:

$$
a_k=(B_k,H_k),\qquad
u_{i|k}=
\begin{cases}
v_{i|k},&i<B_k,\\
0,&B_k\le i<H_k.
\end{cases}
$$

All $H_k$ commands are executed before replanning. Thus $H_k$ is not merely a
prediction horizon: it is also the next decision time. That coupling is
precisely what makes the CR3BP useful.

Keep two objectives separate:

$$
J_{\mathrm{inner}}
=
J_{\mathrm{predicted\ task}}
+\lambda_uJ_{\mathrm{thrust}}
+\lambda_{\Delta u}J_{\mathrm{smooth}}
+V_f(x_H),
$$

which MPC/MPPI uses to calculate thrust, versus

$$
c_k=
w_eJ_{\mathrm{executed\ error}}
+w_{\Delta v}\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\|u\|\,dt
+w_t\Delta t_k
+w_c t_{\mathrm{solve},k}
+w_r
+P_f\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{failure}},
\qquad r_k=-c_k,
$$

which trains the timing critic. Here $w_r$ is a per-replan charge. The learned
target must approximate closed-loop Bellman cost,

$$
Q^*(s,a)=c(s,a)+\gamma\min_{a'}Q^*(s',a'),
$$

not an MPC-objective imitation or a Monte Carlo return under random
continuation.

## 1. Planar $L_1$ Lyapunov-Orbit Stationkeeping — Recommended First

### Task and Goal

Generate one planar Earth-Moon $L_1$ Lyapunov reference orbit $\bar x(\phi)$.
Perturb the initial position and velocity and maintain the spacecraft inside a
prescribed tube around that orbit for, for example, 10-20 revolutions.

The goal is not to reach a point. It is:

$$
d_r(x,\bar x)\le \rho_{\max},\qquad
d_v(x,\bar x)\le v_{\max}
$$

throughout the fixed mission duration, while minimizing thrust, computation,
and replans.

Libration-point orbits are unstable and require correction under navigation,
modeling, and execution errors. NASA has examined finite maneuvers, skipped
maneuvers, and perturbations for NRHO stationkeeping, while recent MPC work uses
approximately one maneuver per orbital revolution rather than continuous
control: [NASA NRHO stationkeeping study](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/20180006800.pdf),
[full-state targeting MPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05013).

### Constraints

- Exact zero acceleration during the coast suffix.
- Finite low-thrust bound $\|u_i\|_2\le a_{\max}$.
- Maximum duty cycle $B/H\le\beta_{\max}$.
- Position and velocity tracking tubes.
- Earth and Moon keep-out radii.
- Fixed mission duration and terminal survival.
- Random initial state error and occasional small velocity perturbations.

The disturbances are essential. With an exact initial condition, exact CR3BP
model, and no perturbations, the reference orbit simply coasts and the task
becomes trivial.

### MPC Loss

Use phase-aligned transverse error rather than raw distance to a single
time-indexed point:

$$
J_{\mathrm{MPC}}
=
\sum_{i=1}^{H}
\left(
q_\perp\|e_{\perp,i}\|^2
+q_\phi e_{\phi,i}^2
\right)
+
\sum_{i=0}^{B-1}
\left(
r_u\|u_i\|_2^2
+r_{\Delta u}\|u_i-u_{i-1}\|_2^2
\right)
+
q_f\|e_H\|^2.
$$

A particularly interpretable state representation projects tracking error
onto the orbit's Floquet modes:

$$
s_k=[
e_{\mathrm{unstable}},
e_{\mathrm{stable}},
e_{\mathrm{center},1},
e_{\mathrm{center},2},
\sin\phi,\cos\phi,
t_{\mathrm{remaining}}
].
$$

This lets the critic learn that the same Euclidean error can require different
replanning times depending on whether it points along a stable or unstable
direction.

### RL Reward

For this fixed-duration task, omit a time-to-arrival incentive:

$$
c_k=
w_e\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\|\tilde e(t)\|^2dt
+w_{\Delta v}\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}\|u(t)\|dt
+w_c t_{\mathrm{solve},k}
+w_r
+P_{\mathrm{escape}}.
$$

### Expected Result

MPC/MPPI-only:

- Replanning every step should track tightly but perform many redundant solves
  and small corrections.
- A short fixed $(B,H)$ should be robust but computationally expensive.
- A long fixed $(B,H)$ should reduce computation but react too late during
  unstable orbital phases or after perturbations.
- A globally tuned compromise should work, but waste computation in benign
  parts of the orbit.

MPC/MPPI+RL:

- Select long coast horizons when unstable-mode error is small or decaying.
- Shorten $H$ before phases with high future error amplification.
- Increase burst duration after a disturbance, then return to long coasts.
- The largest expected gain is fewer replans and less solver time at the same
  containment rate. Propellant improvement may be moderate rather than
  dramatic.

This is the cleanest test of intelligently learned best replanning horizon.

## 2. Stable-Manifold Injection and Ballistic Capture

### Task and Goal

Precompute one stable-manifold branch associated with a planar $L_1$ or $L_2$
Lyapunov orbit. Initialize the spacecraft near, but not exactly on, the branch
to represent injection error.

The spacecraft must use limited thrust to enter the manifold tube, coast along
it, and arrive inside a target tube around the periodic orbit or a prescribed
terminal Poincare section.

Invariant manifolds are established low-energy transport mechanisms; Caltech's
work used them for low-energy lunar capture and halo-orbit correction:
[low-energy lunar transfer](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/yhpx9-xtm07),
[halo-orbit mission correction maneuvers](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/6j8pp-1cr05).

### Constraints

- Bounded low thrust and exact zero-thrust coast.
- Earth/Moon collision avoidance.
- Arrival-time window.
- Terminal position and velocity tolerances.
- Total $\Delta v$ budget.
- Optional corridor around the selected manifold branch.

Treat the Jacobi constant as an observable geometric feature, not a hard
invariant during thrust: thrust changes it.

### MPC/MPPI Loss

$$
J_{\mathrm{inner}}
=
q_W\sum_i d_W(x_i)^2
+q_f d_{\mathcal G}(x_H)^2
+r_u\sum_{i<B}\|u_i\|
+J_{\mathrm{keepout}}.
$$

Here $d_W$ is distance to the selected manifold tube and $d_{\mathcal G}$ is
terminal distance to the target set. Avoid strongly tracking every point of a
nominal trajectory; that would discourage naturally equivalent ballistic
paths.

For a single precomputed corridor, nonlinear MPC is simplest. MPPI becomes
attractive only when multiple manifold branches or arrival phases make the
problem genuinely multimodal.

### RL Reward

$$
c_k=
w_{\Delta v}\Delta v_k
+w_t\Delta t_k
+w_c t_{\mathrm{solve},k}
+w_r
+P_{\mathrm{miss}}
+P_{\mathrm{collision}}.
$$

Useful critic inputs are progress along the transfer, distance and velocity
relative to the manifold, current Jacobi value, target-section distance, and
remaining flight time.

### Expected Result

MPC/MPPI-only:

- A short horizon may not see the benefit of entering the stable manifold and
  can waste thrust correcting immediate geometric error.
- A sufficiently long horizon can find the transfer but becomes expensive if
  repeatedly solved.
- One fixed burst/coast schedule will be sensitive to where injection error
  occurs.

MPC/MPPI+RL:

- Use short replanning intervals during initial manifold injection.
- Select long prediction/execution horizons once the state is captured by the
  natural flow.
- Reawaken near the terminal section for arrival correction.
- Expected improvement: substantially fewer long-horizon optimizations and
  potentially lower $\Delta v$ than a myopic baseline.

This will produce the most visually compelling short-burn, long-coast
demonstration.

## 3. Far Rendezvous to a Safe Hold Point

### Task and Goal

Place a target spacecraft on a planar Lyapunov orbit or distant retrograde
orbit. Initialize the chaser on a nearby periodic orbit or transfer branch.

The goal is not docking initially. Stop at a hold point with

$$
\|\rho-\rho_{\mathrm{HP}}\|\le\rho_{\mathrm{tol}},
\qquad
\|\dot\rho\|\le v_{\mathrm{tol}},
$$

where $\rho=r_{\mathrm{chaser}}-r_{\mathrm{target}}$.

Published CR3BP rendezvous strategies explicitly use natural unstable and
stable manifold motion separated by a small number of maneuvers, followed by
hold points and safety analysis:
[Rendezvous Strategies in the Vicinity of Earth-Moon Lagrangian Points](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2018.00045/full).

### Constraints

- Target keep-out sphere.
- Maximum closing speed.
- Specified approach direction or terminal hold-point geometry.
- Passive safety: zero thrust from every replan must remain collision-free over
  the selected coast.
- Bounded thrust, duty cycle, arrival deadline, and total $\Delta v$.

### Loss and Reward

The inner loss should combine terminal relative state, thrust, and collision
margins:

$$
J_{\mathrm{inner}}
=
q_r\|\rho_H-\rho_{\mathrm{HP}}\|^2
+q_v\|\dot\rho_H\|^2
+r_u\sum_{i<B}\|u_i\|
+J_{\mathrm{keepout}}.
$$

The RL cost uses realized relative error, time, $\Delta v$, solve time, replan
count, and large collision/missed-arrival penalties.

### Expected Result

MPC/MPPI-only should succeed with a sufficiently long and frequently replanned
horizon, but short horizons will make poor orbital-phase decisions.
MPC/MPPI+RL should coast for long intervals while natural relative motion is
useful, then shorten the horizon near intercept and the hold point.

This problem has excellent mission relevance but is a less clean first
benchmark because target phase, terminal timing, and safety constraints all
complicate critic training.

## Experimental Comparison Needed for a Defensible Claim

For each task, compare the same inner solver under:

1. Conventional fixed-horizon MPC/MPPI replanned every control interval.
2. Globally tuned fixed burst/coast $(B,H)$.
3. A simple error-threshold or unstable-mode heuristic scheduler.
4. Exhaustive online $(B,H)$ enumeration, if affordable, as a compute-heavy
   reference.
5. Learned $H$ with fixed $B$.
6. Learned joint $(B,H)$.

Learning only $H$ first is important: otherwise any improvement cannot be
attributed specifically to intelligent replanning. Joint $(B,H)$ should be the
second experiment.

Report:

- Success or containment rate.
- RMS and maximum state error.
- Total $\Delta v$ or integrated thrust.
- Number of replans.
- Total and peak solve time.
- Wall-clock or rollout budget.
- Minimum constraint margin.
- Selected $H$ versus orbital phase and unstable-mode error.

A recent event-triggered halo-orbit NMPC study reports over 50% lower average
computation and over 77% lower triggering frequency than standard NMPC, so the
learned method must beat or clarify its advantage over a tuned trigger, not only
over replanning every step:
[event-triggered halo-orbit MPC](https://journal26.magtechjournal.com/kjkxjs/EN/10.16708/j.cnki.1000-758X.2026.0041).

## Recommendation

Implement planar $L_1$ Lyapunov stationkeeping first, learn $H$ alone, and
judge success primarily by the computation-containment Pareto frontier. Then
use the same infrastructure for stable-manifold capture, where the burst-coast
structure should produce its strongest visual and physical result.
