## I. Introduction — three paragraphs

**I1 — Background and overall goal.** Introduce control that exploits a system's natural motion and intervenes for short periods, with longer intervals of idle actuation as the eventual objective. Position the rotary pendulum as a compact nonlinear, underactuated benchmark for studying that objective. Place future learned prediction horizons and terminal values in this broader program. End by narrowing the present paper to the analytical controller that supplies an interpretable starting point.

**I2 — Physical motivation for the hierarchy.** Explain the coupled difficulty of adding enough mechanical energy, transferring kinetic energy into gravitational potential, and managing arm motion with only one actuator. Establish why total energy alone does not specify pendulum phase, velocity direction, or remaining arm travel. Motivate a division between an energy-only work request and a decoder that observes the physical state. Reserve a few verified literature citations for energy-based swing-up and intermittent control when drafting; make no comparative superiority claim at this planning stage.

**I3 — Current achievement.** Identify the delivered energy encoder, scalar work policy, and finite-horizon torque decoder in one compact contribution paragraph. Anchor the achievement to 4,096 randomized validation captures, including 1,024 downward starts without arm crossings, while retaining the six moving-start crossings. State that the present evidence concerns finite-time angle capture in nominal simulation. This paragraph closes the introduction without adding a separate contribution list.

## II. Problem Formulation — five paragraphs

**P1 — Mechanism, signals, and information.** Introduce the motor-driven horizontal arm and passive pendulum, the two angular coordinates, their signed rates, and scalar shaft torque. Define the four-component physical state, continuous time, decision index, and held-action duration when first used. Declare downward as zero pendulum angle and upright as 180 degrees; retain unwrapped arm angle to distinguish winding. State that the present simulator supplies the full state. A small mechanism panel should fix the axes and positive directions; leave the detailed dynamics to Method A.

**P2 — Upright-holding task and present evaluation endpoint.** Define the ideal target as an upright pendulum at rest with admissible arm position and no arm-centering preference. Describe holding as remaining close to this target over a specified observation interval after reaching it. Separately identify the implemented capture endpoint: five consecutive post-integration samples in the 165–195 degree band, sampled every 20 ms, before a 20 s deadline, with no velocity test. Make clear that the existing trajectories stop at this endpoint and cannot supply post-capture holding measurements.

**P3 — Tentative performance metrics.** Plan definitions for capture probability and time, root-mean-square wrapped upright-angle error, fraction of a common observation interval spent within the upright band, and longest uninterrupted residence there. Report signed or magnitude-based angular-speed summaries alongside angle metrics so a transient passage is distinguishable from slow motion near upright. Define the observation interval and any holding tolerances before future evaluations; keep the existing capture label unchanged when describing current data.

**P4 — Tentative intervention and resource metrics.** Plan definitions for cumulative absolute shaft work, actuation duration, number of controller interventions, and per-decision computation time. Explain that absolute mechanical work accounts for both energy injection and removal, whereas net work can cancel them; neither quantity measures electrical consumption. Distinguish a zero-torque interval from an interval without a controller update. A future burst-coast study can add uninterrupted idle duration once its actuator and update schedule are defined. Keep these metrics individually interpretable before choosing any scalar weighting.

**P5 — Limits and design statement.** Define the usable torque interval and desired arm-angle envelope before stating the control design objective. Treat arm containment as a desired property, assessed by crossing frequency, time beyond the envelope, and peak excursion; identify the current controller's finite arm penalty as a soft treatment of that property. Close with the design task: map observed state to an admissible held torque that promotes upright acquisition and retention with limited intervention. The implementation in Method B is a particular short-horizon policy to evaluate against these broader criteria.

## III. Method — overview and two subsections

**M0 — Method roadmap.** Connect the formulated task to two constructions: a mechanics-derived energy representation, followed by hierarchical work selection and torque decoding. Outline the online sequence from physical-state observation through energy encoding, work request, full-state prediction, action ranking, and held-torque execution. Use the same symbols and physical model throughout both subsections.

### A. Rotary-pendulum dynamics and energy representation — five paragraphs

**A1 — Physical assumptions and Lagrangian.** Introduce the configured masses, link lengths, gravity, and uniform-rod assumptions, with their units and their status as simulation inputs. Derive center-of-mass locations and inertias before constructing total kinetic energy, gravitational potential referenced to downward, and the Lagrangian. Retain the kinetic coupling term and identify zero damping as the condition used in the current controller study. Keep the numerical parameter values in one compact table.

**A2 — Physical-state equations.** Derive the forced Euler–Lagrange equations with motor torque applied only at the arm coordinate. Present the coupled inertia-matrix form and its equivalent four-state differential equation. Explain which terms represent inertial coupling and gravity, and identify the nonlinear flow used for prediction. This paragraph provides the auditable connection between the mechanics and the implemented simulator; it does not introduce a separate approximate dynamics model.

**A3 — Three-component energy representation and target.** Define arm-body kinetic energy, full pendulum-body kinetic energy, and gravitational potential as the three energy coordinates, all in joules. Explain that pendulum-body energy includes motion carried by the arm and the coupling contribution. Define their sum as total mechanical energy and the upright-rest energy target from the same physical parameters. State which information the encoding omits: arm position and physical states with different angle or velocity directions can share an energy representation.

**A4 — Energy dynamics and internal exchange.** Differentiate the energy coordinates along the physical-state dynamics. Organize the result into motor power, arm-to-pendulum power transfer, and conversion between kinetic and gravitational potential energy. Define every power in watts and explain its sign. Show how internal exchange cancels in the total-energy balance. Preserve the dependence on the physical state: these are energy-coordinate dynamics evaluated along the plant, not an autonomous three-state model determined by the energy triple alone.

**A5 — Finite-action work and the slowing rationale.** Integrate the lossless total-power balance over one constant-torque action to relate delivered work to torque and arm displacement. Explain how total energy approaching the upright target while potential approaches the same target leaves vanishing kinetic energy. Use this identity to motivate the control construction, while reserving convergence claims for an analysis not yet established. A zero-torque action preserves total energy in the nominal model but can redistribute its components.

### B. Hierarchical work selection and phase-dependent torque decoding — six paragraphs

**B1 — Energy-only work request.** Define the upper layer's input as the energy triple and its output as bounded, signed requested motor work. Describe proportional feedback from total-energy error and distinguish the tunable feedback gain and request bound from physical constants and the actuator torque limit. Introduce the current values in the implementation table: work gain 0.004 and a request bound equal to 4 percent of upright-rest energy. This layer specifies how much net work is desired over an action, without selecting physical torque.

**B2 — Requested-work surface.** Define the surface using candidate endpoint energy triples whose sum equals current total energy plus requested work. Explain that one scalar equality in three energy coordinates produces a two-dimensional plane, restricted to the physically admissible energy region; its admissible portion can degenerate or be empty. Identify the three axes explicitly as the two body kinetic energies and gravitational potential. Torque is not an additional axis of this surface. The plane expresses an energy budget, not reachability from a particular physical phase.

**B3 — Torque-indexed response curve.** Fix the observed physical state and the 100 ms action duration, vary the single held torque over its usable interval, and map each predicted physical endpoint into the three-energy space. This constructs a one-parameter curve whose shape depends on physical phase. Explain that retaining physical phase is the decoder's role. Distinguish the finite-action curve from the instantaneous energy-rate response; zero instantaneous motor power at rest does not imply zero work over an action that accelerates the arm.

**B4 — Intersections and numerical candidate construction.** Interpret intersections of the curve and requested-work surface as torques that realize the requested work in the exact lossless model. Allow multiple intersections, no intersection, or degenerate cases. Describe how the implementation samples 33 torques and refines sign-changing work-error brackets with ten bisection iterations, producing up to 32 additional candidates. Refer to these as resolved work-matching candidates: a finite grid and bracket search do not establish that every possible intersection has been found. Account for numerical integration error when connecting the exact geometric identity to predicted endpoints.

**B5 — Constraint treatment and final selection.** Present the actual selection objective over all valid tested torques: promote endpoint potential, penalize mismatch with requested work, and penalize sampled arm-limit overshoot. Define each normalization and identify the penalty weights as design choices. Make the implementation order precise: roots enrich the candidate set, then roots and grid torques are scored together; the code does not first commit to an intersection and then project it into a hard feasible set. A selected point can depart from the requested-work plane or cross the soft arm boundary. The usable torque cap is enforced directly, and arm containment is assessed separately.

**B6 — Closed-loop execution.** Close the subsection by holding the chosen torque for 100 ms, observing the resulting state, re-encoding its energy, and repeating, subject to the experiment's early termination. Identify the five 20 ms prediction steps and the sampled nature of the arm check. Keep tie-breaking and numerical-validity details in a compact algorithm or implementation table. Identify fixed-duration action selection as the current operating schedule; later learned durations and terminal values require their own formulation and evaluation.
