# Condensed RL–MPC Training Plan

This version keeps the original two-layer design: MPC remains the torque optimizer, while RL selects high-level timing parameters outside the MPC problem. It also keeps the offline-warm-start plus online-simulation-training workflow from the earlier plan.  

---

## 1. Controller structure

The physical controller remains:

$$
s_k
\rightarrow
a_k
\rightarrow
\mathrm{MPC}(s_k,a_k)
\rightarrow
u_k
\rightarrow
x_{k+1}
$$

The MPC is already working with the energy term and the action-change penalty, so freeze the inner MPC objective as:

$$
J_{\mathrm{MPC}} =
\sum_{i=0}^{H_k-1}
\left[
q_E e_E(x_{k+i})^2
+
q_{\Delta u}\bar{\Delta u}_{k+i}^2
\right]
$$

The RL layer does **not** learn torque and does **not** modify the QP objective. It only selects timing parameters for the MPC wrapper.

Note that this $k$ value stands for replanning step -- each step is the entire burst-coast action sequence, and the observed state is at the replanning time, after the entire control sequence and passive rollout from the last observation.

---

## 2. Observation

Use the reduced raw physical state:

$$
s_k =
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin\theta_k \\
\cos\theta_k \\
\omega_k\\
\sum |\frac{u}{u_{max}}|
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\sum_k u$ stands for normalized accumulated action magnitude used so far. No energy error, previous horizon, or tracking deviation is included in the first RL observation.

---

## 3. Action definition

The RL action is two-dimensional:

$$
a_k =
\begin{bmatrix}
\bar B_k \\
\bar H_k
\end{bmatrix}
$$

with

$$
\bar H_k=\frac{H_k}{H_{\max}}, \qquad H_{\max}=T_{\mathrm{natural}}
$$

and

$$
\bar B_k=\frac{B_k}{B_{\max}}, \qquad B_{\max}=0.5H_k
$$

Thus:

$$
H_k = \bar H_k H_{\max}
$$

$$
B_k = \bar B_k B_{\max} = \bar B_k(0.5H_k)
$$

The action space is implemented as a discrete two-dimensional grid:

$$
\mathcal A = \left\{ (\bar B_i, \bar H_j) \right\}_{i=1,j=1}^{N_B,N_H}
$$

The actor does not output a continuous action directly. It selects from this sampled action matrix.

---

## 4. One-network Q-learning design

Use only one learned neural network: the Q-cost critic.

$$
Q_\psi(s_k, \bar B_k, \bar H_k)
$$

or equivalently, for efficient implementation:

$$
Q_\psi(s_k) \in \mathbb R^{N_B \times N_H}
$$

where each matrix entry is the predicted closed-loop cost for one candidate pair:

$$
(\bar B_i, \bar H_j)
$$

There is no separate actor network. The “actor” is a rule applied to the Q matrix.

### Deployment actor

At deployment:

$$
a_k^\star = \arg\min_{a\in\mathcal A} Q_\psi(s_k, a)
$$

Equivalently:

$$
(\bar B_k^\star, \bar H_k^\star) = \arg\min_{i,j} Q_\psi(s_k)_{i,j}
$$

This is deterministic 2D matrix sampling over the action grid.

### Training actor / exploration

During training, use weighted stochastic exploration over the same Q matrix:

$$
p(a\mid s_k) \propto \exp\left(
-\frac{Q_\psi(s_k,a)}{\tau}
\right)
$$

with optional uniform mixing:

$$
p_{\mathrm{train}}(a\mid s_k) =
(1-\epsilon)
\frac{\exp(-Q_\psi(s_k,a)/\tau)}
{\sum_{a'\in\mathcal A}\exp(-Q_\psi(s_k,a')/\tau)}
+
\epsilon\frac{1}{|\mathcal A|}
$$

Then sample:

$$
a_k \sim p_{\mathrm{train}}(\cdot \mid s_k)
$$

This preserves exploration without introducing a second neural network.

---

## 5. Q value / closed-loop cost

The Q value represents the expected closed-loop cost-to-go:

$$
Q_\psi(s_k, a_k)
\approx
\mathbb E
\left[
G_k
\mid
s_k,a_k
\right]
$$

where the return contains exactly four cost components:

$$
G_k = \sum_{i=k}^{T}
\gamma^{i-k}
\left[
w_t G_{\mathrm{time},i}
+
w_u G_{\mathrm{act},i}
+
w_c G_{\mathrm{cmp},i}
\right]
+
G_{\mathrm{term}}
$$

with:

### 1. Time-used cost

Physical task time, non-negative and linear:

$$
G_{\mathrm{time},i} = \Delta t
$$

This encourages faster swing-up and stabilization.

### 2. Accumulated-action-used cost

Use action squared effort:

$$
G_{\mathrm{act},i} = ( \frac{u_i}{u_{\max}})^2
$$

Over a trajectory, this produces:

$$
\sum_{i=k}^{T} ( \frac{u_i}{u_{\max}})^2
$$

### 3. Computation-time cost

Use non-negative linear computation time:

$$
G_{\mathrm{cmp},i} = \frac{t_{\mathrm{solve},i}}{T_s}
$$

For offline training, use the logged MPC solve time:

$$
t_{\mathrm{solve},i} = t_{\mathrm{logged},i}
$$

For online estimation, use a fitted variable-power model:

$$
\hat t_{\mathrm{solve}}(H,B) = f_{\mathrm{pow}}(H,B)
$$

for example:

$$
\hat t_{\mathrm{solve}}(H,B) =
\alpha_0
+
\alpha_H H^{p_H}
+
\alpha_B B^{p_B}
% +
% \alpha_{HB}(HB)^{p_{HB}}
$$

with the final fitted form selected from solve-time logs.

### 4. Goal/fail terminal penalty

Use a positive terminal constant:

$$
G_{\mathrm{term}} = P_{\mathrm{fail}}
$$

A practical implementation is:

$$
G_{\mathrm{term}} =
% P_{\mathrm{goal}}\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{goal}}
% +
P_{\mathrm{fail}}\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{fail}}
$$

with:

$$
0 < P_{\mathrm{fail}}
$$

---

## 6. Critic training target-

Store transitions as:

$$
(s_k, a_k, c_k, s_{k+1}, d_k,  G_{\mathrm{time},k},G_{\mathrm{act},k}, G_{\mathrm{cmp},k},G_{\mathrm{term},k})

$$

where:

$$
a_k=(\bar B_k, \bar H_k)
$$

For Q-learning, use the target:

$$
y_k =
c_k
+
\gamma (1-d_k)
\min_{a'\in\mathcal A}
\bar Q_\psi(s_{k+1},a')
$$

and minimize:

$$
\mathcal L_Q(\psi) =
\mathbb E
\left[
\left(
Q_\psi(s_k, a_k) - y_k
\right)^2
\right]
$$

A frozen target copy ($\bar Q_\psi$) may be used for stability, but it is not a separate actor.

For offline Monte Carlo pretraining, compute full returns backward:

$$
G_T = c_T
$$

$$
G_k = c_k + \gamma G_{k+1}
$$

and fit:

$$
Q_\psi(s_k, a_k)\approx G_k
$$

The earlier training plan already supports Monte Carlo return fitting and later TD or (n)-step bootstrapping. 

---

## 7. Training loop

At each decision step:

$$
s_k
\rightarrow
Q_\psi(s_k)
\rightarrow
a_k=(\bar B_k,\bar H_k)
\rightarrow
(H_k,B_k)
\rightarrow
\mathrm{MPC}
\rightarrow
u_k
\rightarrow
s_{k+1}
$$

Then log:

$$
(s_k,a_k,H_k,B_k,u_k,t_{\mathrm{solve},k},c_k,s_{k+1},d_k)
$$

The RL update happens outside the MPC solve. The MPC remains a standalone controller called by the RL wrapper.

---

## 8. Development phases

### Phase 1 — Generate Monte Carlo data for offline training

Run the standalone MPC simulation under sampled or scripted $$(\bar B,\bar H)$$ policies.

Log full trajectories, including:

$$
s_k,\, a_k,\, H_k,\, B_k,\, u_k,\, t_{\mathrm{solve},k},\, c_k,\, s_{k+1},\, d_k
$$

At episode end, compute Monte Carlo returns:

$$
G_k = \sum_{i=k}^{T} \gamma^{i-k}c_i
$$

This creates the initial offline dataset:

$$
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{MC}} = \left\{(s_k, a_k, G_k)\right\}
$$

### Phase 2 — RL model design

Define:

$$
s_k = [\sin\theta_k, \cos\theta_k, \omega_k]^T
$$

$$
a_k = (\bar B_k, \bar H_k)
$$

$$
Q_\psi(s_k)\in \mathbb R^{N_B\times N_H}
$$

Choose the action grid resolution:

$$
N_B \times N_H
$$

and define the exploration distribution over this grid.

### Phase 3 — Offline training and computation-time fitting

Train the critic using Monte Carlo targets:

$$
Q_\psi(s_k, a_k) \leftarrow G_k
$$

Fit the computation-time model:

$$
\hat t_{\mathrm{solve}}(H,B)
$$

using the logged MPC solve times.

### Phase 4 — Online interactive handle

Develop an interface that lets the RL module call the already standalone MPC simulation:

$$
Q_\psi(s_k)
\rightarrow
(\bar B_k,\bar H_k)
\rightarrow
\mathrm{MPC}(H_k,B_k)
\rightarrow
u_k
\rightarrow
\mathrm{simulation}
$$

The MPC simulation should remain independently runnable without RL.

### Phase 5 — Online training with surveillance snapshots

Run online simulated training using weighted stochastic exploration.

Store full surveillance snapshots:

$$
x_k,\, s_k,\, Q_\psi(s_k),\, a_k,\, H_k,\, B_k,\, u_k,\, t_{\mathrm{solve},k},\, c_k,\, s_{k+1},\, d_k
$$

Update the critic from replay using TD, (n)-step TD, or mixed Monte Carlo/TD targets.

---

## 9. Final design decisions

The current implementation target is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{MPC solves torque; Q-learning selects }(\bar B,\bar H).
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Only one learned network is used: }Q_\psi.
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Deployment uses } \arg\min \text{ over the 2D action matrix.}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Training uses weighted stochastic exploration over the same matrix.}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
J_{\mathrm{MPC}}
=
\sum
q_E e_E^2
+
q_{\Delta u}\bar{\Delta u}^2
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
Q =
\text{time-used}
+
\text{accumulated action magnitude}
+
\text{computation time}
+
\text{goal/fail terminal penalty}
}
$$
