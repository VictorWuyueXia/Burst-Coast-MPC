# Training Program: How MPC and Actor Critic Work Together

### 1. Training Interpretation

The reinforcement learning layer should not replace the MPC. The MPC remains the low level optimizer that computes the actual torque sequence. The actor critic layer learns a higher level timing policy that selects the MPC prediction horizon:

$$
a_k = H_k
$$

where

$$
H_k \in \{1,2,\dots,H_{\max}\}
$$

At this stage, the burst sleep split ratio remains fixed:

$$
\lambda=\lambda_0
$$

so that

$$
B_k=\lceil \lambda_0 H_k\rceil
$$

$$
S_k=H_k-B_k
$$

The MPC receives $H_k$, builds the corresponding burst sleep optimization problem, solves the QP, and applies the first torque action:

$$
u_k = u_{k|k}^{\star}
$$

The RL layer observes the closed loop consequence of choosing $H_k$, then updates its actor and critic networks from the resulting transition.

The training loop is therefore:

$$
\text{RL chooses horizon}
\rightarrow
\text{MPC solves torque plan}
\rightarrow
\text{plant evolves}
\rightarrow
\text{closed loop cost is logged}
\rightarrow
\text{RL updates timing policy}
$$

The RL action is not torque. The RL action is the prediction horizon.

---

### 2. Offline First, Then Interactive Online Training

The recommended training program should use two phases.

First, train offline from logged MPC rollouts. This is the safer phase. It verifies the observation, reward, critic target, and horizon action interface without allowing an immature neural policy to destabilize the controller.

Second, use online interactive training in simulation. In this phase, the actor selects horizons during closed loop execution, the MPC executes those horizons, and the networks update from newly collected experience.

The physical interpretation is:

$$
\text{Offline training}
\\
=======================
\\
\text{learn from known MPC behavior}
$$

$$
\text{Online simulation training}
\\
=================================
\\
\text{improve the timing policy through interaction}
$$

For the current project stage, the first complete implementation should support both modes, but the first experiments should start with offline pretraining followed by online fine tuning in simulation.

No hardware or high consequence deployment should use online learning directly. A trained policy should be frozen during evaluation.

---

### 3. Should the Q Value Be Used as MPC Terminal Cost?

At this stage, no. The $Q$ value should not be inserted as a terminal cost into the MPC.

The actor critic is currently selecting the prediction horizon:

$$
H_k = \pi_\phi(s_k)
$$

The MPC then solves its own open loop QP using the existing MPC cost:

$$
J_{\mathrm{MPC}}
=
\sum_{i=0}^{H_k-1}
\ell_{\mathrm{MPC}}(x_{k+i},u_{k+i},u_{k+i-1})
$$

The critic estimates the closed loop value of choosing a horizon:

$$
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)
$$

or equivalently

$$
Q_\psi(s_k,H_k)
$$

if reward maximization notation is used.

This critic should be used for training the actor and for selecting the horizon, not for shaping the internal MPC trajectory cost.

In other words:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Use the critic to choose } H_k.
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Do not yet use the critic as MPC terminal cost.}
}
$$

The reason is structural. The current MPC is a quadratic program. A neural terminal value generally makes the MPC objective nonlinear unless it is approximated or constrained to a QP compatible form. Also, using the same critic both to choose horizon and to reshape the MPC plan creates a coupled learning problem that is harder to diagnose.

A later research extension may add a learned terminal value to the MPC, but only after horizon selection is stable. That would be a separate stage.

---

### 4. Network Update Timing

The MPC should run at every control decision point that requires a new plan. The RL networks do not need to update at every physical simulation step.

Use this update structure:

1. During rollout, the actor selects a horizon $H_k$.

2. The MPC solves the QP using that horizon.

3. The plant executes the first control action, or executes the planned burst sleep segment depending on the current wrapper design.

4. The transition is stored:

$$
(s_k,H_k,c_k^{\mathrm{AC}},s_{k+1},d_k)
$$

5. The actor and critic update after enough transitions have accumulated.

A practical rule is:

$$
\text{collect } N_{\mathrm{warmup}} \text{ transitions before any neural update}
$$

then update every:

$$
N_{\mathrm{update\_interval}}
$$

environment steps, or after each episode.

For this project, episode level or mini batch level updates are cleaner than updating after every single step. Updating every step may make the controller behavior change too quickly while the MPC is already adapting its horizon.

Recommended first version:

$$
\text{collect one full episode}
\rightarrow
\text{append transitions to replay buffer}
\rightarrow
\text{run } N_{\mathrm{grad}} \text{ gradient updates}
\rightarrow
\text{start next episode}
$$

This makes debugging easier because each episode is generated by a fixed actor snapshot.

### 5. Training Workflow

#### Step 1: Freeze the current MPC formulation

Keep the current working MPC cost fixed:

$$
\ell_{\mathrm{MPC}}
=
q_E e_E^2
+
q_{\Delta u} \bar{\Delta u}^2
$$

Do not tune the MPC weights while introducing the RL layer. The goal is to isolate whether adaptive horizon selection improves the closed loop behavior.

---

#### Step 2: Define the horizon action set

Choose a discrete action set:

$$
\mathcal{A}_H=\{H_1,H_2,\dots,H_M\}
$$

where the largest horizon is approximately one natural period:

$$
H_M \approx H_{\mathrm{period}}
$$

The actor outputs a categorical distribution:

$$
\pi_\phi(H\mid s)
$$

During training, sample:

$$
H_k \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot\mid s_k)
$$

During deterministic evaluation, select:

$$
H_k^\star=\arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

or equivalently:

$$
H_k^\star=\arg\max_H Q_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

depending on whether cost or reward notation is used.

---

#### Step 3: Build the MPC wrapper

The MPC wrapper should accept the selected horizon:

$$
H_k
$$

and fixed split ratio:

$$
\lambda_0
$$

Then compute:

$$
B_k=\lceil \lambda_0 H_k\rceil
$$

$$
S_k=H_k-B_k
$$

The wrapper solves the QP over the burst sleep structure and applies the first control action or scheduled segment action according to the current controller implementation.

The wrapper should return:

$$
u_k
$$

$$
t_{\mathrm{solve},k}
$$

$$
B_k
$$

$$
S_k
$$

$$
\text{replan status}
$$

$$
\text{predicted trajectory if available}
$$

---

#### Step 4: Define the actor critic observation

Use the reduced first version:

$$
s_k=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin\theta_k\\
\cos\theta_k\\
\omega_k/\omega_s\\
e_E(x_k)\\
u_{k-1}/u_{\max}\\
H_{k-1}/H_{\max}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Once predicted trajectory logging is available, extend it to:

$$
s_k=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin\theta_k\\
\cos\theta_k\\
\omega_k/\omega_s\\
e_E(x_k)\\
u_{k-1}/u_{\max}\\
H_{k-1}/H_{\max}\\
B_{k-1}/B_{\max}\\
S_{\mathrm{remain},k}/S_{\max}\\
\epsilon_{\mathrm{track},k}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

where

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{track},k}
=
\frac{|x_k-\hat x_k^{\mathrm{plan}}|_Q}
{1+|\hat x_k^{\mathrm{plan}}|_Q}
$$

---

#### Step 5: Define the outer closed loop cost

Use the actor critic cost:

$$
c_k^{\mathrm{AC}}
=
w_E^{\mathrm{out}}e_E^2
+
w_x^{\mathrm{out}}g_E
\left[
\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta_s}\right)^2
+
\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_s}\right)^2
\right]
+
w_{\mathrm{act}}\bar u^2
+
w_{\Delta u}^{\mathrm{out}}\bar{\Delta u}^2
+
w_{\mathrm{rep}}\mathbf{1}_{{\mathrm{early\_replan}}}
+
w_{{\mathrm{cmp}}}\frac{t_{\mathrm{solve}}}{T_s}
+
w_{\mathrm{fail}}c_{\mathrm{fail}}
$$

with

$$
g_E=\exp\left(-\frac{e_E^2}{\sigma_E^2}\right)
$$

This cost should be computed from real closed loop execution, not from predicted MPC states.

The reward is:

$$
r_k=-c_k^{\mathrm{AC}}
$$

---

#### Step 6: Collect offline baseline data

Run baseline controllers before training the actor online.

The baseline set should include:

1. Fixed short horizon.

2. Fixed medium horizon.

3. Fixed natural period horizon.

4. Existing hardcoded event triggered controller.

5. Optional simple heuristic horizon policy based on energy error.

For each rollout, log:

$$
(s_k,H_k,c_k^{\mathrm{AC}},s_{k+1},d_k)
$$

This produces the initial replay buffer:

$$
\mathcal{D}_0
$$

This stage should also log solve time:

$$
t_{\mathrm{solve},k}
$$

so the compute cost model can be fitted.

---

#### Step 7: Offline critic pretraining

Pretrain the critic using the logged dataset:

$$
C_\psi(s,H)
$$

Use an $n$ step target:

$$
y_k^{(n)}
=
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
\gamma^i c_{k+i}^{\mathrm{AC}}
+
\gamma^n
\sum_{H'}
\pi_{\bar\phi}(H'\mid s_{k+n})
C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+n},H')
$$

For the earliest offline stage, if no meaningful actor exists yet, use a fitted value target from empirical returns:

$$
y_k
=
\sum_{i=k}^{T}
\gamma^{i-k}c_i^{\mathrm{AC}}
$$

Then train:

$$
\mathcal{L}_C(\psi)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left(
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)-y_k
\right)^2
\right]
$$

This gives the critic an initial estimate of which horizons work better in which states.

---

#### Step 8: Offline actor initialization

Initialize the actor using the pretrained critic.

For each state $s_k$, compute:

$$
H_k^{\mathrm{best}}
=
\arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

Then train the actor by behavior style supervision toward the critic preferred horizon:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{init}}(\phi)
=
-\mathbb{E}
\left[
\log \pi_\phi(H_k^{\mathrm{best}}\mid s_k)
\right]
$$

This is not the final actor training objective. It is only a stable initialization so the actor does not begin online learning from a random horizon selection policy.

---

#### Step 9: Online simulation rollout

Run closed loop simulation episodes.

At each decision step:

1. Observe $s_k$.

2. Sample horizon:

$$
H_k\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid s_k)
$$

3. Pass $H_k$ to the MPC wrapper.

4. MPC computes $(B_k,S_k)$, solves the QP, and returns $u_k$.

5. Apply $u_k$ to the plant.

6. Compute the realized outer cost:

$$
c_k^{\mathrm{AC}}
$$

7. Observe $s_{k+1}$.

8. Store transition:

$$
(s_k,H_k,c_k^{\mathrm{AC}},s_{k+1},d_k)
$$

The neural network weights should remain fixed during this episode in the first implementation. Update them after the episode ends.

---

#### Step 10: Online critic update

After each episode, sample mini batches from the replay buffer:

$$
\mathcal{D}
$$

Compute the TD target:

$$
y_k
=
c_k^{\mathrm{AC}}
+
\gamma
\sum_{H'}
\pi_{\bar\phi}(H'\mid s_{k+1})
C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+1},H')
$$

or the ($n$) step target:

$$
y_k^{(n)}
=
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
\gamma^i c_{k+i}^{\mathrm{AC}}
+
\gamma^n
\sum_{H'}
\pi_{\bar\phi}(H'\mid s_{k+n})
C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+n},H')
$$

Then minimize:

$$
\mathcal{L}_C(\psi)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left(
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)-y_k
\right)^2
\right]
$$

Use a slowly updated target critic:

$$
\bar\psi \leftarrow \tau\psi+(1-\tau)\bar\psi
$$

where $\tau$ is a small update coefficient.

---

#### Step 11: Online actor update

Update the actor after the critic update.

Use the cost based actor objective:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\pi}(\phi)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\sum_H
\pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)C_\psi(s_k,H)
-
\beta\mathcal{H}(\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid s_k))
\right]
$$

The first term pushes probability toward lower cost horizons. The entropy term prevents premature collapse into one horizon before the critic is reliable.

Update the target actor slowly:

$$
\bar\phi \leftarrow \tau\phi+(1-\tau)\bar\phi
$$

During evaluation, remove sampling and choose:

$$
H_k^\star=\arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

or use:

$$
H_k^\star=\arg\max_H \pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)
$$

The critic based selection is more directly tied to the cost objective.

---

#### Step 12: Freeze policy for evaluation

Evaluation should use a frozen actor and critic. No weights should update during evaluation episodes.

Compare against fixed horizon and heuristic baselines using full episode metrics:

$$
J_{\mathrm{eval}}
=
W_{\mathrm{succ}}J_{\mathrm{succ}}
+
W_{\mathrm{time}}J_{\mathrm{time}}
+
W_{\mathrm{act}}J_{\mathrm{act}}
+
W_{\mathrm{cmp}}J_{\mathrm{cmp}}
+
W_{\mathrm{rep}}J_{\mathrm{rep}}
$$

The learned policy should only be considered useful if it improves closed loop performance relative to simple baselines.

---

### 6. Recommended Training Schedule

Use the following development schedule.

#### Training Schedule A: Safe First Version

1. Freeze MPC cost.

2. Implement horizon input interface.

3. Run fixed horizon rollouts.

4. Build replay buffer.

5. Pretrain critic from empirical returns.

6. Initialize actor from critic preferred actions.

7. Run online simulation episodes with fixed network weights per episode.

8. Update critic and actor after each episode.

9. Evaluate frozen policy every fixed number of episodes.

10. Save the best policy by closed loop evaluation metric, not by training loss.

This is the recommended first implementation.

#### Training Schedule B: More Interactive Version

After Schedule A works, allow network updates every fixed number of environment steps:

$$
N_{\mathrm{update\_interval}}
$$

For example:

$$
N_{\mathrm{update\_interval}}=20
$$

The workflow becomes:

$$
\text{collect 20 transitions}
\rightarrow
\text{run gradient updates}
\rightarrow
\text{continue rollout}
$$

This is more sample efficient but harder to debug because the policy changes within an episode.

Do not use this version until the episode based version is stable.

---

### 7. Expected Behavior During Training

Early in training, the actor may over select long horizons because longer horizons often improve control behavior. This is expected unless compute cost is included.

Once the compute term is active:

$$
w_{\mathrm{cmp}}\frac{t_{\mathrm{solve}}}{T_s}
$$

the actor should learn to use long horizons only when they improve closed loop behavior enough to justify the solve time.

Expected learned structure:

1. Short horizon when the energy pumping direction is obvious.

2. Longer horizon near phase sensitive states.

3. Shorter horizon when the pendulum is already in a good local stabilization basin.

4. Longer horizon when bad timing would lose an energy pumping opportunity.

5. Fewer unnecessary early replans once replanning cost is active.

If the actor always selects the longest horizon, the compute cost is too weak or the action set is too narrow.

If the actor always selects the shortest horizon, the compute cost is too strong or the critic is not seeing the delayed benefit of longer horizons.

If the actor switches horizons every step without clear benefit, add entropy annealing, action smoothing penalty, or a horizon change penalty:

$$
c_{\Delta H,k}
=
\left(
\frac{H_k-H_{k-1}}{H_{\max}}
\right)^2
$$

This term belongs in the actor critic objective, not in the MPC cost.

---

### 8. What Not To Do at This Stage

Do not train the RL policy to output torque.

Do not insert the neural critic as an MPC terminal cost yet.

Do not let the actor choose both $H_k$ and $\lambda_k$ in the first version.

Do not tune MPC weights and RL reward weights simultaneously.

Do not update neural network weights during evaluation.

Do not evaluate success only by critic loss.

Do not deploy an online updating policy outside simulation.

The current stage should remain focused on one question:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Can a learned timing policy choose better MPC horizons than fixed or hardcoded timing rules?}
}
$$

Only after this is demonstrated should the project move to adaptive burst sleep split selection or learned terminal value shaping inside MPC.
