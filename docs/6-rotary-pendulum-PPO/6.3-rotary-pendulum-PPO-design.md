# Rotary-Pendulum PPO Actor-Critic Design

## 1. Decision and scope

The first reinforcement-learning proof of concept should be a direct-torque PPO
controller around the existing deterministic rotary-pendulum plant. It should not
put the nonlinear MPC solver inside the training loop and should not initially ask
PPO to choose a prediction horizon.

This choice has three useful consequences:

1. the already-vectorized nonlinear dynamics can train many environments in parallel;
2. the actor supplies a strong learned control baseline without replacing the MPC runtime;
3. the state-value critic can later become the learned MPC terminal value and can score
   candidate coast or replanning horizons.

The implementation is a theory-oriented simulation benchmark, not a hardware-ready
controller. The plant remains deterministic, torque driven, and bounded by the
configured rated torque. No noise, model mismatch, or domain randomization belongs in
the first result.

## 2. Existing physical contract

At simulator step $k$, the state is

$$
x_k=[\theta_k,\alpha_k,\omega_k,\nu_k]^T,
$$

where $\theta$ is the unwrapped rotary-arm angle, $\alpha=0$ is pendulum downward,
$\omega=\dot\theta$, and $\nu=\dot\alpha$. The existing RK4 map advances this state
by $T_s=0.002\,\mathrm{s}$ under shaft torque $\tau$ with

$$
|\tau|\leq\tau_{\max}=0.0204\,\mathrm{N\,m}.
$$

The derived natural frequency, period, upright-energy speed, and arm speed scale are

$$
\omega_n=12.983874\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad T_n=0.483922\,\mathrm{s},
$$

$$
\nu_E=2\sqrt{G/J_p}=21.360690\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad
\omega_E=(\pi/2)\omega_n=20.395022\,\mathrm{rad/s}.
$$

$G=m_pgl$ is the configured gravity-torque scale and $J_p=m_pL_p^2/3$ is the
pendulum pivot inertia. These values are derived from `physics.yaml`; they are not
new tuning parameters.

## 3. RL clock and action

One PPO decision holds a constant action through

$$
m=5
$$

existing RK4 steps. Thus the RL interval is

$$
T_{\rm RL}=mT_s=0.010\,\mathrm{s},
$$

or $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Five internal steps retain the validated 500 Hz physics while
removing nearly duplicate decisions from PPO.

The actor samples an unconstrained scalar latent action

$$
y_k\sim\mathcal N(\mu_\psi(z_k),\sigma_\psi^2),
$$

then applies

$$
a_k=\tanh(y_k),\qquad \tau_k=\tau_{\max}a_k.
$$

$a_k\in(-1,1)$ is normalized torque, $\mu_\psi$ is the actor output, and
$\log\sigma_\psi$ is one learned state-independent scalar initialized to $-0.5$.
PPO must use the exact tanh-Jacobian correction in the action log probability. The
environment should not clip $a_k$ after sampling; the tanh map enforces the bound by
construction.

Direct torque is deliberately the first action. A horizon action would make the
problem a variable-duration semi-Markov process and would require repeated nonlinear
MPC solves during training. That is a later experiment after the critic is useful.

## 4. Fixed physics state encoder

The neural network should receive eight dimensionless features:

$$
z(x_k,a_{k-1})=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta_k/\theta_{\rm lim}\\
\sin\alpha_k\\
\cos\alpha_k\\
\omega_k/\omega_E\\
\nu_k/\nu_E\\
\tanh(e_E(x_k))\\
\chi_\phi(x_k)\\
a_{k-1}
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad \theta_{\rm lim}=\pi/2.
$$

$\theta/\theta_{\rm lim}$ preserves cable winding and distance to the arm limit.
The sine-cosine pair represents pendulum orientation continuously across angle wraps.
The previous normalized torque makes the state Markov when torque-change cost is used.

The pendulum-relative swing energy, its target, and normalized error are

$$
E_s(x)=\frac12J_p\nu^2+G(1-\cos\alpha),\qquad
E_s^\star=2G,\qquad
e_E(x)=\frac{E_s(x)-E_s^\star}{E_s^\star}.
$$

The bounded energy feature exposes whether energy must be injected or removed without
allowing a rare high-speed sample to dominate the network input.

Use the existing smooth phase-origin convention

$$
\widetilde\nu=\nu+0.01\nu_E
\exp\!\left[-\left(\frac{E_s/E_s^\star}{0.05}\right)^2\right],
$$

$$
\chi_\phi(x)=
\frac{\cos\alpha\,\widetilde\nu}
{\sqrt{(\cos\alpha\,\widetilde\nu)^2+(0.01\nu_E)^2}}.
$$

$\chi_\phi\in[-1,1]$ supplies the sign of the dominant energy-transfer channel and
chooses one reproducible first-swing direction at exact downward rest. It is derived,
not observed independently and not tuned by the network.

Do not use running observation normalization in the first implementation. Every scale
above has a physical definition, so training and deployment receive the same encoding.

## 5. One small network with two decoders

Use one shared actor-critic module:

```text
physics features z[8]
        |
Linear(8, 64) -> Tanh -> Linear(64, 64) -> Tanh
        |                                      |
actor decoder                             critic decoder
Linear(64, 32) -> Tanh                   Linear(64, 32) -> Tanh
Linear(32, 1) -> mean mu                 Linear(32, 1) -> value V
        |
learned global log_std -> tanh Gaussian
```

This network has $8{,}963$ trainable parameters including `log_std`. Tanh is suitable
because every encoded feature is order one, the policy mean must change smoothly with
phase, and the critic will later be straightforward to reproduce symbolically inside
MPC. Use orthogonal weight initialization, zero biases, actor output gain $0.01$, and
critic output gain $1.0$.

One shared encoder is the minimum competent structure. Do not add recurrence,
attention, convolution, separate energy/phase branches, twin critics, or learned
dynamics. If diagnostics later show persistent actor-value gradient interference,
the only planned escalation is two independent copies of the same $8\rightarrow64
\rightarrow64$ encoder; widths should not grow first.

## 6. Energy-phase reward

### 6.1 Bounded task potential

Define wrapped upright error

$$
\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\alpha-\pi),\cos(\alpha-\pi)),
$$

the local state error

$$
q_L(x)=
\left(\frac{\beta}{\pi}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_E}\right)^2+
0.1\left[
\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta_{\rm lim}}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_E}\right)^2
\right],
$$

and the energy-shell gate

$$
g_L(x)=\exp[-(e_E(x)/0.25)^2].
$$

The dimensionless task potential is

$$
\Phi(x)=
2\frac{e_E(x)^2}{1+e_E(x)^2}
+g_L(x)\frac{q_L(x)}{1+q_L(x)}.
$$

Lower $\Phi$ is better and $\Phi=0$ only at the complete upright-rest target.
The energy gate prevents the local upright and arm-rest terms from fighting the
necessary early swing-up motion. The endpoint progress reward is

$$
r_{\rm progress,k}=\Phi(x_k)-\Phi(x_{k+1}).
$$

Unlike a sum of squared low-energy errors, this term rewards improvement and does not
charge the agent merely for beginning far below upright energy. Its undiscounted sum
telescopes, so changing the action-repeat segmentation cannot create reward by itself.

### 6.2 Phase quality

At each of the five internal physics states, define

$$
r_E(x)=-\tanh(e_E(x)/0.25),
$$

$$
a_\tau(\alpha)=
\frac{J_p\tau_{\max}}
{J_p(J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha)-C^2\cos^2\alpha},
$$

$$
e_\phi(x,\tau)=\frac{\dot\omega(x,\tau)}{a_\tau(\alpha)}
+r_E(x)\chi_\phi(x).
$$

$r_E>0$ requests energy injection and $r_E<0$ requests removal. $a_\tau$ is the
torque-induced arm-acceleration authority computed from the physical model, while
$\dot\omega$ is the actual coupled arm acceleration. Minimizing $e_\phi^2$ requests
the arm acceleration sign that makes the dominant transfer power
$-C\cos\alpha\,\nu\dot\omega$ correct.

Use the bounded phase cost

$$
c_{\phi,k}=\frac1m\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}
r_E(x_{k,i})^2\cos^2\alpha_{k,i}
\frac{e_\phi(x_{k,i},\tau_k)^2}{1+e_\phi(x_{k,i},\tau_k)^2}.
$$

It is zero on the desired phase-acceleration relation, switches off on the target
energy shell and at horizontal decoupling, and remains bounded during early exploration.

### 6.3 Complete decision reward

Let $a_{k-1}$ be the previous normalized torque. Before terminal bonuses, use

$$
\boxed{
r_k=r_{\rm progress,k}
-0.05c_{\phi,k}
-0.002(a_k-a_{k-1})^2
-0.0005a_k^2
-0.02(\theta_{k+1}/\theta_{\rm lim})^8.
}
$$

The last three terms weakly discourage chatter, unnecessary effort, and operation near
the cable boundary. They are tie-breakers rather than reasons to avoid swing-up.
Add $+5$ once on held goal success, $-5$ on $|\theta|\geq\theta_{\rm lim}$, and $-2$
at the 10 s timeout. Goal tolerances and the five-physics-step hold test remain the
existing mission contract. Also report a stricter nonterminal 0.25 s dwell diagnostic
after training; do not silently redefine project success.

## 7. PPO update

Use physical-time discounting

$$
\gamma=\exp(-T_{\rm RL}/5\,\mathrm{s})=0.998002,
$$

where the fixed $5\,\mathrm{s}$ time constant determines how quickly delayed reward is
discounted. With termination indicator $d_k$, temporal-difference residual and GAE are

$$
\delta_k=r_k+\gamma(1-d_k)V_\varphi(z_{k+1})-V_\varphi(z_k),
$$

$$
A_k=\delta_k+\gamma\lambda(1-d_k)A_{k+1},\qquad \lambda=0.95.
$$

$V_\varphi$ is the critic's predicted discounted return. Normalize advantages once
over the complete rollout batch, but do not normalize or clip rewards.

Use the standard clipped PPO objective with the following fixed first-pass settings:

| Quantity | Value | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Parallel environments | 64 | Batched independent rollouts |
| Decisions per environment per update | 256 | $16{,}384$ samples per update |
| Minibatch size | 1024 | 16 minibatches per epoch |
| Optimization epochs | 8 | Reuse each on-policy rollout moderately |
| Policy ratio clip | 0.2 | Limit policy movement |
| Value-loss coefficient | 0.5 | Fit the future-return critic |
| Entropy coefficient | 0.001 | Preserve early torque exploration |
| Learning rate | $3\times10^{-4}$ to $3\times10^{-5}$ linear decay | Adam update scale |
| Adam epsilon | $10^{-5}$ | Numerical optimizer scale |
| Gradient norm | 0.5 | Global update bound |
| Target KL | 0.015 | Stop an epoch early after an excessive policy move |

Use about three million RL decisions for the first complete run, which corresponds to
fifteen million existing RK4 steps before early episode resets. Save the checkpoint
with the best deterministic validation success rate, breaking ties by lower median
success time and then lower normalized torque effort. Do not select the last checkpoint.

## 8. Three training iterations

### Iteration A: reward and balance audit

Train for $0.5$ million decisions from near-upright states:

$$
\theta\sim U[-0.15,0.15],\quad
\beta\sim U[-0.35,0.35],\quad
\omega\sim U[-1,1],\quad
\nu\sim U[-2,2].
$$

Success flag: at least 95% of 100 deterministic near-upright episodes reach the goal
without an arm-limit termination. Failure here means the reward sign, action/log-prob
mapping, or PPO update is wrong; increasing the network size is not the next step.

### Iteration B: energy acquisition and capture

Train for $1.5$ million decisions with half the environments reset near downward and
half reset across pendulum phase:

$$
\alpha_{\rm down}\sim U[-0.05,0.05],\quad
\theta\sim U[-0.10,0.10],\quad
\omega,\nu\sim U[-0.5,0.5],
$$

or

$$
\alpha\sim U[-\pi,\pi],\quad
\theta\sim U[-0.25,0.25],\quad
\omega\sim U[-2,2],\quad
\nu\sim U[-0.5\nu_E,0.5\nu_E].
$$

Success flag: at least 80% success on 100 downward-noise validation episodes, with
zero arm-limit violations. Inspect energy, phase error, action, and critic traces before
the next iteration. Tune only the phase weight over $\{0.02,0.05,0.1\}$ if energy rises
with systematically wrong phase; tune the local coefficient inside $\Phi$ over
$\{0.5,1,2\}$ only if energy reaches its shell but capture repeatedly fails.

### Iteration C: exact benchmark refinement

Train for $1.0$ million decisions with 80% exact configured downward-rest resets and
20% near-upright resets to prevent capture forgetting. Evaluate three independent
training seeds on the same fixed suite containing the exact configured state followed
by 99 seeded downward-noise states.

Final success flags are:

1. the exact-downward episode succeeds and at least 95 of the 100-state fixed suite
   reach the configured goal;
2. no arm-limit violations;
3. median success time at most $5\,\mathrm{s}$;
4. the stricter 0.25 s dwell diagnostic passes in at least 90 episodes;
5. critic Monte Carlo return error is reported by downward, high-energy, and local-capture regions.

Stop tuning when two consecutive coefficient sweeps do not improve the first four
flags. If energy acquisition succeeds but capture remains poor, the next method change
is a separate balancing phase or terminal-value refinement, not a wider MLP.

## 9. Minimal implementation budget

The future implementation should contain only these task-oriented objects:

- `RotaryPPOConfig`: the action repeat, reward coefficients, PPO settings, curriculum
  ranges, seeds, and evaluation thresholds listed above;
- `BatchedRotaryPPOEnvironment`: batched state, previous action, goal count, reset, and
  five-RK4-step transition/reward logic;
- `RotaryActorCritic`: the shared encoder, actor decoder, critic decoder, and learned
  `log_std`;
- `RolloutBuffer`: fixed tensors for observation, latent/action, corrected log
  probability, reward components, termination, value, advantage, and return.

Only four workflow functions are needed:

- `collect_rollout`: one vectorized on-policy rollout with visible progress updates;
- `compute_gae`: reverse batched GAE and returns;
- `update_ppo`: shuffled minibatch actor-value optimization and diagnostics;
- `evaluate_policy`: deterministic seeded evaluation and artifact generation.

Independent runtime quantities are the eight-feature observation, latent action,
normalized/applied torque, five reward components, log probability, value, advantage,
return, termination cause, and curriculum stage. No wrapper-only functions, fallback
values, exception-swallowing paths, duplicate environment classes, unnecessary CLI
options, or files outside approximately 100--280 lines are planned.

Rollout data and training histories belong in machine-readable CSV/NPZ subfolders.
Success curves, reward decomposition, state/action traces, and phase-energy plots belong
in the human-readable artifact root with a short `interpretation_summary.md`.

## 10. Path from PPO critic to burst-coast MPC

After PPO passes the final flags, freeze the encoder and critic decoder. For a candidate
MPC terminal state $x_H$, compute the normalized features with the planned terminal
torque set to zero and use

$$
J_{\rm terminal}(x_H)=-\kappa_V V_\varphi(z(x_H,0)),
$$

where $\kappa_V>0$ converts the reward-return sign to a minimization cost and is fitted
once from validation return scale. The first terminal-value experiment should compare
$\kappa_V=0$ with a small logarithmic sweep; it must not jointly retune the physical MPC
objective.

Candidate coast horizons can then be rolled out under exact zero torque and scored by
the same critic. Only after this state-value route is stable should PPO itself choose a
discrete horizon. Variable horizon actions require duration-aware discounting and a
separate semi-Markov validation, so they should not be hidden inside this baseline.

## 11. Required analytical and software checks

Before training, require all of the following:

1. encoded pendulum features are identical at $\alpha$ and $\alpha+2\pi$;
2. unwrapped $\theta$ remains distinct and reaches unity at the cable limit;
3. $E_s=0$ downward and $E_s=E_s^\star$ upright at rest;
4. $\Phi=0$ only at the complete target and local cost is suppressed far from the energy shell;
5. below-target desired arm acceleration has lower phase cost than its sign reverse;
6. above-target energy reverses that comparison;
7. phase cost vanishes at $\cos\alpha=0$ and remains bounded in $[0,1)$;
8. tanh-squashed log probabilities match an independent numerical calculation;
9. batched five-step RK4 equals five calls to the existing scalar environment;
10. GAE does not bootstrap after success, arm violation, or timeout;
11. a fixed seed reproduces the first rollout and PPO update;
12. one update changes both actor and critic parameters and keeps all outputs finite.

Passing these checks is Milestone 0. The three curriculum success flags are Milestones
1--3. The result is sufficient for a proof of concept and deliberately leaves hardware
robustness, recurrent policies, learned dynamics, and variable-duration PPO outside scope.
