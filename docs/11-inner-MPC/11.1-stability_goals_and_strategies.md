# MPC with an RL terminal value: stability goals and proof strategies

Date: 2026-10-24. Handoff for a local research agent.

## Status and scope

The goal is to certify the existing rotary pendulum controller, initially without modifying its objective, frozen networks, solver, or execution. No stability theorem for the implementation has been completed. This document defines the claims and candidate strategies. The companion `swing_up_conditional_proof.md` develops one conditional reachability proof and identifies its remaining obligations.

Repository: https://github.com/VictorWuyueXia/Burst-Coast-MPC

Inspected branch: `dev`. Inspected commit: `cdce8f93f98d6c978e6a723d4aea11f4524c59ad`, “inner MPC success.” Pin this commit for reproduction; identify any later changes before transferring conclusions.

User observations: tested nominal starts successfully reach upright holding. Removing the RL terminal term appears to produce convergence; retaining it produces small persistent oscillations. These observations motivate bounded capture and holding claims. They do not establish asymptotic stability, a periodic orbit, or coverage of a continuous set of initial conditions. The no-RL comparison has not been independently inspected here.

## 1. Claims to establish

Let physical state be $x=(\theta,\alpha,\omega,\nu)^\mathsf T$. Arm angle is $\theta$, pendulum angle measured from downward is $\alpha$, and their respective velocities are $\omega,\nu$. Angles are radians and velocities rad/s. Define upright error and error state:

$$
\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(-\sin\alpha,-\cos\alpha),\qquad e=(\theta,\beta,\omega,\nu)^\mathsf T.
$$

The target rest state is $e=0$. Index $k$ counts command intervals of $\Delta=0.1$ s.

### Claim A: swing up and capture

Specify a bounded admissible initial set $\mathcal X_0$ and a capture region $\mathbf D$. Establish that every allowed initialization reaches $\mathbf D$ in finite time without a failed solve or constraint violation:

$$
\forall x_0\in\mathcal X_0,\quad \exists K_D<\infty:\ x_{K_D}\in\mathbf D.
$$

Bounded initial angles and velocities alone do not imply recoverability. State exactly which initial set is certified. Reaching upright angle at high speed does not establish capture.

### Claim B: bounded upright holding

Choose a smaller region $\Omega\subset\mathbf D$. Establish that every allowed initialization in $\mathbf D$ eventually reaches $\Omega$ and remains there. Require feasibility throughout. Prefer a uniform entry bound over the declared initial set.

The intended conclusion is practical stability or ultimate boundedness with an invariant terminal region. Do not label a bounded oscillation proof as asymptotic stability of upright rest. If formal Lyapunov stability of a set is claimed, state its distance-to-set definition and verify it separately or derive it from the certificate.

### Composition and controller memory

CEM uses the previous plan and random samples. The physical state alone does not determine its next action. Let $z=(x,h)$, where $h$ includes the stored plan and any solver random state or decision index needed to determine execution. A deterministic theorem must cover the specified initialization and all reachable histories, or all allowed random outcomes. A fixed-seed theorem must retain the batch layout and random draw convention.

Claim B must cover histories inherited from Claim A, not merely a fresh actor initialization at the same physical state. Alternatively formulate both claims on compatible augmented sets of $(x,h)$. This compatibility is essential for composing the two proofs.

## 2. Existing components available for reuse

The official baseline has horizon $N=10$, normalized torque $a=u/u_{\max}$, documented $u_{\max}=0.00459$ N m, and arm limit $\theta_{\max}=135\pi/180$. Its cost is

$$
q(x)=e^\mathsf TWe,\quad \ell(x,a)=q(x)+a^2,
$$

$$
W=\operatorname{diag}(\theta_{\max}^{-2},0.15^{-2},0.25,0.25),\qquad
J(x,U)=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\ell(x_i,a_i)+F(x_N).
$$

Here $U=(a_0,\ldots,a_{N-1})$ is a selected plan and $x_{i+1}=f(x_i,a_i)$ is the command interval transition. State penalties apply to $x_0$ through $x_{N-1}$. There is no discount or time multiplier in this cost.

$$
F(x)=\max_{j=1,2}\{-Q_j(o(x),\pi(o(x)))\}.
$$

The scaled observation $o(x)$ contains arm angle, sine and cosine of pendulum angle, and both velocities. $Q_j$ are frozen reward critics and $\pi$ is the frozen actor. The critic training reward and discount differ from the present MPC objective.

Hidden activations are SiLU; the actor ends with tanh. Each substituted critic branch is smooth. Their maximum can have a kink at a branch tie. These are explicit finite functions, not unknown black boxes; they are not piecewise affine. Their derivatives and regional bounds can be computed once the weights are available.

CEM uses three seeds, 1,024 samples per seed, 32 elites, 16 generations, and initial normalized spread 0.5. It executes the first command and replans. There is no LQR handoff in the official baseline. Sampling preserves each feasible incumbent, but final audit rejection may remove that incumbent from executable choices.

Prediction uses 1 ms integration; the executed refined trajectory uses 0.5 ms integration; archived samples are at 1 ms. Seed generation uses a different resolution. Exact-map proofs must bound these differences or explicitly limit the claim to a declared discrete simulation map.

## 3. Strategy A: progress of the existing plan cost

Imagine the plan cost as remaining task difficulty. For swing up, its absolute zero and local curvature do not matter if it is bounded below and must decrease outside the capture region.

For exact common dynamics, shift the unused plan and append terminal feedback $\kappa$, initially the frozen actor:

$$
U^{\rm shift}=(a_1,\ldots,a_{N-1},\kappa(x_N)).
$$

Define terminal defect

$$
d(z)=\ell(z,\kappa(z))+F(f(z,\kappa(z)))-F(z),
$$

where $z$ is a terminal physical state. Cancellation gives

$$
J(x_1,U^{\rm shift})-J(x_0,U)=-\ell(x_0,a_0)+d(x_N).
$$

Let $s_k$ be next selected cost minus shifted-candidate cost. Then, when the candidate is well-defined,

$$
J_{k+1}-J_k=-\ell(x_k,a_k)+d(x_{N|k})+s_k.
$$

For implementation-level comparisons, add or bound the discrepancy from prediction, execution, and tail construction. The direct residual $r_k=J_{k+1}-J_k+\ell(x_k,a_k)$ avoids assuming these discrepancies vanish.

If $\ell\ge q_D>0$ outside $\mathbf D$ and $r_k\le\bar r<q_D$, the plan cost decreases by $\delta=q_D-\bar r$. A finite lower bound then forces finite time entry. The companion document proves this implication.

A sufficient condition for asymptotic equilibrium regulation would be stronger: terminal decrease, feasible continuation, accepted cost improvement, equilibrium preservation, and positive bounds relating the certificate to physical error. None follows automatically from successful simulation. Subtracting $F(0)$ changes no decisions but does not establish positivity.

## 4. Strategy B: an independent local certificate for holding

Use $L(e)=e^\mathsf TPe$, with symmetric positive definite $P$. The existing LQR Riccati matrix is a first candidate. It is not automatically a certificate under MPC. Evaluate it under actual selected MPC actions, including the effect of histories.

Seek a verified inequality

$$
L(e_{k+1})\le\rho L(e_k)+\varepsilon,\qquad 0<\rho<1,\quad\varepsilon\ge0.
$$

The constants $\rho,\varepsilon$ are certificate bounds, not controller tuning changes. Iteration yields

$$
L(e_k)\le\rho^kL(e_0)+\frac{1-\rho^k}{1-\rho}\varepsilon.
$$

Let $b=\varepsilon/(1-\rho)$. Choose $R>r>b$ and define

$$
\mathbf D_L=\{e:L(e)\le R\},\qquad \Omega=\{e:L(e)\le r\}.
$$

If the inequality and feasibility hold throughout $\mathbf D_L$ for the allowed histories, both sets are invariant, and every start in $\mathbf D_L$ enters $\Omega$ in finite time. Coordinate bounds are $|e_i|\le\sqrt{r(P^{-1})_{ii}}$. If using a rectangular capture region from Claim A, prove it lies in the holding certificate domain or change the capture specification consistently.

The proof is at command instants. Add bounds on motion between commands for continuous holding and constraint claims. An ultimate bound alone is not a proof of an attracting periodic orbit.

## 5. Further verification strategies

1. Search for violations of cost progress or local contraction using the original controller before changing it. A counterexample rejects a candidate certificate, not necessarily the controller's true reachability.
2. If single decision progress fails, test finite blocks of $m$ decisions. Require net decrease on blocks that avoid capture and feasibility and bounded excursions between block endpoints.
3. Use interval bounds, derivative bounds with certified remainders, branch subdivision, or verified reachable sets to cover regions between samples. Automatic differentiation and dense sampling alone are not regional proofs.
4. Direct reachable-set propagation can certify capture for boxes around the benchmark starts without finding a global swing up Lyapunov function. Account for CEM branch changes and memory; continuity of the NN does not imply continuity of the selected optimizer output.
5. For the terminal function's local shape, inspect active branches, gradients, and Hessians. A unique active smooth branch with zero gradient and positive definite Hessian gives a strict local minimum. A nonzero gradient rules out positive definiteness of the normalized terminal function near that point, but does not prove MPC instability. Branch ties require nonsmooth analysis.
6. If unchanged CEM cannot be certified, report the failure before proposing an acceptance constraint or fallback. Such a safeguard defines a modified controller and must be evaluated separately.

## 6. Local agent workflow and success criteria

| Stage | Work | Success criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Reproduce | Resolve checkpoint, archive, settings, commit, random seed and batch size | Reproduce archived outputs or explain discrepancies |
| Define sets | Recover start distribution; choose capture and holding regions | Explicit inequalities and compatible histories |
| Audit | Log capture, costs, defects, residuals, feasibility, constraints | No unreported failed cases; distinguish angle crossing from capture |
| Falsify | Search initial states, history variations and solver outcomes | Retain worst cases and violation witnesses |
| Certify capture | Verify progress and feasibility or reachable-set containment | Every point of declared initial set covered |
| Certify holding | Verify invariant domain and residual contraction | Explicit final bounds and domain |
| Compose | Match captured augmented states to holding domain | End-to-end theorem with exact scope |

Do not change cost weights or retrain networks merely to make an inequality pass. First report what the original formulation supports. Shrinking the initial set is acceptable if stated and justified. Separate numerical evidence, analytically established facts, verification bounds, and assumptions.
