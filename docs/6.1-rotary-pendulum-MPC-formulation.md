# Rotary Pendulum Burst-Coast MPC Formulation

## 1. Purpose and reading order

This document defines the deterministic Quanser QUBE Servo 3 rotary-pendulum
benchmark and its torque-input burst-coast MPC problem.

The derivation follows one continuous chain:

1. two angular coordinates determine the three-dimensional mechanism;
2. the geometry determines kinetic and potential energy;
3. the Euler-Lagrange equation converts those energies into the coupled
   nonlinear equations of motion;
4. the equations of motion define the continuous state derivative;
5. RK4 converts that derivative into the discrete prediction map used by MPC;
6. MPC optimizes a torque prefix and predicts the complete zero-torque suffix.

Only the resulting nonlinear dynamics, energy, RK4 map, and MPC objective are
used at runtime. The Lagrangian is retained because it is the shortest auditable
derivation of the governing dynamics; it is not a simulator state or an MPC
decision variable.

## 2. Coordinates, state, and target

### 2.1 Why the configuration space is $S^1\times S^1$

The ideal mechanism has two rotary joints:

- the actuated base rotates the horizontal arm through angle $\theta$;
- the passive pendulum rotates through angle $\alpha$.

An angle is periodic: adding $2\pi$ gives the same geometric orientation.
The symbol

$$
S^1
$$

means the unit circle, equivalently the set of angles identified modulo
$2\pi$. Therefore,

$$
\mathcal Q=S^1\times S^1
$$

means that the ideal configuration consists of two independent angular
coordinates. It does **not** mean that the mechanism has four coordinates or
that it moves on a Cartesian plane.

The physical QUBE cable breaks the periodic equivalence of the base angle:
$\theta$ and $\theta+2\pi$ have the same arm orientation but different
cable winding. The simulator therefore stores both angles continuously and
unwrapped, even though their ideal geometric orientations belong to $S^1$.

### 2.2 Generalized coordinates and state

Generalized coordinates are the minimum independent variables needed to
describe the mechanism without adding Cartesian joint constraints. The
generalized-coordinate vector is

$$
q=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta\\
\alpha
\end{bmatrix},
$$

where:

| Symbol | Unit | Meaning |
|---:|---:|---|
| $\theta$ | rad | unwrapped rotary-arm angle |
| $\alpha$ | rad | unwrapped pendulum angle, zero vertically downward |

Its first and second time derivatives are

$$
\dot q=
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot\theta\\
\dot\alpha
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\omega\\
\nu
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\ddot q=
\begin{bmatrix}
\ddot\theta\\
\ddot\alpha
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Thus, $\omega$ is the arm angular velocity and $\nu$ is the pendulum
angular velocity. The first-order state used by the simulator and MPC is

$$
x=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta&\alpha&\omega&\nu
\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in\mathbb R^4.
$$

The scalar control input is

$$
\tau\in\mathbb R,
$$

the mechanical shaft torque applied about the vertical motor axis. Positive
$\tau$ acts in the positive $\theta$ direction. No torque acts directly at
the pendulum joint.

### 2.3 Upright error and target

The global angle convention is

$$
\alpha=0 \quad\text{downward},
\qquad
\alpha=\pi \quad\text{upright}.
$$

The pendulum error relative to upright is

$$
\beta
=\operatorname{wrap}(\alpha-\pi)
=\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(
\sin(\alpha-\pi),\cos(\alpha-\pi)
\right),
$$

so $\beta\in(-\pi,\pi]$ and $\beta=0$ at upright. The wrapped value is used
only for goal and error evaluation; it is never differentiated or integrated.

The benchmark target is

$$
x^\star=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta^\star&\pi&0&0
\end{bmatrix}^{T},
\qquad
\theta^\star=0.
$$

Interpretation: the task is not merely to raise the pendulum. It must raise the
pendulum, return the arm to its home angle, and reduce both angular velocities.

## 3. Three-dimensional geometry used by the derivation

### 3.1 Inertial frame and moving arm basis

Let

$$
\mathcal F_I=\{O,\mathbf e_x,\mathbf e_y,\mathbf e_z\}
$$

be a right-handed inertial frame. $O$ is the motor-axis origin,
$\mathbf e_z$ points vertically upward, and gravity is

$$
\mathbf g=-g\mathbf e_z.
$$

The arm-aligned horizontal unit vectors are

$$
\mathbf e_r(\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta\\
\sin\theta\\
0
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf e_t(\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
-\sin\theta\\
\cos\theta\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$\mathbf e_r$ points along the rotary arm and $\mathbf e_t$ is horizontal
and tangent to the arm's circular motion. Their derivatives are

$$
\dot{\mathbf e}_r=\omega\mathbf e_t,
\qquad
\dot{\mathbf e}_t=-\omega\mathbf e_r.
$$

### 3.2 Link positions

Let $r$ be the distance from the motor axis to the pendulum pivot, $L_p$
the pendulum pivot-to-tip length, and

$$
l=\frac{L_p}{2}
$$

the pivot-to-center-of-mass distance for the uniform-rod model.

The unit vector from the pendulum pivot toward its tip is

$$
\mathbf d(\theta,\alpha)
=\sin\alpha\,\mathbf e_t-\cos\alpha\,\mathbf e_z.
$$

At $\alpha=0$, $\mathbf d=-\mathbf e_z$ and the pendulum points downward.
At $\alpha=\pi$, $\mathbf d=\mathbf e_z$ and it points upward.

The pendulum pivot, center of mass, and tip are

$$
\mathbf p_A=r\mathbf e_r,
$$

$$
\mathbf p_P=r\mathbf e_r+l\mathbf d,
$$

$$
\mathbf p_{\mathrm{tip}}=r\mathbf e_r+L_p\mathbf d.
$$

Differentiating $\mathbf p_P$ gives

$$
\dot{\mathbf p}_P
=r\omega\mathbf e_t
+l\left(
-\sin\alpha\,\omega\mathbf e_r
+\cos\alpha\,\nu\mathbf e_t
+\sin\alpha\,\nu\mathbf e_z
\right).
$$

Interpretation: the pendulum center of mass moves because the arm rotates and
because the pendulum swings. Their simultaneous motion creates the
$\omega\nu$ coupling terms in the nonlinear dynamics.

These Cartesian quantities are used to derive the energy and to reconstruct
visualization geometry. They are not independent states.

## 4. Physical parameters and derived coefficients

### 4.1 Configured physical quantities

The future package configuration owns the following primitive quantities:

| Symbol | Default | Unit | Physical meaning |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| $g$ | $9.81$ | ${\rm m/s^2}$ | gravitational acceleration magnitude |
| $m_r$ | $0.095$ | kg | rotary-arm mass |
| $r$ | $0.085$ | m | motor axis to pendulum pivot |
| $m_p$ | $0.024$ | kg | pendulum mass |
| $L_p$ | $0.129$ | m | pendulum pivot-to-tip model length |
| $b_r$ | $1.0\times10^{-3}$ | ${\rm N\,m\,s/rad}$ | passive viscous damping at the rotary axis |
| $b_p$ | $5.0\times10^{-5}$ | ${\rm N\,m\,s/rad}$ | passive viscous damping at the pendulum joint |
| $\tau_{\max}$ | $0.0204$ | ${\rm N\,m}$ | symmetric shaft-torque bound |
| $T_s$ | $0.002$ | s | discrete control and integration step |

The damping coefficients define torques proportional and opposite to angular
velocity:

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{damp},r}=-b_r\omega,
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{damp},p}=-b_p\nu.
$$

They remove mechanical energy at rates $b_r\omega^2$ and $b_p\nu^2$.

The $0.129\,{\rm m}$ pendulum is the Quanser manual/courseware model used to
reproduce the published linear reference. The current product page reports
$0.095\,{\rm m}$; hardware use requires an explicitly measured hardware
configuration.

### 4.2 Derived inertia, coupling, and gravity coefficients

The dynamics use the following coefficients, all derived from the configured
physical quantities:

| Symbol | Definition | Physical meaning |
|---:|---:|---|
| $J_a$ | $m_rr^2/3$ | rotary-arm moment of inertia about the motor axis |
| $J_{p,c}$ | $m_pL_p^2/12$ | pendulum transverse moment of inertia about its center |
| $J_p$ | $J_{p,c}+m_pl^2=m_pL_p^2/3$ | pendulum transverse moment of inertia about its pivot |
| $J_\theta$ | $J_a+m_pr^2$ | fixed base-axis inertia from the arm and translation of the pendulum pivot |
| $C$ | $m_prl$ | inertial coupling strength between arm and pendulum acceleration |
| $G$ | $m_pgl$ | gravity torque and potential-energy scale |

All $J$ coefficients have unit ${\rm kg\,m^2}$. $C$ also has unit
${\rm kg\,m^2}$. $G$ has unit ${\rm N\,m}$, equivalently joules.

For the default parameters,

$$
J_a=2.2879167\times10^{-4}\ {\rm kg\,m^2},
$$

$$
J_{p,c}=3.3282\times10^{-5}\ {\rm kg\,m^2},
$$

$$
J_p=1.33128\times10^{-4}\ {\rm kg\,m^2},
$$

$$
J_\theta=4.0219167\times10^{-4}\ {\rm kg\,m^2},
$$

$$
C=1.3158\times10^{-4}\ {\rm kg\,m^2},
\qquad
G=0.01518588\ {\rm N\,m}.
$$

Interpretation:

- $J_\theta$ measures resistance to base rotation that is present at every
  pendulum angle.
- $J_p\sin^2\alpha$, introduced below, adds angle-dependent resistance to
  base rotation because the pendulum mass moves around the vertical axis.
- $C\cos\alpha$ transfers acceleration between the actuated arm and passive
  pendulum.
- $G\sin\alpha$ is the gravity torque that restores the pendulum toward
  downward and destabilizes upright.

The $m_pr^2$ term in $J_\theta$ is required to match Quanser's published
linear reference. Motor and hub inertias are not added to this nominal model.

## 5. From geometry to the governing dynamics

### 5.1 Why the Lagrangian is written down

The mechanism is coupled and underactuated, so direct force balances are easy
to sign incorrectly. The Lagrangian method derives both equations from the
same scalar energy model and automatically preserves the symmetric inertia
matrix and correct gravity coupling.

The kinetic energy is

$$
\begin{aligned}
K(q,\dot q)=\;&
\frac12\left(J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha\right)\omega^2
+C\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu
+\frac12J_p\nu^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Its three terms are:

1. kinetic energy associated with base rotation;
2. arm-pendulum cross-coupling energy;
3. pendulum swing kinetic energy.

With zero potential energy at downward, the gravitational potential is

$$
V(q)=G(1-\cos\alpha).
$$

The Lagrangian is the energy difference

$$
\mathcal L(q,\dot q)=K(q,\dot q)-V(q).
$$

It is used only in the following derivation. The simulator evaluates the
resulting equations of motion, not $\mathcal L$.

### 5.2 Euler-Lagrange equation and generalized force

For each generalized coordinate $q_i\in\{\theta,\alpha\}$, the
Euler-Lagrange equation is

$$
\frac{d}{dt}
\left(
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot q_i}
\right)
-\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial q_i}
=(Q_{\mathrm{nc}})_i.
$$

The left side converts kinetic and potential energy into inertial and gravity
terms. $Q_{\mathrm{nc}}$ is the vector of non-conservative generalized
forces: applied torque and viscous damping. Here,

$$
Q_{\mathrm{nc}}=
\begin{bmatrix}
\tau-b_r\omega\\
-b_p\nu
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The first entry acts in the $\theta$ coordinate. The second entry contains no
actuator torque because the pendulum joint is passive.

### 5.3 Governing nonlinear mechanical equation

Applying the Euler-Lagrange equation yields the benchmark plant.

> **Governing nonlinear dynamics**
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> M(\alpha)\ddot q+h(q,\dot q)+D_v\dot q=\mathbf b_\tau\tau
> }
> $$

The mass matrix is

$$
M(\alpha)=
\begin{bmatrix}
J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha&C\cos\alpha\\
C\cos\alpha&J_p
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Its diagonal entries are effective rotational inertias. Its equal off-diagonal
entries express reciprocal acceleration coupling between the two coordinates.

The nonlinear velocity and gravity vector is

$$
h(q,\dot q)=
\begin{bmatrix}
2J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu-C\sin\alpha\,\nu^2\\
-J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2+G\sin\alpha
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The first entry contains Coriolis and centripetal terms acting on the arm. The
second contains the pendulum's centrifugal and gravity terms.

The viscous damping matrix and input-distribution vector are

$$
D_v=
\begin{bmatrix}
b_r&0\\
0&b_p
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf b_\tau=
\begin{bmatrix}
1\\
0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$\mathbf b_\tau$ states that shaft torque acts directly on $\theta$ and
not directly on $\alpha$.

Written as two scalar equations, the same governing dynamics are

> $$
> \boxed{
> \begin{aligned}
> \left(J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha\right)\ddot\theta
> &+C\cos\alpha\,\ddot\alpha
> +2J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu\\
> &-C\sin\alpha\,\nu^2+b_r\omega=\tau,
> \end{aligned}
> }
> $$
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> J_p\ddot\alpha
> +C\cos\alpha\,\ddot\theta
> -J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2
> +G\sin\alpha+b_p\nu=0.
> }
> $$

These two equations are coupled through both accelerations. A torque applied
to the arm accelerates the unactuated pendulum through $C\cos\alpha$. The
system is underactuated because it has two coordinates but only one independent
input.

### 5.4 Explicit acceleration solve

The simulator needs $\ddot\theta$ and $\ddot\alpha$. Move all non-inertial
terms to the right side and define the physically named net generalized
torques

$$
\begin{aligned}
s_\theta(x,\tau)=\;&
\tau-b_r\omega
-2J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu
+C\sin\alpha\,\nu^2,\\
s_\alpha(x)=\;&
-b_p\nu
+J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2
-G\sin\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then

$$
M(\alpha)
\begin{bmatrix}
\ddot\theta\\
\ddot\alpha
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
s_\theta\\
s_\alpha
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Let

$$
M_{11}=J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha,
\qquad
M_{12}=C\cos\alpha,
\qquad
M_{22}=J_p.
$$

The determinant of the mass matrix is

$$
\Delta(\alpha)
=\det M(\alpha)
=M_{11}M_{22}-M_{12}^2.
$$

$\Delta(\alpha)$ measures whether the coupled inertia matrix can be solved
uniquely. The decoupled accelerations are

> $$
> \boxed{
> \ddot\theta
> =\frac{M_{22}s_\theta-M_{12}s_\alpha}{\Delta(\alpha)},
> \qquad
> \ddot\alpha
> =\frac{-M_{12}s_\theta+M_{11}s_\alpha}{\Delta(\alpha)}.
> }
> $$

The earlier one-use acceleration aliases are intentionally absent. The
implementation should solve the $2\times2$ linear system directly; the
fractions are retained as an independent mathematical check.

The first-order continuous plant is therefore

> $$
> \boxed{
> \dot x=f(x,\tau)=
> \begin{bmatrix}
> \omega\\
> \nu\\
> \ddot\theta(x,\tau)\\
> \ddot\alpha(x,\tau)
> \end{bmatrix}.
> }
> $$

This $f$ is the governing function used by both the simulator and MPC
integrator.

### 5.5 Well-posedness

At the vertical configurations, define

$$
\Delta_0=J_\theta J_p-C^2.
$$

For all angles,

$$
\Delta(\alpha)
=\Delta_0+\left(J_p^2+C^2\right)\sin^2\alpha.
$$

Therefore, the mass matrix is symmetric positive definite for every
$\alpha$ when

$$
J_p>0,
\qquad
\Delta_0>0.
$$

Positive definiteness means every nonzero generalized velocity has positive
kinetic energy; nonsingularity then guarantees a unique acceleration solve.

For the default model,

$$
\Delta_0=3.62296758\times10^{-8}\ {\rm kg^2\,m^4}>0.
$$

Interpretation: every admissible state and torque produces one unique pair of
angular accelerations.

## 6. Mechanical energy and zero-torque coast

The total mechanical energy is

$$
E(x)=K(q,\dot q)+V(q),
$$

or explicitly,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(x)=\;&
\frac12\left(J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha\right)\omega^2
+C\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu\\
&+\frac12J_p\nu^2+G(1-\cos\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

The downward-rest and upright-rest energies are

$$
E_{\mathrm{down}}=0,
\qquad
E^\star=E_{\mathrm{up}}=2G.
$$

For the default model,

$$
E^\star=0.03037176\ {\rm J}.
$$

Along the exact governing dynamics,

> $$
> \boxed{
> \dot E=\tau\omega-b_r\omega^2-b_p\nu^2.
> }
> $$

$\tau\omega$ is mechanical input power. The two negative terms are passive
dissipation. During the benchmark coast,

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{coast}}=0,
$$

so

$$
\dot E_{\mathrm{coast}}
=-b_r\omega^2-b_p\nu^2\le0.
$$

This identity is used to interpret coast behavior and to verify the numerical
model. A zero-voltage motor model would add back-EMF braking and is not the same
plant.

The normalized target-energy error used by MPC is

$$
e_E(x)=\frac{E(x)-E^\star}{E^\star}.
$$

It satisfies

$$
e_E(x^\star)=0,
\qquad
e_E(x_{\mathrm{down}})=-1.
$$

## 7. Equilibria, linearization, and natural period

### 7.1 Equilibrium families

With zero torque and zero velocity, the downward and upright equilibrium
families are

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{down}}
=\{(\theta,0,0,0):\theta\in S^1\},
$$

$$
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{up}}
=\{(\theta,\pi,0,0):\theta\in S^1\}.
$$

They are families rather than single points because the ideal energy does not
depend on $\theta$. The benchmark goal separately selects
$\theta^\star=0$.

### 7.2 Local linear model

The nonlinear dynamics remain the MPC prediction model. Linearization is used
only to verify the nonlinear Jacobian, expose upright instability, and define
the natural time scale used by the prediction horizon.

Choose either vertical equilibrium

$$
\alpha_e\in\{0,\pi\},
$$

and define:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| $\delta=\alpha-\alpha_e$ | small pendulum displacement from that equilibrium |
| $\tilde\theta=\theta-\theta^\star$ | arm displacement from home |
| $\sigma_e=\cos\alpha_e$ | $+1$ downward and $-1$ upright |
| $z=[\tilde\theta,\delta,\omega,\nu]^T$ | local perturbation state |

Keeping only terms that are first order in $z$ and $\tau$ gives

$$
\dot z=A_{\alpha_e}z+\Gamma_{\alpha_e}\tau,
$$

where $A_{\alpha_e}$ is the state Jacobian
$\partial f/\partial x$, and $\Gamma_{\alpha_e}$ is the torque Jacobian
$\partial f/\partial\tau$, both evaluated at the selected equilibrium:

$$
A_{\alpha_e}=
\begin{bmatrix}
0&0&1&0\\
0&0&0&1\\
0&CG/\Delta_0&-J_pb_r/\Delta_0&\sigma_eCb_p/\Delta_0\\
0&-\sigma_eJ_\theta G/\Delta_0&\sigma_eCb_r/\Delta_0&-J_\theta b_p/\Delta_0
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
\Gamma_{\alpha_e}=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
0\\
J_p/\Delta_0\\
-\sigma_eC/\Delta_0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Interpretation:

- at downward, $\sigma_e=+1$, so gravity produces a restoring pendulum term;
- at upright, $\sigma_e=-1$, the sign reverses and produces an unstable mode;
- the first column is zero because the ideal plant has no restoring torque in
  the arm angle.

Ignoring damping, the coupled downward oscillation frequency and corresponding
upright growth-rate scale are

$$
\omega_n=\sqrt{\frac{J_\theta G}{\Delta_0}},
\qquad
T_n=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_n}.
$$

For the default model,

$$
\omega_n=12.983874\ {\rm rad/s},
\qquad
T_n=0.483922\ {\rm s}.
$$

## 8. RK4 discrete prediction model

MPC and the simulator operate in discrete steps. They share the map

$$
x_{k+1}=F_d(x_k,\tau_k),
$$

where $x_k$ is the state at step $k$, $\tau_k$ is held constant for one
step, and $F_d$ is one fourth-order Runge-Kutta update of the continuous
governing function $f$.

The four RK4 slope estimates are

$$
k_1=f(x_k,\tau_k),
$$

$$
k_2=f\left(x_k+\frac{T_s}{2}k_1,\tau_k\right),
$$

$$
k_3=f\left(x_k+\frac{T_s}{2}k_2,\tau_k\right),
$$

$$
k_4=f\left(x_k+T_sk_3,\tau_k\right).
$$

Each $k_i\in\mathbb R^4$ is a candidate state derivative evaluated within
the step. The discrete map is

> $$
> \boxed{
> F_d(x_k,\tau_k)
> =x_k+\frac{T_s}{6}\left(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4\right).
> }
> $$

The same configured parameters and the same $F_d$ must be used for plant
rollout and MPC prediction. The angles remain unwrapped.

## 9. Burst-coast MPC problem

### 9.1 Prediction length and burst candidates

The prediction horizon spans

$$
n_T=5.0
$$

coupled natural periods. The number of discrete prediction steps is

$$
H=
\left\lceil
\frac{n_TT_n}{T_s}
\right\rceil.
$$

$H$ is the full number of predicted steps, including both burst and coast.

The configured burst-fraction candidates are

$$
\Lambda=\{0.1,0.2,0.3\}.
$$

For each $\lambda\in\Lambda$,

$$
B(\lambda)=\operatorname{round}(\lambda H)
$$

is the number of optimized burst steps, and

$$
H-B(\lambda)
$$

is the number of fixed zero-torque coast steps. No separate symbol is
introduced for coast length because it is used only as this difference.

For the default model,

$$
H=1210,
$$

with

| $\lambda$ | Burst steps $B$ | Coast steps $H-B$ |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.1$ | $121$ | $1089$ |
| $0.2$ | $242$ | $968$ |
| $0.3$ | $363$ | $847$ |

The finite set is enumerated outside the nonlinear solver. Each $\lambda$
therefore produces one smooth, fixed-dimension torque optimization.

### 9.2 Decision variables and enforced coast

For one candidate $\lambda$, the decision vector contains only the active
torques:

$$
\boldsymbol\tau_\lambda=
\begin{bmatrix}
\tau_0&\tau_1&\cdots&\tau_{B(\lambda)-1}
\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
$$

The complete predicted input is

$$
\tau_k=
\begin{cases}
\text{optimized continuous signed torque},
&0\le k<B(\lambda),\\
0,
&B(\lambda)\le k<H.
\end{cases}
$$

The active input bound is

$$
|\tau_k|\le\tau_{\max},
\qquad
0\le k<B(\lambda).
$$

Interpretation: sparsity penalties are unnecessary. The required structure is
imposed exactly as one variable-magnitude torque prefix followed by one fixed
zero-torque suffix.

### 9.3 Objective and constraints

Let $\tau_{-1}$ denote the torque applied immediately before the new plan.
The normalized torque-slew weight inherited from the inverted-pendulum
baseline is

$$
w_{\Delta\tau}=10^{-3}.
$$

For candidate $\lambda$, the optimized value is

> $$
> \boxed{
> \begin{aligned}
> J_\lambda^\star(x_0,\tau_{-1})
> =\min_{\boldsymbol\tau_\lambda}
> \Bigg[
> &\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}e_E(x_k)^2\\
> &+w_{\Delta\tau}
> \sum_{k=0}^{B(\lambda)-1}
> \left(
> \frac{\tau_k-\tau_{k-1}}{\tau_{\max}}
> \right)^2
> \Bigg].
> \end{aligned}
> }
> $$

The first sum drives total mechanical energy toward the upright target energy
through both burst and coast. The second sum weakly regularizes abrupt changes
within the active torque sequence. There is no terminal term because its
inherited weight is zero.

The optimization is subject to the measured initial state

$$
x_0=x_{\mathrm{measured}},
$$

active propagation

$$
x_{k+1}=F_d(x_k,\tau_k),
\qquad
0\le k<B(\lambda),
$$

coast propagation

$$
x_{k+1}=F_d(x_k,0),
\qquad
B(\lambda)\le k<H,
$$

and the active torque bound stated above.

The selected split is

$$
\lambda^\star
=\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{argmin}}\;
J_\lambda^\star(x_0,\tau_{-1}).
$$

$\operatorname{argmin}$ means the candidate $\lambda$ attaining the
smallest optimized objective. Its torque prefix and zero-torque suffix form the
accepted plan.

Energy matching does not uniquely identify the upright state: many states can
have $E=E^\star$. This limitation is intentional in the inherited baseline.
The complete state goal below, not zero energy error alone, determines episode
success.

### 9.4 Goal and execution

The goal set is the collection of states satisfying

$$
\mathcal X_{\mathrm{goal}}=
\left\{
x:
|\theta-\theta^\star|\le\varepsilon_\theta,\;
|\beta|\le\varepsilon_\beta,\;
|\omega|\le\varepsilon_\omega,\;
|\nu|\le\varepsilon_\nu
\right\}.
$$

The default tolerances are

$$
\varepsilon_\theta=\varepsilon_\beta=0.05\ {\rm rad},
$$

$$
\varepsilon_\omega=\varepsilon_\nu=0.05\ {\rm rad/s},
\qquad
N_g=5.
$$

The state must remain in $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{goal}}$ for $N_g$
consecutive samples. Arrival at $\mathcal X_{\mathrm{goal}}$ ends the
benchmark episode; it does not claim that the unstable upright neighborhood is
invariant under zero torque.

At each decision epoch:

1. solve every configured $\lambda$ from the same measured state;
2. require every candidate solve to succeed;
3. select $\lambda^\star$;
4. execute its torque prefix and complete zero-torque suffix on the fixed
   control clock;
5. replan after step $H$.

There is no fallback plant, fallback torque, or skipped failed candidate.

## 10. Configuration ownership

The future `rotary_pendulum/configs/default-config.yaml` owns only physical
truth and experiment choices:

| Configuration group | Required quantities |
|---|---|
| simulation | $T_s$ |
| rotary pendulum | $g,m_r,r,m_p,L_p,b_r,b_p,\tau_{\max}$ |
| initial state | $\theta_0,\alpha_0,\omega_0,\nu_0$ |
| goal | $\varepsilon_\theta,\varepsilon_\beta,\varepsilon_\omega,\varepsilon_\nu,N_g$ |
| MPC | $n_T,\Lambda$ |

The following are derived and must not be independently configured:

$$
l,\ J_a,\ J_{p,c},\ J_p,\ J_\theta,\ C,\ G,\ \Delta_0,\ \omega_n,\ T_n,\ H,
\ B(\lambda).
$$

This prevents duplicate physical sources of truth.

## 11. Required mathematical checks

The formulation is consistent only when:

1. $\mathbf e_r$, $\mathbf e_t$, and $\mathbf e_z$ are orthonormal.
2. $\|\mathbf d\|=1$.
3. The Cartesian velocity matches a finite difference of $\mathbf p_P$.
4. $M(\alpha)=M(\alpha)^T$.
5. $\Delta(\alpha)>0$ over a complete pendulum revolution.
6. The mass-matrix solve and explicit acceleration formulas agree.
7. Numerical Jacobians at downward and upright match
   $A_{\alpha_e},\Gamma_{\alpha_e}$.
8. The lossless zero-torque model conserves $E$ as RK4 error tends to zero.
9. The damped model satisfies
   $\dot E=\tau\omega-b_r\omega^2-b_p\nu^2$.
10. Simulator and MPC one-step maps agree for identical $x,\tau,T_s$, and
    physical parameters.
11. Every input from step $B(\lambda)$ through $H-1$ is exactly zero.
12. The objective evaluates the complete coast interval.
13. Goal evaluation uses $\beta$, while integration uses unwrapped
    $\alpha$.

## 12. Explicit exclusions

This benchmark does not include:

- RL policies, critics, replay, rewards, or training;
- learned or adaptive selection of $B,H$;
- process noise, sensor noise, or state estimation;
- voltage-input, current, or back-EMF dynamics;
- hardware drivers or safety-supervisor logic;
- collision obstacles or redundant Cartesian states;
- local LQR switching or invariant-set claims;
- continuous-time collocation or mixed-integer MPC.
