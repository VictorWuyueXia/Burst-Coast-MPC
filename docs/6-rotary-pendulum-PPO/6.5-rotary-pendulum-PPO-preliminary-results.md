# Rotary-Pendulum PPO Preliminary Results and Next-Step Recommendation

## 1. Outcome

The first PPO formulation is not successful, but the experiment answered the intended
design question. Sixteen trials were trained in two sweeps, with eight independent
processes running concurrently on eight NVIDIA L40S GPUs. No trial achieved the strict
swing-up-and-hold objective.

The failures are structured rather than random:

1. short training learns energy acquisition, then maintains a high-speed rotating
   limit cycle instead of capturing upright;
2. longer curriculum training suppresses that overshoot, but settles into an
   under-energy oscillation that never reaches upright;
3. even policies trained only from near-upright states cannot hold the strict goal for
   0.25 s.

PPO optimization remained finite and repeatable, and later evaluations usually had no
arm-limit violations. The evidence therefore points to reward and curriculum mismatch,
not inadequate neural-network capacity. The current 8,963-parameter actor-critic should
be retained for the next controlled experiment.

This is a proof-of-concept conclusion. No trained checkpoint from these sweeps should
be used as an MPC terminal value or treated as a working controller.

## 2. Experiment design

### 2.1 Parallel execution

Each GPU trained one complete independent seed. A trial used 512 CPU-vectorized
environments to advance the nonlinear RK4 plant and one GPU for actor-critic inference
and PPO updates. Independent trials are a better use of the eight GPUs than distributed
data parallelism here because the network has only 8,963 parameters and PPO data are
on-policy.

Every update collected

$$
N_{\rm batch}=512\times128=65{,}536
$$

decisions, where 512 is the number of parallel environments and 128 is the rollout
length per environment. Each decision holds torque for five 0.002 s physics steps, so
the policy acts every

$$
\Delta t=5(0.002)=0.010\ \mathrm{s}.
$$

All trials used minibatches of 8,192 samples, six optimization epochs, and a learning
rate decreasing linearly from $3\times10^{-4}$ to $3\times10^{-5}$.

### 2.2 Sweep 1: energy and exploration

Four configurations were run with two seeds each for 1,507,328 collected decisions per
seed. The common curriculum budgets were 250,000 near-upright decisions, 750,000 mixed
phase/downward decisions, and 500,000 exact-downward refinement decisions.

| Family | Phase weight | Entropy weight | Initial $\log\sigma$ | Question |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `baseline` | 0.05 | 0.001 | -0.5 | Does the proposed default work? |
| `explore` | 0.05 | 0.005 | -0.2 | Is exploration the bottleneck? |
| `phase_low` | 0.02 | 0.001 | -0.5 | Is phase shaping too strong? |
| `phase_high` | 0.10 | 0.001 | -0.5 | Does stronger phase shaping improve pumping? |

$\sigma$ is the state-independent standard deviation of the unsquashed Gaussian actor;
it is initialized by the listed $\log\sigma$. All other reward and PPO settings were
held fixed.

### 2.3 Sweep 2: capture curriculum

The second sweep used the best first-sweep phase weight, 0.02, and isolated local
capture difficulty. Again, every family used two seeds.

| Family | Curriculum decision budgets | Near-upright box | Decisions collected per seed |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `capture_wide` | $[3.0\mathrm{M},0,0]$ | original | 3,014,656 |
| `capture_tight` | $[3.0\mathrm{M},0,0]$ | tight | 3,014,656 |
| `integrated_wide` | $[2.0\mathrm{M},2.0\mathrm{M},4.0\mathrm{M}]$ | original | 8,060,928 |
| `integrated_tight` | $[2.0\mathrm{M},2.0\mathrm{M},4.0\mathrm{M}]$ | tight | 8,060,928 |

The tight near-upright initialization box was

$$
|\theta|\leq0.08,\quad |\beta|\leq0.12,\quad
|\omega|\leq0.15,\quad |\nu|\leq0.30,
$$

where $\theta$ is rotary-arm angle, $\beta$ is wrapped pendulum error from upright,
$\omega=\dot\theta$ is arm speed, and $\nu=\dot\alpha$ is pendulum speed. Bounds were
sampled uniformly. The original box is wider: $0.15$, $0.35$, $1.0$, and $2.0$ in the
same order and units.

## 3. Evaluation definitions

The pendulum-relative swing energy is

$$
E_s=\frac12J_p\nu^2+G(1-\cos\alpha),
$$

where $J_p=m_pL_p^2/3$ is the derived pendulum pivot inertia, $G=m_pgl$ is the gravity
torque scale, $m_p$ is pendulum mass, $L_p$ is total pendulum length, $l=L_p/2$ is its
center-of-mass length, and $g$ is gravitational acceleration. These values come from
the physical configuration. The normalized energy diagnostic is

$$
\eta=\frac{E_s}{2G}.
$$

$\eta=0$ is downward rest and $\eta=1$ is the upright potential-energy target. Values
above one are not automatically good: the pendulum may cross upright too quickly to
capture.

Wrapped upright error is

$$
\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\alpha-\pi),\cos(\alpha-\pi)).
$$

Each checkpoint was evaluated deterministically from one exact downward-rest state and
99 seeded downward perturbations. Strict success requires

$$
|\theta|,|\beta|,|\omega|,|\nu|\leq0.05
$$

in their respective radian and radian-per-second units continuously for 125 physics
steps, or 0.25 s. A separate diagnostic sampled 256 common tight-box states and tested
both the 0.01 s training dwell and the required 0.25 s dwell.

Entries are two-seed means except the count and maximum columns, which are seed totals
and maxima. Every family had 0% final strict success. Return is only a within-reward
optimization diagnostic; it must not be interpreted as task success across reward
weights.

Sweep 1 answers the energy question clearly. All policies pass extremely close to
upright, but $\eta$ peaks between 1.58 and 1.83 and pendulum speed reaches approximately
27--29 rad/s. The lower phase weight gives the best reported return and greatest energy,
yet its trace becomes a persistent rotation rather than a capture trajectory.

Sweep 2 shows that more training is not sufficient. Both integrated families converge
near $\eta=0.77$, never closer than about 1.2 rad from upright. The few recorded training
successes in tight-box runs are sparse and consistent with favorable initialization;
they do not grow into a learned stabilization skill. In the common 256-state diagnostic,
all eight final checkpoints scored 0% for the required 0.25 s dwell. Their 0.01 s success
rates were only 0--1.56%.

## 5. Diagnosis

### 5.1 Local reward resolution is far coarser than the goal

The current local potential normalizes pendulum angle by $\pi$ and pendulum speed by
$\nu_E=21.360690$ rad/s. At the success boundary, its two pendulum contributions are

$$
\left(\frac{0.05}{\pi}\right)^2=2.53\times10^{-4},\qquad
\left(\frac{0.05}{\nu_E}\right)^2=5.48\times10^{-6}.
$$

Their change over one 0.01 s action is smaller still. Meanwhile, observed mean phase
penalties were commonly $2.6\times10^{-3}$ to $1.16\times10^{-2}$ per decision, before
torque-change and effort penalties. PPO can improve the recorded return without learning
the precision required by the terminal condition.

### 5.2 Endpoint progress is bounded while regularizers accumulate

The current progress reward is

$$
r_{\rm progress,k}=\Phi(x_k)-\Phi(x_{k+1}),
$$

where $\Phi$ is the bounded task cost potential and lower is better. Its undiscounted
episode sum is only $\Phi(x_0)-\Phi(x_T)$, while phase, effort, slew, and boundary costs
accumulate at every decision. This creates attractive compromise limit cycles. The
discount factor is

$$
\gamma=\exp(-\Delta t/\tau_\gamma)=\exp(-0.01/5)=0.998002,
$$

where $\tau_\gamma=5$ s is the configured discount time constant. Discount-consistent
potential shaping for a cost potential should use

$$
r_{\rm shape,k}=\Phi(x_k)-\gamma\Phi(x_{k+1}).
$$

This change alone is not enough; $\Phi$ must first resolve the actual capture tolerances.

### 5.3 The curriculum advances without demonstrating prerequisite skill

Stages change after fixed decision counts. The agent therefore leaves near-upright
training with approximately zero capture competency, enters energy acquisition, and
finally receives mostly exact downward states. Longer training then optimizes a safer
sub-target oscillation rather than composing swing-up with a learned local controller.

### 5.4 The network is not the first bottleneck

Policy loss, value loss, entropy, KL divergence, actions, and states remained finite.
Seed pairs showed the same qualitative modes. Increasing width would give the optimizer
more capacity to represent the same misaligned objective. There is no current evidence
for recurrence, separate encoders, or a larger actor-critic.

## 6. Recommended next development iteration

### Milestone R1: repair reward scale before training

Replace the local error with tolerance-scaled quantities:

$$
q_{\rm cap}(x)=
\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta_{\rm tol}}\right)^2+
\left(\frac{\nu}{\nu_{\rm tol}}\right)^2+
c_a\left(\frac{\theta}{\theta_{\rm tol}}\right)^2+
c_a\left(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{\rm tol}}\right)^2.
$$

$\beta_{\rm tol}$, $\nu_{\rm tol}$, $\theta_{\rm tol}$, and $\omega_{\rm tol}$ are the
configured success tolerances, all currently 0.05; $c_a$ is one tunable arm-rest weight,
initially 0.1. Saturate $q_{\rm cap}$ smoothly so rare states cannot dominate. Retain the
energy gate and phase concept, but ensure a one-step improvement inside the capture tube
is larger than slew and effort regularization.

Use $\Phi(x_k)-\gamma\Phi(x_{k+1})$ for discount-consistent shaping. Scale regularizers
by $\Delta t$ so changing the action rate does not change their physical meaning.

Validation flag: deterministic action-sign and one-step reward tests pass at downward
rest, energy injection, energy removal, upright capture, and arm-boundary states.

### Milestone R2: require local capture competency

Train only on the tight near-upright box and evaluate a fixed 256-state set after every
few updates. Do not advance the curriculum until two seeds each exceed 90% success for
the full 0.25 s dwell with no arm violations.

Validation flag: strict local success is at least 90% for two seeds and remains above
80% on the original wider near-upright box.

### Milestone R3: expand energy without forgetting capture

Once R2 passes, expand initial energy and phase ranges gradually. Keep a fixed fraction
of near-upright resets in every batch and evaluate both local capture and exact downward
swing-up. Record peak $\eta$, first upright entry speed $|\nu|$, capture-tube entries,
dwell duration, and arm violations.

Validation flag: at least 50% of exact-downward episodes enter the capture tube with
$|\nu|<1$ rad/s before attempting a full success target. This partial flag distinguishes
energy braking from final precision.

### Milestone R4: use linear control as a teacher only if R2 fails

If the repaired PPO still cannot pass local capture, use the upright region from the
piecewise-affine plan to construct an LQR feedback teacher. Behavior-clone the actor in
that local region, then fine-tune the same network with PPO. This directly tests whether
exploration, rather than representation, blocks local learning.

Validation flag: the cloned actor matches teacher torque over the local validation grid
and retains at least 90% strict local success after PPO fine-tuning.

Do not enlarge the network, add recurrence, or start MPC-terminal-value training before
these milestones pass.
