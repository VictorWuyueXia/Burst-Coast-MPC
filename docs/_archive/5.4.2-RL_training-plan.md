## Summary

Use **simulation online training with offline warm start**. The RL agent should not be trained only once from a fixed dataset, because the MPC behavior changes when the actor starts choosing horizons. The clean workflow is:

$$
\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\text{actor chooses } H
\rightarrow
\text{MPC solves torque plan}
\rightarrow
\text{control applied}
\rightarrow
\text{environment advances}
\rightarrow
\text{transition stored}
\rightarrow
\text{critic and actor updated}
$$

For the current stage, do **not** use the learned $Q$ value as a terminal cost inside the QP MPC yet. Use $Q(s,H)$ to choose the horizon outside MPC. Later, if you want a learned terminal cost inside MPC, use a state value $V(s)$ or cost value $C(s)$, not the horizon action value $Q(s,H)$ directly.

The core direction: horizon only action, fixed burst sleep ratio, cost critic $C_\psi(s,H)$, actor $\pi_\phi(H\mid s)$, closed loop actor critic cost, and $n$ step targets. I will build the training workflow directly on that structure.

# 1. Training formulation

The current physical loop is:

$$
o_k
\rightarrow
\mathrm{MPC}
\rightarrow
u_k
\rightarrow
\mathrm{environment}
\rightarrow
o_{k+1}
$$

The new training loop should be:

$$
o_k
\rightarrow
s_k
\rightarrow
\pi_\phi(H_k\mid s_k)
\rightarrow
\mathrm{MPC}(s_k,H_k)
\rightarrow
u_k
\rightarrow
\mathrm{environment}
\rightarrow
s_{k+1}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{replay}
\rightarrow
\mathrm{RL~update}
$$

The actor chooses only:

$$
a_k=H_k
$$

The MPC still chooses torque:

$$
\begin{align*}
u_{k:k+H_k-1}^{\star}
&= 
\arg\min J_{\mathrm{MPC}}
\end{align*}
$$

The environment transition is therefore:

$$
s_{k+1} = F(s_k,H_k)
$$

where $F$ includes the MPC solve, control application, pendulum dynamics, and observation construction.

The RL layer is not learning torque. It is learning:

$$
\boxed{
\text{which horizon gives the best closed loop behavior at the current state.}
}
$$

# 2. Offline or online interactive training?

Use a two stage scheme.

## Stage 1: Offline warm start from baseline rollouts

First collect data using:

1. Fixed short horizon.

2. Fixed medium horizon.

3. Fixed long horizon.

4. Current hardcoded event triggered version.

5. Random sampled horizon policy.

This creates an initial replay buffer:

$$
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{D}_0 = \{(s_k,H_k,c_k,s_{k+1},d_k)\}
\end{align*}
$$

where $d_k$ is the terminal flag.

Then train the critic offline on this buffer. This gives the critic a first approximate notion of which states and horizons are good.

This stage is **offline training** because the policy is not yet controlling its own data distribution.

## Stage 2: Online interactive training in simulation

After the warm start, let the actor choose horizons during simulated rollouts:

$$
H_k \sim \pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)
$$

Then run MPC, apply control, observe the next state, store the transition, and update the networks.

This is **online interactive training** because the agent’s current policy generates the data it learns from.

This should be done in simulation only at first. Do not run online learning on hardware or expensive real systems until the policy is frozen and validated.

Recommended answer:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Use offline warm start first, then online interactive training in simulation.}
}
$$

Do not train from scratch only online. The early $Q$ estimates will be poor, exploration will be noisy, and MPC solve cost will make bad exploration expensive.

# 3. RL step by step workflow built on your existing loop

## Step 0: Reset episode

Sample initial pendulum state:

$$
x_0=[\theta_0,\omega_0]^T
$$

Construct actor critic observation:

$$
s_0
$$

For global pendulum motion, I recommend using:

$$
s_k=
\begin{bmatrix}
\sin\theta_k\\
\cos\theta_k\\
\omega_k/\omega_s\\
e_E(x_k)\\
\bar u_{\mathrm{cum},k}\\
H_{k-1}/H_{\max}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

instead of raw $\theta_k$, unless $\theta_k$ is always wrapped and the discontinuity is handled carefully.

## Step 1: Actor selects horizon

During training:

$$
H_k \sim \pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)
$$

During evaluation:

$$
H_k^\star = \arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

where $C_\psi$ is the cost critic.

## Step 2: Compute burst and sleep lengths

For the current stage, keep fixed split ratio:

$$
\lambda=\lambda_0
$$

$$
B_k = \max(1,\lceil \lambda_0 H_k\rceil)
$$

$$
S_k = H_k - B_k
$$

Do not let RL choose $\lambda$ yet.

## Step 3: MPC solves inner QP

Use the current MPC cost:

$$
J_{\mathrm{MPC}} =
\sum_{i=0}^{H_k-1}
\left[
q_E e_E(x_{k+i})^2
+
q_{\Delta u}\bar{\Delta u}_{k+i}^2
\right]
$$

Optional:

$$
+ q_u \bar u_{k+i}^2
$$

but keep $q_u$ zero or small for now.

The MPC outputs:

$$
u_{k:k+B_k-1}^{\star}
$$

Apply only the first control:

$$
u_k = u_k^\star
$$

## Step 4: Environment advances

Propagate:

$$
x_{k+1} = f_d(x_k,u_k)
$$

Construct:

$$
s_{k+1}
$$

## Step 5: Compute actor critic cost

Use a closed loop cost:

$$
c_k^{\mathrm{AC}} =
w_{\mathrm{conv}}c_{\mathrm{conv}}(x_k)
+
w_{\mathrm{act}}c_{\mathrm{act}}(u_k)
+
w_{\mathrm{cmp}}c_{\mathrm{cmp}}(H_k,B_k)
+
w_{\mathrm{fail}}c_{\mathrm{fail}}(x_k)
$$

For this stage, use:

$$
c_{\mathrm{conv}}(x_k) =
e_E(x_k)^2
+
g_{\mathrm{loc}}(x_k)x_k^TQ_xx_k
$$

$$
c_{\mathrm{act}}(u_k)=\left(\frac{u_k}{u_{\max}}\right)^2
$$

$$
c_{\mathrm{cmp}}(H_k,B_k) =
\sum_{\lambda \in \{0.1,0.2,0.3\}}
\left[
w_{\mathrm{cmp},B}
\left(\frac{B_\lambda}{B_{\max}}\right)^2
+
w_{\mathrm{cmp},C}
\left(\frac{H_k}{H_{\max}}\right)
\right]
$$

where:

$$
B_\lambda=\max(1,\lceil \lambda H_k\rceil)
$$

Your current note says $\bar u_k$ is accumulated actuation effort. I would separate this carefully:

$$
\bar u_{\mathrm{inst},k}^2 = \left(\frac{u_k}{u_{\max}}\right)^2
$$

$$
\bar u_{\mathrm{cum},k} = \sum_{i=0}^{k}
\left(\frac{u_i}{u_{\max}}\right)^2
$$

Use instantaneous actuation in the per step cost. Use cumulative actuation as an episode metric or observation. If you put cumulative actuation inside every per step cost, you double count earlier actuation many times.

## Step 6: Store transition

Store:

$$
(s_k,H_k,c_k^{\mathrm{AC}},s_{k+1},d_k)
$$

in replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$.

Also log:

$$
x_k,\ u_k,\ H_k,\ B_k,\ S_k,\ t_{\mathrm{solve},k},\ e_E(x_k),\ \text{success flag}
$$

## Step 7: Update RL networks

After warmup, sample minibatches from replay:

$$
(s_i,H_i,c_i,s_{i+1},d_i)\sim \mathcal{D}
$$

Then update critic and actor.

Do not update inside the MPC QP solve. The RL update happens outside the control computation.

# 4. When should the neural networks update weights?

Recommended schedule:

1. Collect at least $N_{\mathrm{warm}}$ random or baseline transitions before any update.

2. After warmup, update the critic every environment step or every few environment steps.

3. Update the actor less frequently than the critic.

4. Update the target networks slowly.

A practical first setting:

$$
N_{\mathrm{warm}}=5\text{ to }20\text{ episodes}
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{critic}}=1\text{ to }4\text{ gradient steps per environment step}
$$

$$
N_{\mathrm{actor}}=1\text{ actor update every }2\text{ to }4\text{ critic updates}
$$

Target network update:

$$
\bar\psi \leftarrow \tau \psi + (1-\tau)\bar\psi
$$

with:

$$
\tau \in [0.005,0.02]
$$

For first implementation, update after each environment step using replay. If training becomes unstable, switch to episode batch updates:

$$
\text{collect one full episode}
\rightarrow
\text{compute returns}
\rightarrow
\text{update several epochs}
$$

The episode batch version is slower but easier to debug.

# 5. How does the Q value learn future cost at the beginning?

At the beginning, the network does not know future discounted cost. That is normal.

You solve this by using actual completed rollouts.

After an episode finishes, you have the full sequence:

$$
(s_0,H_0,c_0),\dots,(s_T,H_T,c_T)
$$

You can compute empirical cost to go backward:

$$
G_T = c_T
$$

$$
G_k = c_k+\gamma G_{k+1}
$$

Then train:

$$
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)\approx G_k
$$

This is Monte Carlo return fitting.

It directly propagates terminal success or failure information backward through the trajectory.

So the first critic training stage should use Monte Carlo targets:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MC}}(\psi)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left(
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)-G_k
\right)^2
\right]
$$

After the critic becomes roughly useful, switch to mixed Monte Carlo and temporal difference learning.

# 6. How to back propagate goal value through the whole trajectory

Use three mechanisms together.

## Mechanism 1: Reverse return computation

At the end of each episode, compute:

$$
G_k = \sum_{i=k}^{T} \gamma^{i-k}c_i
$$

This explicitly assigns every earlier state action pair the future cost that followed it.

That is how the goal state information reaches early trajectory states.

## Mechanism 2: $n$ step TD targets

Once the critic is partially trained, use:

$$
y_k^{(n)} =
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}
\gamma^i c_{k+i}
+
\gamma^n
\sum_{H'}
\pi_{\bar\phi}(H'\mid s_{k+n})
C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+n},H')
$$

This is better than one step TD because horizon choice may only show its consequence after several control steps.

Use $n$ comparable to a meaningful control segment:

$$
n \approx B+S
$$

or a smaller fixed number if trajectories are long.

## Mechanism 3: Dense shaping cost

Do not rely only on terminal success.

Use dense terms such as:

$$
e_E^2
$$

$$
g_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)x^TQ_xx
$$

$$
u^2
$$

$$
t_{\mathrm{solve}}/T_s
$$

The dense cost gives local learning signal before the agent has found many successful trajectories.

Recommended practical target:

$$
y_k =
(1-\alpha)y_k^{(n)}
+
\alpha G_k
$$

where $\alpha$ can start large and then decay:

$$
\alpha: 1.0 \rightarrow 0.2
$$

That means early training relies more on full episode returns, later training relies more on bootstrapped TD targets.

# 7. Should future cost be discounted or emphasized?

Use discounting, but keep $\gamma$ close to 1.

For a control problem where delayed swing up matters, heavy discounting is harmful. If $\gamma$ is too small, the actor will learn short sighted horizon choices.

Recommended:

$$
\gamma \in [0.98,0.995]
$$

for step based simulation.

If the control time step is small, choose $\gamma$ from a physical time constant:

$$
\gamma=\exp\left(-\frac{T_s}{\tau_{\mathrm{disc}}}\right)
$$

where $\tau_{\mathrm{disc}}$ should be comparable to the desired swing up and stabilization time scale.

Do not use $\gamma>1$ to emphasize future cost. That usually destabilizes value learning.

To emphasize future success, use:

1. Near one discount factor.

2. Terminal failure penalty.

3. Terminal success bonus if using reward form.

4. $n$ step or Monte Carlo targets.

5. Episode level evaluation.

For finite horizon cost minimization, using $\gamma=1$ is also acceptable if episode length is fixed or capped:

$$
G_k=\sum_{i=k}^{T}c_i
$$

The safest first choice is:

$$
\gamma=0.99
$$

Then test sensitivity.

# 8. Can $Q$ value be used as terminal cost for MPC?

Not directly in the first version.

Your current $Q$ critic is:

$$
C_\psi(s,H)
$$

It estimates the closed loop cost of choosing horizon $H$ at state $s$. That is a high level timing value, not a physical terminal state cost.

The MPC terminal cost should be a function of predicted terminal state:

$$
V(x_{k+H})
$$

or:

$$
C(s_{k+H})
$$

not a function of the currently chosen horizon unless the high level action is explicitly part of the terminal decision.

Correct first use:

$$
H_k^\star = \arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

Then run MPC with $H_k^\star$.

Do not insert $C_\psi(s_k,H)$ inside the QP objective. It is already being used to select the QP horizon.

A later possible use is:

$$
J_{\mathrm{MPC}} = J_{\mathrm{base}} + \lambda_V V_\psi(\hat s_{k+H})
$$

where:

$$
V_\psi(s) = \min_H C_\psi(s,H)
$$

This uses the critic as a terminal value after the predicted MPC rollout.

However, this creates a nonlinear learned terminal term. If your MPC is a QP, a neural value term generally breaks the QP structure unless you:

1. Evaluate it outside the QP for candidate horizons.

2. Use a local quadratic approximation.

3. Train a structured quadratic terminal value.

For this stage, use $Q$ outside MPC only.

# 9. Recommended algorithm for this project

Because the action space is discrete and small, use a discrete cost actor critic.

Critic:

$$
C_\psi(s,H)
$$

Actor:

$$
\pi_\phi(H\mid s)
$$

Target value:

$$
V_{\bar\psi,\bar\phi}(s') = \sum_{H'}
\pi_{\bar\phi}(H'\mid s')
C_{\bar\psi}(s',H')
$$

TD target:

$$
y_k = c_k+\gamma V_{\bar\psi,\bar\phi}(s_{k+1})
$$

Critic loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_C(\psi) =
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left(
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)-y_k
\right)^2
\right]
$$

Actor loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\pi(\phi) =
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\sum_H\pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)C_\psi(s_k,H)
- 
\beta \mathcal{H}(\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid s_k))
\right]
$$

For evaluation:

$$
H_k^\star = \arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

This is consistent with cost minimization and MPC.

$$

$$

$$
H\sim \mathrm{Uniform}(\mathcal{H})

$$

$$

Train critic:

$$
C_\psi(s_k,H_k)\leftarrow G_k
$$

Do not train the actor aggressively yet.

## Phase C: Warm start actor

Initialize the actor from the critic:

$$
\pi_\phi(H\mid s)
\propto
\exp\left(
-\frac{C_\psi(s,H)}{\alpha}
\right)
$$

where $\alpha$ is a temperature.

This makes low cost horizons more likely while preserving exploration.

## Phase D: Online simulated training

For each simulation episode:

1. Reset environment.

2. Build $s_k$.

3. Sample $H_k\sim\pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)$.

4. Compute $B_k,S_k$.

5. Run MPC.

6. Apply $u_k$.

7. Step environment.

8. Compute $c_k^{\mathrm{AC}}$.

9. Store transition.

10. Update critic from replay.

11. Update actor every few critic updates.

12. Soft update target networks.

At episode end, compute full returns and optionally add Monte Carlo supervision.

## Phase E: Evaluation

Freeze actor and critic.

Use deterministic action:

$$
H_k^\star = \arg\min_H C_\psi(s_k,H)
$$

Evaluate against fixed and heuristic baselines.

Do not report training reward alone. Report:

1. Success rate.

2. Time to stabilize.

3. Total actuation.

4. Total computation cost.

5. Average solve time.

6. Number of MPC solves.

7. Horizon distribution over episode phase.

# 11. Common RL training questions for this setup

## Should exploration be random or learned?

Use stochastic actor sampling during training:

$$
H_k\sim\pi_\phi(H\mid s_k)
$$

Keep an entropy bonus early. Decay it slowly.

Do not use deterministic $\arg\min$ during early training.

## Should bad MPC solves be included?

Yes, but mark failure clearly. If the QP fails, assign a failure cost and terminate or fall back to a safe default outside the learning objective. Do not silently remove failed samples, or the critic will never learn which horizons cause infeasibility.

## Should the actor update from old replay data?

Yes, but with caution. The MPC induced transition changes as the actor changes the horizon distribution. Replay is useful, but keep the buffer refreshed with recent rollouts.

A practical buffer mix:

$$
70\% \text{ recent actor data}
$$

$$
30\% \text{ baseline and random horizon data}
$$

## Should successful trajectories be oversampled?

Yes. Early successful swing up trajectories may be rare. Oversampling successful and near successful episodes helps propagate useful value gradients.

## Should the critic learn reward or cost?

Use cost.

The controller already minimizes cost, and your MPC formulation is cost based. So use:

$$
C_\psi(s,H)
$$

not reward value unless you have a specific reason.

## Should the actor output all horizons?

For now, yes. The action space is small. Use a categorical distribution over all allowed horizons.

Later, you can reduce action count by using horizon bins:

$$
H\in\{H_1,H_2,\dots,H_m\}
$$

instead of every integer horizon.

## Should the critic be updated before or after the actor?

Update the critic first.

A stable sequence per training iteration is:

1. Critic update.

2. Critic update.

3. Actor update.

4. Target network update.

The actor depends on the critic. A poor critic gives poor policy gradients.

# 13. When see the same transition, different future returns

If the same $$(s,a)$$ appears in different rollouts but produces different later trajectories, the critic should **not memorize one value**. It should learn the conditional expected future cost:

$$
C^\pi(s,a) = \mathbb{E}_\pi[G_k\mid s_k=s,\,a_k=a]
$$

where

$$
G_k = \sum_{i=k}^{T} \gamma^{i-k} c_i .
$$

For Monte Carlo offline training, each completed rollout gives a target $$G_k^{(j)}$$. Use:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MC}}(\psi)
=
\frac{1}{2}\left[C_\psi(s_k,a_k) - G_k^{(j)}\right]^2
$$

$$
\psi
\leftarrow
\psi
- \eta_C
\left[
C_\psi(s_k,a_k)-G_k^{(j)}
\right]
\nabla_\psi C_\psi(s_k,a_k)
$$

If the same $$(s,a)$$ has multiple observed returns,

$$
G_k^{(1)},\,G_k^{(2)},\,\dots,\,G_k^{(N)}
$$

then the MSE optimum is:

$$
C_\psi(s,a) \rightarrow \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N G^{(j)}
$$

so the critic learns the **average outcome**, not the best or latest outcome.

If trajectories come from different policies, then the value is policy dependent:

$$
C^\pi(s,a) \neq C^{\pi'}(s,a)
$$

so either train on data from the current policy, or use an off policy target:

$$
y_k = c_k + \gamma(1-d_k)\,
\mathbb{E}_{a' \sim \pi_\phi(\cdot\,|\,s_{k+1})}
\left[
C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+1},a')
\right]
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TD}}(\psi)
=
\frac{1}{2}\left[C_\psi(s_k,a_k) - y_k \right]^2
$$

This matches your cost critic formulation $C_\psi(s,H)$ for horizon selection.

# 14. Online bootstrapping before the episode finishes
They **can update during the episode**. They do not need to know the final return yet.

After one environment step, you already know:

$$
(s_k, a_k, c_k, s_{k+1}, d_k)
$$

Then use bootstrapping:

$$
y_k =
c_k + \gamma (1 - d_k) C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+1}, a_{k+1})
$$

with

$$
a_{k+1} \sim \pi_{\bar\phi}(\cdot \mid s_{k+1})
$$

or, for cost minimization with deterministic deployment,

$$
y_k =
c_k + \gamma (1 - d_k) \min_{a'} C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+1}, a')
$$

but for huge continuous $H$, replace the minimum by actor output:

$$
a' = \mu_{\bar\phi}(s_{k+1})
$$

$$
y_k =
c_k + \gamma (1 - d_k) C_{\bar\psi}(s_{k+1}, \mu_{\bar\phi}(s_{k+1}))
$$

Then update critic:

$$
\psi \leftarrow \psi - \eta_C \left[ C_\psi(s_k,a_k) - y_k \right] \nabla_\psi C_\psi(s_k,a_k)
$$

Update actor less frequently:

$$
\mathcal{L}_\pi(\phi) = C_\psi(s, \mu_\phi(s))
$$

$$
\phi \leftarrow \phi - \eta_\pi \nabla_\phi C_\psi(s, \mu_\phi(s))
$$

So the practical rule is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Update critic during episodes using TD bootstrapping.}
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\text{Optionally add Monte Carlo return updates after episode end.}
}
$$

A stable schedule is: critic updates every step, actor updates every 2 to 4 critic updates, and Monte Carlo or ($n$) step correction after each completed episode.
