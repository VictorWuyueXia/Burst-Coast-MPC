# Rotary-Pendulum MPC Objective Redesign

## 1. Scope

This document replaces the inherited rotary-pendulum objective and defines the
initial-guess policy for its next implementation. It supplements the plant,
RK4, burst-coast, torque-bound, arm-limit, and full-plan execution contracts in
`6.1-rotary-pendulum-MPC-formulation.md`.

The objective has five performance terms:

1. terminal pendulum swing energy;
2. phase chasing through rotary-arm acceleration;
3. actuation continuation;
4. pendulum local error;
5. rotary-arm local error.

The existing high-penalty arm-angle soft constraint remains a separate safety
term. RL is outside this formulation.

---

## Part I — Intuitive formulation

### 2. Why the inherited objective is unsuitable

The current objective integrates squared **whole-mechanism** energy error over
the complete prediction. That has two undesirable effects.

First, arm kinetic energy and arm-pendulum cross energy can make the total
energy look correct while the pendulum itself still lacks the energy needed to
reach upright. Second, swing-up must begin far below the target energy, so an
integral energy penalty charges the controller for the physically necessary
early part of every successful swing.

The replacement asks a more task-directed question:

> At the end of the burst-coast prediction, does the pendulum itself have the
> upright energy, and did the arm move in the phase that transfers energy
> efficiently?

This agrees with the classical energy-control view of pendulum swing-up and
with Quanser's description of commanding rotary-arm acceleration from the
pendulum energy target.

### 3. Pendulum energy in its moving swing plane

At arm angle $\theta$, the pendulum swings in the plane spanned by the moving
tangent direction $\mathbf e_t(\theta)$ and the vertical direction
$\mathbf e_z$. The plane changes azimuth as the rotary arm moves.

Relative to its moving pivot, the pendulum center-of-mass velocity inside that
plane is

$$
\mathbf v_{p/A}^{\rm swing}
=l\nu\left(\cos\alpha\,\mathbf e_t+\sin\alpha\,\mathbf e_z\right).
$$

Therefore, the mechanical energy belonging to the pendulum's own swing is

$$
\boxed{
E_s(\alpha,\nu)
=\frac12J_p\nu^2+G(1-\cos\alpha).
}
$$

It deliberately excludes:

- rotary-arm kinetic energy;
- translation of the pendulum pivot;
- transport caused by rotation of the swing plane;
- the $C\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu$ cross term.

Those excluded motions transfer energy into and out of $E_s$; they are not
energy already stored in the pendulum's relative swing.

This reference-frame choice matters. Projecting the pendulum's inertial
velocity into the instantaneous swing plane would instead give

$$
E_{p,\parallel}^{I}
=\frac12m_pr^2\omega^2
+C\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu
+\frac12J_p\nu^2
+G(1-\cos\alpha).
$$

That quantity contains translation of the moving pivot. It can equal the
upright target while the pendulum has little relative swing, so it is not the
terminal task energy. The moving-pivot frame is intentional: the changing
plane supplies work to $E_s$, while $E_s$ measures what that work has stored in
the passive swing degree of freedom.

The upright-rest target is

$$
E_s^\star=2G.
$$

Only the final predicted state receives the normalized energy penalty

$$
J_E=w_E
\left(
\frac{E_s(x_H)-E_s^\star}{E_s^\star}
\right)^2.
$$

### 4. Phase chasing follows the actual energy-transfer channel

Differentiating $E_s$ along the governing dynamics in Section 5 of the 6.1
document gives

$$
\boxed{
\dot E_s
=\nu\left[
-C\cos\alpha\,\ddot\theta
+J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2
-b_p\nu
\right].
}
$$

The first term is the dominant sign-controllable pumping channel:

$$
P_{\rm accel}=-C\cos\alpha\,\nu\ddot\theta.
$$

When the pendulum lacks energy, positive pumping requires

$$
\operatorname{sign}(\ddot\theta)
=-\operatorname{sign}(\cos\alpha\,\nu).
$$

When it has excess energy, the sign should reverse. The required arm
acceleration consequently changes every half swing: it chases pendulum phase
instead of prescribing a geometric arm trajectory. Near horizontal,
$\cos\alpha\approx0$, acceleration coupling vanishes and the phase command
naturally fades.

The MPC phase term tracks this desired acceleration sign and magnitude. A
smooth energy-deficit signal makes it pump below the target, become inactive
on the target energy shell, and remove energy above the target.

### 5. How the five terms cooperate

The terms have distinct jobs:

| Term | Job | Where it acts |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal swing energy | Arrive with the pendulum's upright mechanical energy | Terminal state only |
| Phase chasing | Time arm acceleration to pump or remove swing energy efficiently | Active burst only |
| Actuation continuation | Avoid unnecessary torque discontinuities | Active burst only |
| Pendulum local error | Prefer upright rest over other states on the same energy shell | Weak, gated terminal tie-breaker |
| Rotary local error | Prefer home arm angle and zero arm speed | Weaker, gated terminal tie-breaker |

The local terms are not allowed to fight swing-up while energy and phase are
poor. An energy-shell gate suppresses them until the terminal energy term is
already small, and their weights remain much lower than the energy and phase
weights.

### 6. Initial-guess policy

For every burst split, the first nonlinear solve uses an all-zero torque
initial guess. Every later solve uses that same split's previous optimal torque
vector as its initial guess.

No one-step shift is applied. This controller executes the complete selected
burst and coast before replanning, so there is no unexecuted suffix to shift.
The previous vector is a numerical initial guess for a new full-plan problem,
not a claim that old inputs remain scheduled.

A smooth phase-origin convention is required at exact downward rest. Without
it, every physical state-only energy and local term is symmetric, their first
derivatives with respect to torque vanish at the all-zero initial guess, and an
exact-gradient solver can remain at the zero-input stationary point. The
convention chooses one of the two physically equivalent first swing directions
and disappears once measurable swing energy exists.

## Part II — Normative machine-scannable specification

### 7. Inherited prediction contract

For each configured burst fraction $\lambda\in\Lambda$, retain

$$
x_{k+1}=F_d(x_k,\tau_k),
\qquad
x_0=x_{\rm measured},
$$

with

$$
\tau_k=
\begin{cases}
v_k,&0\le k<B(\lambda),\\
0,&B(\lambda)\le k<H,
\end{cases}
$$

and

$$
|v_k|\le\tau_{\max}.
$$

$F_d$ is the shared RK4 map, $H=\lceil2T_n/T_s\rceil$, and only the burst
vector

$$
\mathbf v_\lambda
=\begin{bmatrix}v_0&\cdots&v_{B(\lambda)-1}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
$$

is optimized.

### 8. Pendulum-relative swing energy

#### 8.1 Definitions

Define

$$
E_s(x)=\frac12J_p\nu^2+G(1-\cos\alpha),
\qquad
E_s^\star=2G,
$$

and the dimensionless error and energy level

$$
e_E(x)=\frac{E_s(x)-E_s^\star}{E_s^\star},
\qquad
\eta(x)=\frac{E_s(x)}{E_s^\star}=e_E(x)+1.
$$

Required identities are

$$
E_s(\theta,0,\omega,0)=0,
$$

$$
E_s(\theta,\pi,\omega,0)=E_s^\star,
$$

and

$$
\frac{\partial E_s}{\partial\theta}
=\frac{\partial E_s}{\partial\omega}=0.
$$

Although the swing-plane basis depends on $\theta$, its rotation preserves
vector norms. Therefore, the intrinsic swing-energy scalar has no explicit
$\theta$ dependence. Arm motion appears in $\dot E_s$, not as stored swing
energy.

#### 8.2 Exact energy-rate audit identity

The second governing equation is

$$
J_p\dot\nu
+C\cos\alpha\,\dot\omega
-J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2
+G\sin\alpha
+b_p\nu=0.
$$

Consequently,

$$
\dot E_s
=J_p\nu\dot\nu+G\sin\alpha\,\nu
$$

and therefore

$$
\dot E_s
=-C\cos\alpha\,\nu\dot\omega
+J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2\nu
-b_p\nu^2.
$$

This identity must be checked against finite differences of $E_s$ under the
continuous model. It is diagnostic; the NLP evaluates the state, acceleration,
and cost expressions directly.

### 9. Loss features

#### 9.1 Terminal energy

The energy contribution is

$$
\boxed{J_E=w_Ee_E(x_H)^2.}
$$

No stage sum or integral of $e_E^2$ is permitted.

#### 9.2 Phase chasing

Let

$$
\Delta(\alpha)
=J_p\left(J_\theta+J_p\sin^2\alpha\right)
-C^2\cos^2\alpha.
$$

The instantaneous arm-acceleration authority associated with the torque bound
is

$$
a_\tau(\alpha)=\frac{J_p\tau_{\max}}{\Delta(\alpha)}>0.
$$

$a_\tau$ is the magnitude of the torque-induced arm-acceleration change, not
a hard bound on total acceleration; drift dynamics can make
$|\bar a_\theta|>1$.

Let $\dot\omega(x,\tau)$ be the arm acceleration returned by the exact coupled
mass-matrix solve. Its normalized value is

$$
\bar a_\theta(x,\tau)
=\frac{\dot\omega(x,\tau)}{a_\tau(\alpha)}.
$$

Define the smooth signed energy command

$$
r_E(x)
=\tanh\left(
\frac{E_s^\star-E_s(x)}{\sigma_EE_s^\star}
\right)
=-\tanh\left(\frac{e_E(x)}{\sigma_E}\right).
$$

Thus $r_E>0$ commands energy injection, $r_E=0$ commands neither injection
nor removal, and $r_E<0$ commands energy removal.

The physical phase signal is $\cos\alpha\,\nu$. Its sign is undefined at exact
rest, so define the smooth phase-origin convention

$$
\nu_E=2\sqrt{\frac{G}{J_p}},
\qquad
\nu_o=0.01\nu_E,
\qquad
\eta_o=0.05,
\qquad
\sigma_o=+1,
$$

$$
\widetilde\nu(x)
=\nu+\sigma_o\nu_o
\exp\left[-\left(\frac{\eta(x)}{\eta_o}\right)^2\right],
$$

and

$$
\chi_\phi(x)
=\frac{\cos\alpha\,\widetilde\nu(x)}
{\sqrt{\left(\cos\alpha\,\widetilde\nu(x)\right)^2+\nu_o^2}}.
$$

The squared coupling gate is

$$
g_C(x)=\cos^2\alpha.
$$

$\sigma_o=+1$ is a reproducible coordinate convention; $-1$ produces the
mirror-image initial motion for the ideal symmetric plant. The exponential
term is effectively absent after the pendulum acquires a small fraction of the
target swing energy. It changes only the loss geometry and never changes the
measured or predicted state.

The phase error is

$$
\boxed{
e_\phi(x,\tau)
=\bar a_\theta(x,\tau)+r_E(x)\chi_\phi(x).
}
$$

When $E_s<E_s^\star$, minimizing $e_\phi^2$ requests

$$
\dot\omega\approx-a_\tau r_E\chi_\phi,
$$

which makes the dominant transfer power
$-C\cos\alpha\,\nu\dot\omega$ positive. When $E_s>E_s^\star$, $r_E$ changes
sign and the same expression requests negative transfer power.

The centrifugal transfer term
$J_p\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,\omega^2\nu$ remains in the exact prediction and
terminal energy. It receives no separate phase target because the sign of
$\omega^2$ is not an independent instantaneous control choice.

The phase contribution is

$$
\boxed{
J_\phi
=\frac{w_\phi}{B(\lambda)}
\sum_{k=0}^{B(\lambda)-1}
r_E(x_k)^2g_C(x_k)e_\phi(x_k,\tau_k)^2.
}
$$

The $r_E^2$ gate switches off phase chasing on the target energy shell. The
$g_C$ gate switches it off where arm acceleration has zero instantaneous
coupling into the pendulum swing. The $1/B(\lambda)$ factor compares the mean
active phase-tracking error across candidates with different burst lengths.
Phase chasing is intentionally absent during coast: zero torque leaves arm
acceleration as passive drift rather than an independently selectable pumping
action. The terminal terms still evaluate the state after the complete coast.

#### 9.3 Actuation continuation

Let $\tau_{-1}$ be the measured torque applied immediately before the new
plan. Preserve the current normalized torque-slew expression:

$$
\boxed{
J_{\Delta\tau}
=w_{\Delta\tau}
\sum_{k=0}^{B(\lambda)-1}
\left(
\frac{v_k-\tau_{k-1}}{\tau_{\max}}
\right)^2,
}
$$

where $\tau_{k-1}=v_{k-1}$ for $k>0$. There is no torque-magnitude penalty and
no saturation-reward term.

#### 9.4 Weak local capture terms

Define wrapped pendulum error

$$
\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\alpha-\pi),\cos(\alpha-\pi)),
$$

the arm soft-limit scale

$$
\theta_{\rm lim}=\frac\pi2,
$$

and local normalized error vectors

$$
e_p(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\beta/\pi\\
\nu/\nu_E
\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
e_r(x)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\theta/\theta_{\rm lim}\\
\omega/(\theta_{\rm lim}\omega_n)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$\nu_E$ is exactly the pendulum speed whose relative kinetic energy at
downward equals $E_s^\star$. The rotary velocity scale is the peak velocity of
a $\theta_{\rm lim}$ harmonic arm oscillation at $\omega_n$.

Define the terminal energy-shell gate

$$
g_L(x_H)
=\exp\left[-\frac{e_E(x_H)^2}{\sigma_L^2}\right].
$$

The local contribution is

$$
\boxed{
J_L
=g_L(x_H)
\left[
w_p\lVert e_p(x_H)\rVert_2^2
+w_r\lVert e_r(x_H)\rVert_2^2
\right].
}
$$

The gate makes these terms negligible away from the target energy shell. The
weight hierarchy must satisfy

$$
0<w_r<w_p\ll w_\phi\le w_E.
$$

The pendulum local term is stronger because upright capture is the primary
task. The rotary term only resolves otherwise similar terminal solutions in
favor of arm home and rest.

#### 9.5 Arm-angle safety penalty

Retain the high-penalty soft constraint

$$
c_{\rm lim}(x)
=w_{\rm lim}
\left[
\left(\frac{[\theta-\theta_{\rm lim}]_+}{\theta_{\rm lim}}\right)^2
+\left(\frac{[-\theta-\theta_{\rm lim}]_+}{\theta_{\rm lim}}\right)^2
\right].
$$

It is evaluated for every predicted state, including $x_H$. This term is a
safety constraint penalty, not one of the five performance features.

### 10. Complete candidate objective

For each $\lambda\in\Lambda$, solve

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
J_\lambda^\star(x_0,\tau_{-1})
=\min_{\mathbf v_\lambda}\Bigg[\;&
w_Ee_E(x_H)^2\\
&+\frac{w_\phi}{B(\lambda)}
\sum_{k=0}^{B(\lambda)-1}r_E(x_k)^2g_C(x_k)e_\phi(x_k,\tau_k)^2\\
&+w_{\Delta\tau}
\sum_{k=0}^{B(\lambda)-1}
\left(\frac{v_k-\tau_{k-1}}{\tau_{\max}}\right)^2\\
&+g_L(x_H)
\left(w_p\lVert e_p(x_H)\rVert_2^2
+w_r\lVert e_r(x_H)\rVert_2^2\right)\\
&+\sum_{k=0}^{H}c_{\rm lim}(x_k)
\Bigg].
\end{aligned}
}
$$

The measured-state, RK4, burst-only decision, exact coast, and torque-bound
constraints are those in Section 7. Candidate selection remains

$$
\lambda^\star
=\underset{\lambda\in\Lambda}{\operatorname{argmin}}\;J_\lambda^\star.
$$

Every candidate uses the same horizon, terminal definition, phase-origin
convention, and state scales, so the optimized values remain comparable.

### 11. Initial guesses and update transaction

Index replans by $j=0,1,2,\ldots$. Each fixed-dimension candidate owns one
primal initial-guess vector.

For the first solve,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf v_{\lambda,\rm guess}^{(0)}
=\mathbf 0_{B(\lambda)}.
}
$$

For later solves,

$$
\boxed{
\mathbf v_{\lambda,\rm guess}^{(j)}
=\mathbf v_\lambda^{\star(j-1)},
\qquad j\ge1.
}
$$

Required update order:

1. solve every $\lambda$ using its current candidate-local initial guess;
2. require every solver status and objective to be valid;
3. commit every successful $\mathbf v_\lambda^{\star(j)}$ as one transaction;
4. select and execute the minimum-objective plan.

The implementation must not:

- shift the previous vector by one step;
- interpolate one split's vector into another split's dimension;
- use only the selected split to initialize all candidates;
- retain a partially updated collection if another candidate fails;
- introduce a dual-variable initial guess under this contract.

### 12. Why the zero initial guess has a descent direction

At exact downward rest,

$$
x_d=[\theta,0,0,0]^T,
\qquad
E_s(x_d)=0,
\qquad
\eta(x_d)=0.
$$

The phase-origin convention gives

$$
\widetilde\nu(x_d)=\sigma_o\nu_o,
\qquad
\chi_\phi(x_d)=\frac{\sigma_o}{\sqrt2}.
$$

The coupled acceleration solve gives

$$
\dot\omega(x_d,\tau)=\frac{J_p}{\Delta_0}\tau,
\qquad
a_\tau(0)=\frac{J_p}{\Delta_0}\tau_{\max},
$$

so

$$
e_\phi(x_d,\tau)
=\frac{\tau}{\tau_{\max}}
+r_E(x_d)\frac{\sigma_o}{\sqrt2}.
$$

At $\tau=0$,

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}
\left[r_E(x_d)^2g_C(x_d)e_\phi(x_d,\tau)^2\right]_{\tau=0}
=\frac{\sqrt2\,r_E(x_d)^3\sigma_o}{\tau_{\max}}
\ne0.
$$

Therefore, the all-zero first initial guess is not stationary. Without the
phase-origin convention, this derivative, the terminal-energy derivative, the
local-error derivative, and the continuation derivative would all be zero at
the symmetric downward equilibrium.

### 13. MPC configuration contract

The following are experiment-facing loss parameters in the single rotary
`mpc.yaml` domain:

| YAML key | Symbol | Retained value |
|---|---:|---:|
| `terminal-swing-energy-weight` | $w_E$ | $1.0$ |
| `phase-chasing-weight` | $w_\phi$ | $1.0$ |
| `energy-transition-width` | $\sigma_E$ | $0.25$ |
| `local-energy-shell-width` | $\sigma_L$ | $0.25$ |
| `pendulum-local-weight` | $w_p$ | $0.01$ |
| `rotary-local-weight` | $w_r$ | $0.001$ |
| `torque-slew-weight` | $w_{\Delta\tau}$ | $0.001$ |
| `arm-angle-soft-penalty-weight` | $w_{\rm lim}$ | $10000$ |

These are the retained dimensionless tuning values. The hierarchy and
normalization remain part of the formulation; later experiments may retune
them only with complete closed-loop artifact comparisons.

The following are derived or fixed and must not be independently configured:

$$
E_s^\star,\ \eta,\ \nu_E,\ \nu_o,\ \eta_o,\ \sigma_o,\
\theta_{\rm lim},\ a_\tau(\alpha),\ \omega_n,\ T_n.
$$

### 14. Required implementation checks

The implementation is conforming only when all checks pass:

1. Numeric and CasADi $E_s$, $e_E$, $r_E$, $\chi_\phi$, $g_C$, $e_\phi$,
   $e_p$, $e_r$, and full objectives agree on batched sampled states.
2. $E_s$ is invariant to $\theta$ and $\omega$ for fixed $(\alpha,\nu)$.
3. $E_s(0,0)=0$ and $E_s(\pi,0)=2G$.
4. Finite-difference $\dot E_s$ matches the identity in Section 8.2.
5. Desired acceleration produces positive dominant transfer power below the
   energy target and negative transfer power above it.
6. The phase contribution is zero at $\cos\alpha=0$ and the phase-origin
   convention fades as $\eta/\eta_o$ grows.
7. The all-zero first initial guess has a nonzero objective gradient at exact
   downward rest.
8. Every candidate uses zeros at replan zero.
9. Every candidate uses its own previous primal solution at later replans.
10. Candidate initial guesses update only after all candidate solves succeed.
11. No squared energy-error stage sum remains.
12. The local terms are terminal, energy-gated, and lower weighted than the
    energy and phase terms.
13. Torque continuation remains burst-only and begins from the measured prior
    applied torque.
14. Arm-limit cost covers $x_0$ through $x_H$.
15. Phase chasing covers only the active burst; arm-limit cost covers the
    complete zero-torque coast.
16. The selected action sequence is fully executed before the next replan.

### 15. Rejected alternatives

The following formulations are intentionally excluded:

- **Whole-system terminal energy:** arm motion can satisfy it without storing
  the required energy in pendulum swing.
- **Inertial energy projected into the swing plane:** the
  $m_pr^2\omega^2/2$ and $C\cos\alpha\,\omega\nu$ terms allow moving-pivot
  energy to masquerade as passive swing energy.
- **Integrated squared energy error:** it penalizes the necessary low-energy
  beginning of swing-up and scales with horizon sample count.
- **Raw angle phase error:** phase wraps, is undefined at zero oscillation
  amplitude, and does not identify the energy-transfer direction.
- **Ungated local state penalties:** they request arm and pendulum rest before
  swing-up has acquired enough energy.
- **Torque sign as the phase variable:** torque affects swing energy through
  the coupled arm acceleration, so acceleration is the physically correct
  intermediate variable.
- **Shifted receding-horizon initial guesses:** the entire old plan has already
  been executed before replanning.

### 16. Implementation boundary

This document is the objective and initial-guess design contract. The next code
change must replace the current whole-system stage-energy helpers, update the
MPC configuration schema, add candidate-local initial-guess state, and extend
the objective tests. It must not add RL, a balancing-controller switch, a new
horizon parameter, or a second configuration file for the MPC domain.
